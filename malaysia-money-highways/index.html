Malaysia sedang membina lebuh raya wang baharu. Siapa yang akan mengutip tol?

Dalam tempoh enam minggu, Malaysia melancarkan platform bon bertoken, peraturan kewangan terbuka, bursa pembiayaan e-Invois dan ETF Bitcoin spot pertamanya. Bagi PMKS, soalan yang penting ialah siapa memiliki lorong, siapa memegang data, dan siapa yang dibayar tepat pada masanya.

Azman Taufik · 28 November 2026 · 16 minit bacaan Komen di LinkedIn
Ringkasan pantas
  • Dalam enam minggu, Malaysia membuka beberapa lebuh raya kewangan baharu: SSDS+ untuk bon dan sukuk bertoken (20 Okt), peraturan kewangan terbuka (17 Nov), bursa pembiayaan e-Invois NEFX (MoU 11 Nov) dan ETF Bitcoin Patuh Syariah Halogen (disenaraikan 2 Dis).
  • Kebanyakan lorong baharu ini dimiliki atau dikendalikan pemain sedia ada: token keluaran bank, rantaian blok milik CIMB sendiri, dan PayNet yang dimiliki BNM (35.5%) bersama 11 bank.
  • Pilot-pilot ini menghala kepada institusi. Contoh pilot awam: sukuk bertoken RM1.38 bilion, berbanding pilot pembiayaan rantaian bekalan PMKS bernilai RM1 juta.
  • Tol paling mahal bagi PMKS ialah menunggu dibayar. Kerajaan membayar dalam 14 hari; syarikat besar mengambil kira-kira 58 hari. Bagi PMKS dengan jualan RM5 juta, ilustrasi saya menunjukkan jurang ini menelan kira-kira 14% untung setahun.
  • Penyelesaian yang murah pula paling perlahan: undang-undang cagaran harta alih yang dicadangkan sejak 2017 belum dibentangkan, 1.1 juta PMKS dikecualikan daripada e-Invois, dan data akaun PMKS hanya masuk ke kewangan terbuka pada 2030.

Siapakah pemberi pinjaman kepada syarikat besar Malaysia yang tidak mengenakan faedah, tidak meminta cagaran dan tidak pernah digelar pemberi pinjaman? Jawapannya: pembekal kecil mereka sendiri. Setiap barang yang dihantar hari ini tetapi dibayar dua bulan kemudian ialah satu pinjaman.

Soalan itu terus bermain di fikiran saya apabila pengumuman demi pengumuman muncul. Pada 20 Oktober, Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) melancarkan SSDS+ untuk bon dan sukuk bertoken. Pada 11 November, MDEC memeterai MoU dengan tujuh pembiaya bagi bursa pembiayaan e-Invois nasional. Pada 17 November, BNM mengeluarkan peraturan kewangan terbuka. Pada 2 Disember, ETF Bitcoin Patuh Syariah Halogen akan disenaraikan di Bursa Malaysia.

Jika dibaca bersama, inilah Malaysia membina lebuh raya wang baharu. Sesiapa yang memandu ke tempat kerja di negara ini tahu soalan seterusnya. Siapa yang membina jalan tidak sepenting siapa yang mengutip tol, untuk berapa lama, dan siapa yang tersangkut di plaza tol.

Bagi bank pusat, bermula dengan sistem penyelesaian memang wajar. Di situlah kegagalan paling mahal harganya, dan BNM membina SSDS+ sendiri menggunakan teknologi awan dan lejar teragih. Yang membimbangkan ialah apa yang menyusul selepas itu. Lapisan yang paling diperlukan perniagaan kecil biasa ialah lapisan undang-undang: undang-undang yang membolehkannya mencagarkan akaun belum terima, stok atau jentera, serta daftar awam yang menunjukkan sama ada pihak lain sudah memberi pinjaman berdasarkan aset yang sama. Risiko kedua sudah biasa kita lihat. Pelancaran bergerak lebih laju daripada peraturan dan data hasil di belakangnya. BNM sendiri menyatakan bahawa penyertaan dalam Hab Inovasi Aset Digital (DAIH) tidak menjamin pengiktirafan kawal selia, dan tiada tarikh "graduasi" yang ditetapkan.

02Ke mana pilot ini dihalakan

Wang bertoken pertama Malaysia sedang diuji pada institusi, bukan pada perniagaan kecil.

CIMB Islamic mentokenkan RM1.38 bilion daripada terbitan sukuk bernilai RM1.68 bilion, yang dilanggan 12 pelabur institusi. Sukuk itu dan deposit bertoken yang digunakan untuk membayarnya diselesaikan di atas rantaian blok milik CIMB sendiri. Khazanah pula menjalankan pilot sukuk bertoken RM100 juta bertempoh setahun. Di pihak PMKS, Maybank mengumumkan pilot pembiayaan rantaian bekalan bertoken bernilai RM1 juta pada Ogos, dibangunkan bersama sebuah fintech dalam kotak pasir kawal selia SC.

Bermula dengan institusi memang munasabah. Urus niaga besar menguji kemuktamadan penyelesaian, dan pelabur profesional mampu menanggung masalah awal. Namun pengguna pertama lazimnya mencorakkan peraturan, dan pengguna pertama di sini ialah jabatan perbendaharaan dan pengurus dana.

Lihat juga siapa yang mengendalikan lorong-lorong ini. Deposit bertoken ialah tuntutan terhadap bank komersial. Stablecoin ringgit yang diuji pula diterajui sebuah bank, Standard Chartered Malaysia, bersama Capital A. Urus niaga CIMB berjalan di atas rantaian CIMB. Lorong wang baharu sedang dibina oleh bank yang sama yang mengendalikan lorong lama. Mungkin inilah cara paling selamat. Namun satu soalan teknikal akan menentukan sama ada persaingan benar-benar tiba: bolehkah token daripada Bank A berpindah ke Bank B, atau kepada pemberi pinjaman bukan bank yang berlesen, dan berapakah yurannya?

Awas: pilot bukan pengeluaran

Dagangan sekunder sukuk bertoken di atas rantaian belum beroperasi, menurut komen industri yang dilaporkan The Edge pada Oktober. Menghubungkan bon bertoken baharu dengan bon dan sukuk sedia ada bernilai lebih RM2 trilion juga masih belum selesai.

03ETF Bitcoin hanyalah cermin pameran

ETF Bitcoin ialah lorong yang paling menonjol, tetapi paling kecil kepentingannya kepada ekonomi sebenar.

Fakta terlebih dahulu. Dana ini menawarkan sehingga satu bilion unit pada harga US$1 seunit. Itu had maksimum, bukan wang yang sudah dikumpul. Sekurang-kurangnya 95% asetnya dilaburkan dalam Bitcoin spot, yang disimpan melalui penjaga kecil di Amerika Syarikat, BitGo Bank & Trust. Yuran tahunan dihadkan pada 0.60% untuk pengurusan, 0.05% untuk pemegang amanah, 0.04% untuk lesen indeks dan 0.15% untuk penjagaan. Halogen sendiri menyatakan dana ini hanya sesuai untuk pelabur yang sanggup menanggung risiko yang amat tinggi.

Ikut aliran wang (pengiraan saya)

Bagi setiap RM100 juta yang dipegang dana, yuran maksimum berjumlah kira-kira RM840,000 setahun. Kira-kira RM650,000 kepada pengurus dan pemegang amanah tempatan. Kira-kira RM190,000 kepada penjaga dan penyedia indeks di luar negara. Bitcoin itu sendiri disimpan oleh penjaga di Amerika Syarikat.

Ada hujah yang adil untuk menyokongnya. Rakyat Malaysia berdagang RM17.14 bilion di bursa aset digital berdaftar pada 2025. ETF ini menarik sebahagian permintaan itu ke akaun CDS, broker berlesen dan tadbir urus Syariah yang formal. Itu satu kemajuan dari segi perlindungan pelabur.

Namun ia bukan pembentukan modal. Ringgit yang masuk ke dana ini membeli aset yang dinilai dalam dolar AS dan disimpan di luar negara. Ia tidak membiayai mesin, ladang atau gaji pekerja di Malaysia. Patuh Syariah bermaksud harus untuk dimiliki. Sama ada ia sesuai atau selamat ialah soalan lain.

Inilah ujian yang akan saya perhatikan. Jika ETF ini mengumpul lebih banyak dana pada tahun pertamanya berbanding semua pilot pembiayaan PMKS bertoken digabungkan, landasan baharu ini sedang membawa simpanan rakyat keluar lebih laju daripada membawa kredit kepada syarikat Malaysia.

04Tol paling mahal tiada papan tanda

Bagi PMKS, menunggu untuk dibayar lebih mahal daripada kadar faedah itu sendiri.

Kerajaan mengikat dirinya dengan peraturan 14 hari. Di bawah Arahan Perbendaharaan AP 103(a), bil mesti dijelaskan dalam tempoh 14 hari selepas tuntutan lengkap diterima. Pada Oktober 2026, Kementerian Kewangan menjelaskan kesemua 15,110 bilnya dalam tempoh 14 hari, dan 97% daripadanya dalam tujuh hari.

Pembeli swasta tiada peraturan sedemikian. Laporan State of Credit 2025 oleh Experian mendapati hutang dagangan syarikat Malaysia mengambil masa purata kira-kira 61 hari untuk menjadi tunai. Bagi syarikat besar, kira-kira 58 hari. Sektor pembinaan mencatat 76 hari pada akhir 2024, manakala pengangkutan dan logistik mencecah 76 hari pada awal 2025. Allianz Trade pula menyatakan undang-undang Malaysia tiada rangka kerja khusus bagi bayaran lewat. Faedah lewat bayar hanya terpakai jika dinyatakan dalam kontrak.

Letakkan angka ini di sebelah cerita tokenisasi. Lejar yang lebih pantas tidak boleh memaksa pembeli membayar lebih awal jika kontrak menyebut 90 hari. Ia boleh menjadikan pinjaman berasaskan invois lebih murah, dan itu membantu. Tetapi PMKS masih membayar faedah atas wang yang dipegang pelanggannya.

Negara lain menganggap ini masalah undang-undang. Kerajaan UK mengumumkan pada Mei 2026 rancangan mewajibkan syarikat besar membayar pembekal kecil dalam tempoh 60 hari, yang mungkin dipendekkan kepada 45 hari, dengan faedah 8% di atas kadar asas. Penguatkuasaan proaktif Jepun menurunkan kadar bayaran lewat daripada 25% kepada 12% dalam tempoh 18 tahun. Korea menetapkan had undang-undang 60 hari bagi bayaran subkontrak. Malaysia pun pernah melakukannya: Akta Pembayaran dan Adjudikasi Industri Pembinaan 2012 (CIPAA) memberi kontraktor jalan pantas untuk menyelesaikan pertikaian bayaran.

Pengajaran

Peraturan yang bergantung kepada PMKS menyaman pelanggan sendiri akan gagal, kerana pembekal tidak akan menyaman pembeli yang menjadi sumber rezekinya. Kajian Enterprise Research Centre (Julai 2026) mendapati sistem yang berkesan mempunyai pengawal selia yang memantau, menerbitkan data dan menguatkuasakan.

05Data ialah cagaran baharu. Siapa yang memegang kuncinya?

Aset paling bernilai yang sedang dibina ialah rekod yang disahkan tentang siapa menjual apa kepada siapa, dan sama ada ia telah dibayar.

Malaysia sudah memiliki sebahagian besarnya. Sehingga 1 Oktober, lebih 268,000 pembayar cukai telah menghantar 1.99 bilion e-Invois ke MyInvois. Bursa Pembiayaan e-Invois Nasional (NEFX) oleh MDEC akan membaca e-Invois sah yang ditukar melalui rangkaian Peppol, serta data yang dipersetujui untuk dikongsi oleh PMKS. Pilotnya merangkumi penilaian data, semakan pembiayaan berganda, pengesanan pinjaman dan pengurusan persetujuan. Tujuh pembiaya telah menyertai: CIMB, CIMB Islamic, Affin, Alliance, Funding Societies, CapBay dan Bettr. Keputusan kredit kekal di tangan mereka.

Peraturan kewangan terbuka BNM pula menambah data bank. Bank dengan lebih sejuta pelanggan wajib berkongsi data akaun pelanggan individu menjelang 1 Januari 2028. Data akaun PMKS hanya masuk menjelang 1 Januari 2030, dan itu pun hanya daripada penyedia yang mempunyai lebih 30,000 pelanggan PMKS. Platformnya, Open Finance Malaysia, dikendalikan PayNet.

Di sinilah jurangnya. Pada 1 September, kerajaan menaikkan had pengecualian e-Invois daripada jualan tahunan RM1 juta kepada RM3 juta. LHDN menyatakan lebih 1.1 juta perniagaan mendapat manfaat. Tujuannya, meringankan beban pematuhan perniagaan kecil, memang munasabah.

Namun letakkan kedua-dua keputusan ini bersebelahan. Firma yang paling memerlukan pembiayaan berasaskan data ialah firma kecil, baharu dan tidak memiliki hartanah. Kini, merekalah yang paling kurang menghasilkan data yang digunakan bursa baharu itu. Firma melebihi RM3 juta akan menikmati kredit yang lebih murah dan pantas terlebih dahulu. Firma mikro kekal di takuk lama, dengan soalan yang sama setiap kali memohon: ada tanah?

Kemudian, soal tol itu sendiri. Siapa yang membayar kos pengesahan, panggilan API dan pengurusan persetujuan? Pengumuman MoU NEFX tidak menyebut soal yuran, dan saya belum menemui jadual yuran Open Finance Malaysia. Ketika PayNet ditubuhkan pada 2017, BNM menjanjikan akses terbuka dan adil kepada bank dan entiti bukan bank yang layak. Kata-kata itu betul. Ujiannya ialah senarai harga. Rancangan BNM untuk mengurangkan pegangannya dalam PayNet, seperti dimaklumkan MOF pada Disember 2025, menjadikan soalan siapa memiliki suis data negara lebih mendesak.

06Penyelesaian paling murah ialah undang-undang, dan ia lewat

Kebanyakan PMKS yang memohon diluluskan. Masalahnya ialah berapa banyak yang diluluskan, dan apa yang diminta sebagai cagaran.

"Ini bukan kisah firma ditolak oleh bank."
Zafer Mustafaoglu, Pengarah Bahagian Bank Dunia bagi Filipina, Malaysia dan Brunei, semasa pelancaran Malaysia Economic Monitor, 11 November 2026 (terjemahan daripada laporan berbahasa Inggeris)

Malaysia Economic Monitor Bank Dunia (Oktober 2026) mendapati 37.9% firma Malaysia mempunyai pinjaman atau kemudahan kredit bank, setanding beberapa negara berpendapatan tinggi. Kadar kelulusan keseluruhan naik daripada kira-kira 68% pada 2015 kepada 86% pada 2024. Namun PMKS membiayai kira-kira dua pertiga pelaburan aset tetap mereka dengan wang sendiri. Dari segi nilai, firma kecil hanya menerima 48% daripada jumlah pembiayaan yang dipohon pada 2025, berbanding 74% bagi firma besar.

Cadangan pertama Bank Dunia ialah mengoperasikan rejim kepentingan keselamatan harta alih, dengan satu daftar berasaskan notis, supaya PMKS boleh meminjam berasaskan peralatan, akaun belum terima dan stok, bukan terutamanya tanah dan bangunan.

Malaysia sudah lama mendraf undang-undang ini. Undang-undang sekuriti harta peribadi pertama kali dicadangkan pada 2017, dengan sesi konsultasi pada 2021 dan 2023. RUU MPSI sepatutnya diluluskan menjelang suku ketiga 2026. Menurut The Star pada 14 November, ia tidak kelihatan telah dibentangkan di Parlimen, dan SSM tidak memberi sebarang kemas kini sejak 2023.

Daftar berasaskan notis hanyalah pangkalan data yang disokong undang-undang. Ia menjawab soalan setiap pembiaya invois: sudahkah pihak lain memberi pinjaman berdasarkan aset ini? NEFX cuba menyelesaikan masalah pembiayaan berganda untuk e-Invois melalui teknologi. Daftar ini menyelesaikannya untuk setiap aset alih, melalui undang-undang, termasuk bagi peniaga tunggal dan perniagaan tidak diperbadankan yang hari ini langsung tiada rangka kerja.

Secara terus terang: Malaysia melancarkan platform bon berasaskan lejar teragih sebelum meluluskan undang-undang yang membolehkan sebuah kedai runcit mencagarkan stoknya.

07Siapa untung, siapa menanggung

Setiap jalan baharu ada pemenangnya, dan pemenang itu tidak semestinya pemandu.

Untung
Bank besar

Menerbitkan token, mengendalikan rantaian dan turut memiliki suis data. Risikonya, pendapatan FX, perbankan koresponden dan apungan menipis apabila penyelesaian menjadi lebih pantas.

Untung kekal
Pembeli besar dan GLC

Menikmati kira-kira dua bulan kredit pembekal tanpa faedah. Paling rugi jika peraturan tempoh bayaran diperkenalkan.

Untung
Pengurus dana, broker, penjaga aset

Menikmati kumpulan yuran daripada ETF dan produk bertoken, dengan sebahagian pendapatan penjagaan mengalir ke luar negara.

Bercampur
Fintech dan pemberi pinjaman bukan bank

Menang jika akses dan yuran terbuka. Kalah jika suis data dan rangkaian token dikawal ketat.

Untung dahulu
PMKS melebihi RM3 juta

Data e-Invois, NEFX dan kredit yang lebih pantas sampai kepada mereka terlebih dahulu.

Berisiko
Firma mikro bawah RM3 juta

Di luar data e-Invois, dan masih diminta tanah atau bangunan sehingga RUU MPSI menjadi undang-undang.

Bercampur
Isi rumah dan ahli KWSP

Akses lebih mudah kepada produk baharu, tetapi pasaran modal yang lebih besar tidak dengan sendirinya menambah simpanan persaraan.

Pelan Induk Pasaran Modal menyasarkan pasaran modal berkembang daripada RM4.3 trilion pada 2025 kepada RM5.8–6.3 trilion menjelang 2030, dan RM13.8–20 trilion menjelang 2045. Itu unjuran saiz pasaran, bukan kekayaan isi rumah. Sehingga 31 Mei 2026, hanya 38.3% ahli aktif KWSP sektor formal berumur 18 hingga 60 tahun telah mencapai sasaran simpanan asas mengikut umur. Pasaran yang lebih besar tidak semestinya menaikkan bahagian mereka.

08Di mana saya mungkin silap

Hujah menentang pandangan saya
  1. Bank membina landasan ialah satu kelebihan. BNM mengikat token kepada bank berlesen untuk mengelakkan deposit lari ke stablecoin swasta. Mendahulukan kestabilan ialah susunan yang boleh dipertahankan.
  2. Undang-undang tempoh bayaran boleh memakan diri. Cadangan peraturan bayaran lewat EU pada 2023 gagal diteruskan. Pembeli mungkin beralih kepada pembekal lebih besar atau menuntut diskaun untuk bayaran awal, dan PMKS jarang menguatkuasakan hak terhadap pelanggan utama.
  3. Pengecualian e-Invois menjimatkan kos sebenar bagi 1.1 juta firma. Penyata bank dan data lain yang dipersetujui boleh menampung sebahagian jurang, dan portal MyInvois percuma bagi pengguna sukarela.
  4. ETF Bitcoin mungkin membawa aktiviti pulang ke dalam negara secara berdaftar dan bercukai, dan kaunter USD boleh menarik dana asing.
  5. Daftar cagaran bukan ubat ajaib. Bank mungkin terus memilih hartanah, dan daftar memerlukan bertahun-tahun untuk dipercayai.

Walaupun kelima-lima hujah ini diterima, susunannya tetap kelihatan salah bagi perniagaan kecil: bayar tepat pada masanya, daftarkan cagaran, buka data, kemudian barulah tokenkan.

1
Bayar tepat pada masanya, bermula dengan keluarga kerajaan sendiri

Ukuran: purata tempoh bayaran 100 pembeli terbesar, diterbitkan setiap enam bulan.

Bentangkan RUU MPSI pada 2027 dan bina daftarnya

Akses terbuka yang tertulis dalam senarai yuran

4

Ukuran: kad skor awam tahunan.
Apa yang boleh dibuat perniagaan dalam 90 hari
  • Ukur berapa hari sebenar lima pelanggan terbesar anda mengambil masa untuk membayar.
  • Tulis tempoh bayaran dan klausa faedah lewat bayar dalam kontrak. Di Malaysia, faedah lewat hanya boleh dituntut jika dinyatakan dalam kontrak.

  • Tanya bank atau pembiaya anda sama ada mereka menyertai NEFX atau akan menggunakan kewangan terbuka, dan berapa yurannya.
  • Sebelum mengambil pembiayaan invois, bandingkan kos keseluruhan (yuran, kadar dan liabiliti rekursa), bukan kadar iklan sahaja.
  • Jauhkan modal kerja perniagaan daripada produk spekulatif, patuh Syariah atau tidak.

Pandangan saya

Malaysia betul membina landasan baharu ini, dan BNM membina lapisan penyelesaian dengan baik. Kebimbangan saya ialah susunannya.

Bagi perniagaan kecil, urutan yang mencipta kekayaan cukup mudah: dibayar tepat pada masanya, boleh mencagarkan apa yang dimiliki, biarkan data bercakap bagi pihaknya, dan hanya selepas itu jadikannya boleh diprogram. Kita sedang melakukannya secara terbalik.

Economics & Public Policy · Digital Finance · SMEs

Malaysia is building new money highways. Who will collect the tolls?

In six weeks, Malaysia switched on a tokenised bond platform, open finance rules, an e-invoice financing exchange and its first spot Bitcoin ETF. For small businesses, the questions that matter are who owns the lanes, who holds the data, and who gets paid on time.

Azman Taufik · 28 November 2026 · 17 min read Comment on LinkedIn
The short version
  • In six weeks Malaysia opened several new financial highways: SSDS+ for tokenised bonds and sukuk (20 Oct), open finance rules (17 Nov), the NEFX e-invoice financing exchange (MoUs 11 Nov) and the Halogen Shariah Bitcoin ETF (lists 2 Dec).
  • Most of the new lanes are owned or run by incumbents: bank-issued tokens, CIMB's own blockchain, and PayNet, owned by BNM (35.5%) and 11 banks.
  • The pilots point at institutions. Among public pilots: RM1.38 billion of tokenised sukuk against a RM1 million SME supply chain financing pilot.
  • The costliest toll for small firms is waiting to be paid. Government pays within 14 days; large companies take about 58 days. For an SME with RM5m in sales, my illustration puts that gap at about 14% of a year's profit.
  • The cheap fixes are the slow ones: the movable collateral law, proposed in 2017, has not been tabled; 1.1 million MSMEs are exempt from e-invoicing; SME account data joins open finance only in 2030.

Which lender to Malaysia's big companies charges no interest, asks for no collateral and is never called a lender? Their small suppliers. Every delivery made today and paid for two months later is a loan.

I kept coming back to that question as the announcements arrived. On 20 October, Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) launched SSDS+ for tokenised bonds and sukuk. On 11 November, MDEC signed seven financiers to a national e-invoice financing exchange. On 17 November, BNM issued its open finance rules. On 2 December, the Halogen Shariah Bitcoin ETF lists on Bursa Malaysia.

Read together, this is Malaysia building new money highways. Anyone who drives to work here knows what to ask next. Who built the road matters less than who collects the toll, for how long, and who ends up stuck at the booth.

01Five layers, five owners, five speeds

Malaysia has built the most expensive layer first and left the cheapest one for last.

Starting with settlement is right for a central bank. It is where a failure costs the most, and BNM built SSDS+ in-house on cloud and distributed ledger technology. The concern is what comes after. The layer an ordinary small firm needs most is the legal one: a law that lets it pledge receivables, stock or machinery, and a public registry showing who has already lent against them. That needs Parliament, not a blockchain.

The other risk is a familiar one. Launches run ahead of the rules and the outcome data behind them. BNM itself says joining its Digital Asset Innovation Hub (DAIH) does not guarantee regulatory recognition, and there is no set "graduation" milestone.

02Where the pilots are pointed

Malaysia's first tokenised money is being tested on institutions, not on small firms.

CIMB Islamic tokenised RM1.38 billion of a RM1.68 billion sukuk issue, taken up by 12 institutional investors. Both the sukuk and the tokenised deposits that paid for it settled on CIMB's own blockchain. Khazanah ran a RM100 million, one-year tokenised sukuk pilot. On the SME side, Maybank announced a RM1 million tokenised supply chain financing pilot in August, built with a fintech in the SC's regulatory sandbox.

Starting with institutions is sensible. Large deals test settlement finality, and professional investors can absorb teething problems. But first users tend to shape the rules, and the first users here are treasuries and fund managers.

Look also at who runs the lanes. A tokenised deposit is a claim on a commercial bank. The ringgit stablecoin being tested is bank-led, by Standard Chartered Malaysia with Capital A. The CIMB deal ran on CIMB's chain. The new money lanes are being built by the banks that run the old ones. That may well be the safest way to do it. It also means one technical question decides whether competition arrives: can a token from Bank A move to Bank B, or to a licensed non-bank lender, and at what fee?

Watch: a pilot is not production

Secondary on-chain trading of the tokenised sukuk is not yet live, according to industry comments reported by The Edge in October. Linking new tokenised bonds to more than RM2 trillion of existing bonds and sukuk is still unsolved.

03The Bitcoin ETF is the shop window

The Bitcoin ETF is the most visible lane and the least important one for the real economy.

The facts first. The fund offers up to one billion units at US$1 each. That is a ceiling, not money raised. At least 95% of its assets go into spot bitcoin, held through a US sub-custodian, BitGo Bank & Trust. Annual fees are capped at 0.60% for management, 0.05% for the trustee, 0.04% for the index licence and 0.15% for sub-custody. Halogen itself says the fund suits investors with an "extremely high-risk tolerance".

Follow the money (my calculation)

For every RM100 million the fund holds, maximum fees come to about RM840,000 a year. Around RM650,000 goes to the Malaysian manager and trustee. Around RM190,000 goes to the overseas custodian and index provider. The bitcoin itself sits with the US custodian.

There is a fair case for it. Malaysians traded RM17.14 billion on regulated digital asset exchanges in 2025. The ETF pulls some of that demand into CDS accounts, licensed brokers and formal Shariah governance. That is a gain in investor protection.

But it is not capital formation. Ringgit that goes into the fund buys an asset priced in US dollars and held abroad. It does not pay for a Malaysian machine, a farm or a payroll. Shariah-compliant means permissible to hold. Whether it is suitable or safe is a separate question.

Here is the test I will watch. If the ETF raises more in its first year than every tokenised SME financing pilot combined, the new rails are carrying Malaysian savings out faster than they carry credit to Malaysian firms.

04The most expensive toll has no signboard

For small firms, waiting to be paid costs more than the interest rate does.

The government holds itself to a 14-day rule. Under Treasury Instruction AP 103(a), bills must be paid within 14 days of a complete claim. In October 2026, the Ministry of Finance paid all 15,110 of its bills within 14 days, and 97% within seven.

Private buyers have no such rule. Experian's 2025 State of Credit report found that Malaysian companies' trade debts take about 61 days on average to turn into cash. For large corporations it is about 58 days. Construction stood at 76 days at the end of 2024, and transport and logistics reached 76 days in early 2025. Allianz Trade notes that Malaysian law has no specific framework for late payment. Late interest applies only if the contract says so.

Put that next to the tokenisation story. A faster ledger cannot make a buyer pay sooner if the contract says 90 days. It can make it cheaper to borrow against the invoice, which helps. But the SME is still paying interest on money its customer is holding.

Other countries treat this as a legal problem. In May 2026 the UK government announced plans to require large businesses to pay small suppliers within 60 days, possibly falling to 45, with interest at 8% above base rate. Japan's proactive enforcement cut the incidence of late payment from 25% to 12% over 18 years. Korea sets a 60-day legal deadline for subcontract payments. Malaysia has done it once already: the Construction Industry Payment and Adjudication Act 2012 (CIPAA) gives contractors a fast route to settle payment disputes.

Lesson

Rules that depend on small firms suing their customers fail, because suppliers do not sue the buyer they depend on. The Enterprise Research Centre's July 2026 review of late payment laws found that the systems that worked had a regulator that monitored, published and enforced.

05Data is the new collateral. Who holds the keys?

The most valuable thing being built is a verified record of who sold what to whom, and whether it was paid.

Malaysia already has much of it. As at 1 October, more than 268,000 taxpayers had submitted 1.99 billion e-invoices to MyInvois. MDEC's National E-Invoice Financing Exchange (NEFX) will read authenticated e-invoices exchanged on the Peppol network, plus data the SME agrees to share. Its pilot covers data assessment, double-financing checks, loan tracking and consent management. Seven financiers have signed on: CIMB, CIMB Islamic, Affin, Alliance, Funding Societies, CapBay and Bettr. Credit decisions stay with them.

BNM's open finance rules add bank data. Banks with more than a million customers must share individual customers' account data by 1 January 2028. SME account data comes in only by 1 January 2030, and only from providers with more than 30,000 SME customers. The platform, Open Finance Malaysia, is run by PayNet.

Now the gap. On 1 September, the government raised the e-invoice exemption threshold from RM1 million to RM3 million in annual sales. LHDN says more than 1.1 million businesses benefit. The aim, easing compliance costs for small firms, is reasonable.

But put the two decisions side by side. The firms that most need data-based lending are small, young and without property. They are now the least likely to produce the data the new exchange runs on. Firms above RM3 million will reach cheaper, faster credit first. Micro firms stay on the old road, where every application still starts with the same question: do you have land?

Then there is the toll itself. Who pays for verification, API calls and consent? The NEFX MoU announcements did not mention fees, and I could not find a fee schedule for Open Finance Malaysia. When PayNet was formed in 2017, BNM promised open and fair access for banks and eligible non-banks. Those are the right words. The test is the price list, and BNM's plan to reduce its PayNet stake, which the Finance Ministry disclosed in December 2025, makes the question of who owns the national data switch more pressing.

06The cheapest fix is a law, and it is late

Most Malaysian SMEs that apply get a yes. The catch is how much they get, and what the bank wants pledged.

"This is not a story of firms being turned away from banks."
Zafer Mustafaoglu, World Bank division director for the Philippines, Malaysia and Brunei, at the launch of the Malaysia Economic Monitor, 11 November 2026

The World Bank's October 2026 Malaysia Economic Monitor found 37.9% of Malaysian firms have a bank loan or credit line, in line with several high-income economies. The headline approval rate rose from about 68% in 2015 to 86% in 2024. Yet SMEs fund about two-thirds of their fixed-asset investment from their own cash. By value, small firms received 48% of the financing they applied for in 2025, against 74% for large firms.

The World Bank's first recommendation is to put the movable property security interest regime into operation, with a single notice-based registry, so SMEs can borrow against equipment, receivables and stock rather than mainly land and buildings.

Malaysia drafted that law years ago. A personal property securities law was first proposed in 2017, with consultations in 2021 and 2023. The MPSI Bill was meant to pass by the third quarter of 2026. According to The Star on 14 November, it does not appear to have been tabled, and SSM has given no update since 2023.

A notice-based registry is a database with a law behind it. It answers the question every invoice financier asks: has someone already lent against this? NEFX is trying to solve double financing for e-invoices with technology. A registry solves it for every movable asset, by law, including for sole traders and unincorporated businesses that today have no framework at all.

Put plainly: Malaysia launched a distributed-ledger bond platform before passing the law that would let a sundry shop pledge its stock.

07Who gains, who pays

Every new road has winners, and the winners are not always the drivers.

Gains
Big banks

Issue the tokens, run the chains and co-own the data switch. The risk for them is thinner FX, correspondent and float income as settlement speeds up.

Gains from status quo
Large buyers and GLCs

About two months of interest-free supplier credit. They lose the most under a payment-time rule.

Gains
Fund managers, brokers, custodians

Fee pools from ETFs and tokenised products, with part of the custody income going overseas.

Mixed
Fintechs and non-bank lenders

Win if access and fees are open. Lose if the data switch and token networks are gated.

Gains first
SMEs above RM3 million

E-invoice data, NEFX and faster credit reach them first.

At risk
Micro firms below RM3 million

Outside the e-invoice data, and still asked for land or buildings until the MPSI Bill becomes law.

Mixed
Households and EPF members

Easier access to new products, but a bigger capital market does not by itself lift retirement savings.

The Capital Market Masterplan aims to grow the market from RM4.3 trillion in 2025 to RM5.8–6.3 trillion by 2030, and to RM13.8–20 trillion by 2045. Those are market-size projections, not household wealth. As at 31 May 2026, only 38.3% of formal-sector active EPF members aged 18 to 60 had reached their age-based basic savings target. A bigger market does not by itself raise their share.

08Where I could be wrong

The case against my argument
  1. Banks building the rails is a feature. BNM anchors tokens to licensed banks to stop deposits fleeing into private stablecoins. Stability first is a defensible order.
  2. Payment-time laws can backfire. The EU's 2023 proposed late payment regulation collapsed. Buyers may switch to bigger suppliers or demand discounts for faster payment, and SMEs rarely enforce against key customers.
  3. The e-invoice exemption saves real cost for 1.1 million firms. Bank statements and other consented data can fill part of the gap, and the MyInvois portal is free for voluntary users.
  4. The Bitcoin ETF may bring activity onshore, regulated and taxed, and its US dollar counter could draw foreign money.
  5. A collateral registry is not magic. Banks may keep preferring property, and registries take years to earn trust.

Even granting all five, the order still looks wrong for small firms: get paid on time, register the collateral, open the data, then tokenise.

09Eight hard questions

For BNMWhen tokenised deposits leave the sandbox, will a token from one bank move freely to another bank and to licensed non-bank lenders, on published fees?
For MOF and SSMThe MPSI Bill was first proposed in 2017. What stops it being tabled in 2027?

10A practical plan

1
Pay on time, starting with the government's own family

GLCs and GLICs pay SME invoices within 30 days and publish their average days-to-pay twice a year. Then consult on a 60-day cap with statutory late interest for large companies.

Measure: average days-to-pay of the top 100 buyers, published every six months.
2
Table the MPSI Bill in 2027 and build the registry

A notice-based registry covering receivables, stock and equipment, searchable alongside NEFX so financiers see one complete picture.

Measure: registrations a year and the share of SME loans secured by movable assets.
3
Open access written into fees

Any licensed financier can connect to NEFX, Open Finance Malaysia and future tokenised deposit networks on published, non-discriminatory fees.

Measure: number of non-bank participants, and a published fee schedule.
4
Do not let the exemption become exclusion

A free, simple route for firms below RM3 million to build a financing record, through voluntary e-invoices or consented bank statements.

Measure: first-time SME borrowers who used the route.
5
Publish outcomes, not launches

BNM and the SC report each pilot's volume, cost per transaction against today's systems, and the number of SMEs reached.

Measure: an annual public scorecard.
What a business can do in the next 90 days
  • Measure how many days your five biggest customers actually take to pay you.
  • Put payment terms and a late-interest clause in writing. In Malaysia, late interest can only be charged if the contract says so.
  • If you supply large companies, consider joining e-invoicing voluntarily. The MyInvois portal is free, and validated invoices are the data the new financing exchanges will read.
  • Ask your bank or financier whether it is in NEFX or will use open finance, and what it will charge.
  • Before taking invoice financing, compare the all-in cost (fees, rate and recourse), not just the advertised rate.
  • Keep business working capital out of speculative products, Shariah-compliant or not.
To the Government: four dated asks
  1. First Dewan Rakyat meeting of 2027: table the MPSI Bill, with a notice-based registry running within 18 months of passage.
  2. Mid-2027: start a payment-time rule with the government's own family. GLCs and GLICs pay SMEs within 30 days and publish their days-to-pay. Consult on extending it to large listed companies.
  3. End-2027: publish open-access and fee rules for NEFX and Open Finance Malaysia.
  4. From 2027: BNM and the SC publish outcome data for DAIH and sandbox pilots, and let willing providers bring SME data into open finance before 2030.

Self-check
  1. Do I know how many days each of my five biggest customers takes to pay me?
  2. Does my contract say what happens when they pay late?
  3. If a lender asked for 12 months of my sales data tomorrow, could I produce it?
  4. Am I buying the Bitcoin ETF because I understand it, or because it is new and Shariah-compliant?
  5. Who owns the platform I am about to join, and can I leave with my data?
My take

Malaysia is right to build these rails, and BNM has built the settlement layer well. My concern is the order.

For a small firm, the sequence that creates wealth is simple: get paid on time, be able to pledge what you own, let your data speak for you, and only then make it programmable. We are doing it the other way round.

If we keep this order, the new highway will be faster and the same people will collect the tolls. The fix is a payment-time rule, a collateral law and open access written into fee schedules. All three cost less than a new platform. They are also harder to launch with a ribbon.
