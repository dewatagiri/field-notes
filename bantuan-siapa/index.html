Who Is Our Help Really Helping?

Five decades of Bumiputera enterprise support have built real champions, and a far larger number of businesses that never stood on their own. Before the PuTERA35 review closes, we should ask a harder question than "how much did we give?"

Azman Taufik22 October 202618 min read
The short version
  • MSMEs produce 39.5% of GDP and 48.7% of jobs, but only 14.3% of exports. For every medium-sized firm there are almost 39 micro firms.
  • Bumiputera are 63.6% of the population but own 29.8% of enterprises, after fifty years of targeted support.
  • The problem is not too little help. It is help with no market attached, and a system that rewards giving more than it rewards graduating.
  • Nobody in the system is paid to make a business stop needing support. Until that changes, we will keep producing participants, not entrepreneurs.
  • The government is reviewing PuTERA35 now. This article closes with seven practical changes and a direct ask: measure graduation, publish it, and tie every ringgit to it.

There is a question I have rarely heard asked in a meeting about Bumiputera entrepreneurs: if we stopped every programme tomorrow, how many of these businesses would still be standing in three years?

We ask how much was approved, how many people were trained, how many contracts were awarded. These are easy to count and easy to announce. The harder question is whether the help worked: whether it left behind a business that no longer needs it.

This is not an argument against helping. Every successful economy supports young firms, and Malaysia's support for Bumiputera enterprise has achieved a great deal. It is an argument about what kind of help we give, what we expect in return, and how we know it worked. Those three questions decide whether help builds people up or holds them in place.

01The upper hand: what help is for

Malay and Islamic tradition already holds the principle we keep forgetting in policy. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The upper hand is better than the lower hand", meaning the hand that gives is better than the hand that receives (Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim). The aim was never to keep people receiving. It was to bring them to a position where they could give.

A well-known narration makes the method clear. A man came asking for charity. Instead of giving him money, the Prophet asked what he owned, sold the man's few belongings, and used the proceeds to buy an axe. He told him to cut firewood and sell it, and to come back in fifteen days. The man returned having earned more than he would have received as charity (reported by Abu Dawud).

That is development finance in one story. Not a handout. Not a lecture. A tool, a market, a deadline, and a check-back.

Three principles worth holding on to
  1. Good help ends the need for help. A programme that has the same people returning after ten years has not succeeded; it has become a subsidy.
  2. Expecting something back is a form of respect. When we set conditions, ask for repayment and hold people to milestones, we treat them as capable adults. When we give with no expectation, we quietly tell them we do not believe they can do it.
  3. Help given out of pity keeps people small. Help given out of belief helps them grow. The difference is in the expectations, not the amount.

The New Economic Policy was built on two aims: eradicating poverty, and restructuring society so that race would no longer be identified with economic function. Ownership targets were a means to that end, not the end itself. Somewhere along the way, the means became the measure.

Most businesses never leave the bottom rung. Seven in ten MSMEs are micro. The climb from micro to small to medium is where most stall, and it is where support is thinnest.

The ownership gap remains. Bumiputera are almost two-thirds of the population but own fewer than one in three enterprises. PuTERA35 targets Bumiputera-owned companies contributing 31% of GDP by 2035. In September 2026 the Economy Minister said the target will be reviewed, with a progress report expected around the end of this year.

And the market is mostly local. Most MSMEs never export, and many Bumiputera firms remain inside the government and GLC market. A business that only knows one buyer has not yet been tested.

What the data tells us

Most agencies report inputs: grants approved, loans disbursed, people trained. In March 2026, IDEAS found that Bumiputera trade and vendor programmes are judged mainly by participation and funds disbursed, that "graduation" is not clearly defined, and that no single database tracks a business from entry to exit.

The programmes that can show real outcomes, such as TERAJU's survival rate and UEM Edgenta's revenue growth, share one feature: support is tied to a buyer, a market or a milestone.

04Who succeeds, and why

The winners are not the businesses that received the most help. They are the ones that used help to reach a customer who pays on commercial terms.

In the cities

Halal FMCG maker Al-Meswak Mu'min used TERAJU backing to expand manufacturing and move into mainstream retail. SumiSaujana Group and Orkim listed on Bursa Malaysia within a year of PuTERA35's launch. A listing is the clearest form of graduation: the business now raises capital from investors, not agencies.

In the kampung

  • FELDA: about 15,779 entrepreneurs, mainly in trading and livestock, generating an estimated RM1.5b a year.
  • Banghuris Homestay, Sepang: three villages share bookings, standards and marketing, and run as one business.
  • Kampung Sungai Kubang Badak, Langkawi: most of 82 fishing and farming entrepreneurs already sell online, riding the Geopark tourist flow.
  • Women exporters: RM229m in potential export sales through WiEX at MIHAS 2026.
What they did right
  1. Sales first, support second. A retailer, a contract with performance terms, a tourist flow, an export order.
  2. Certification before expansion. Halal, MeSTI, GMP or homestay standards opened doors grants could not.
  3. Strength in numbers. Cooperatives, clusters and shared kitchens gave small players real volume.
  4. A relationship with a bank. Moving from agency loans to bank credit, often with a guarantee, built a credit record.
  5. Profits put back into the business before lifestyle upgrades.

05Eight traps that keep businesses small

The most dangerous problems are forms of help that feel like progress but quietly remove the pressure to grow. Each one makes sense to the entrepreneur in the short term, which is exactly why they last.

1. Help with no conditions

Grants with no targets. The business learns to fill in application forms, not to find customers.

Way out
Release support in stages, against results: sales, certification, hiring.

2. Grant chasers

The same person moves from one agency to the next. Nobody sees the full picture.

Way out
One business reference number shared by every agency.

3. Loans treated as gifts

Agency loans are seen as "government money". TEKUN carries RM340m in arrears over 24 months. A default damages the credit record and closes the door to banks for years.

Way out
Make agency loans a stepping stone to bank loans.

4. One contract, one customer

Living on a quota or a single GLC buyer. When the contract ends, so does the business.

Way out
Vendor programmes with a clear end date.

5. Ali Baba

A Bumiputera licence or company name is "lent" out. The owner collects a fee and learns nothing.

Way out
Check who actually manages, works and owns the assets.

6. Staying small on purpose

Support stops the moment a business crosses a size limit, so growth is punished. Korea calls it "Peter Pan syndrome".

Way out
Reduce support gradually; reward growth.

7. Too many programmes

Dozens of schemes, each agency counting its own participants. Entrepreneurs spend their energy finding the right door.

Way out
One front door, one scorecard.

8. Everyone in the same trade

Kuih, food stalls, small retail. Easy to start, thin margins, hard to grow.

Way out
Steer support to sectors with room to grow.

On "free" help

Help itself is not the problem. The problem is help that is unconditional, repeated and disconnected from a market.

A simple test: when the programme ends, does the business have regular paying customers, clean financial records and a skill it did not have before? If not, the money was spent, not invested.

06Who benefits when a business stays small?

This is the uncomfortable part. Traps persist not because people are careless, but because almost everyone in the system gains something from the way things are.

07What other countries learned

Countries that succeeded attached conditions to support and reduced it gradually. Countries that failed handed over ownership or contracts without building the ability to run them.

Worked

Indonesia

KUR micro-credit runs through banks: Rp270 trillion to 4.58m borrowers in 2025. The government tracks how many borrowers move up a business category.

Lesson
Lend through banks; publish who moves up.

Worked

Taiwan

SMEs grew as suppliers to export manufacturers, backed by credit guarantees and public research institutes.

Lesson
Plug small firms into export supply chains.

Worked, with a catch

South Korea

Strong SME growth, but many firms avoid growing to keep benefits. In 2025 Seoul moved to activity-based support that tapers gradually.

Lesson
Never cut support off at a cliff.

Partly worked

Malaysia

Poverty fell sharply and a Bumiputera middle class formed. The 30% equity target is still unmet after 50 years.

Lesson
Shift from "how much we own" to "how much we produce and export".

Fell short

South Africa

Under BEE, critics report gains concentrated among a politically connected few, widespread fronting, and failed projects by firms that won tenders without capacity.

Lesson
Ownership without capability creates middlemen.

Failed

Zimbabwe

The 2008 Indigenisation Act moved over 51% of businesses into local hands. Investors left and an economy already in crisis got worse.

Lesson
Forced transfer shrinks the pie.

Malaysia sits in the middle. We avoided Zimbabwe's path and have stronger institutions than South Africa. But we share South Africa's fronting problem and Korea's stay-small problem. The fixes Korea and Indonesia now use are within our reach.

08Questions we must have the courage to ask

Good policy starts with honest questions. These are the ones I believe every minister, agency head, banker and entrepreneur should sit with.

If the same businesses keep coming back to the same programme for ten years, is that programme succeeding or failing?For agencies and their boards
Why do we announce how much we gave, but never how many businesses no longer need us?For the government
Are we protecting Bumiputera entrepreneurs, or protecting them from the competition they need in order to grow?For policymakers
Who in the system is actually rewarded when a business graduates? If the answer is "no one", why are we surprised?For everyone in the system
Would we raise our own children by giving them pocket money forever, with no expectation of independence?For all of us
Is the Bumiputera agenda about owning a share of the economy, or about being able to build one?For the community
When a loan from an agency goes unpaid, who pays the real price: the agency, or the next entrepreneur who is refused?For borrowers
If support were cut tomorrow, would my business survive? If not, what am I doing about it this year?For entrepreneurs

09Seven practical changes

None of these needs a bigger budget. They need the same budget tied to results. Each can start within the current Thirteenth Malaysia Plan and the PuTERA35 review.

1

Publish a national Graduation Scorecard every year

One number that matters: how many supported businesses moved from micro to small, small to medium, became bankable, or started exporting within three years. Publish it by agency and by programme.

Measure: share of supported firms that moved up a size band within 3 years.

2

One business ID across every agency

Use the SSM registration number as a shared key so MARA, TEKUN, PUNB, SME Corp, TERAJU, SME Bank and the GLICs can see the same support history. Grant-chasing becomes visible overnight.

Measure: share of programmes reporting to one shared database.

3

A sunset clause on every programme

No business should receive the same type of support indefinitely. Set a maximum period, and taper support gradually as revenue grows, instead of cutting it off at a size threshold.

Measure: share of beneficiaries who exit support within the set period.

4

Release grants in stages, against milestones

Pay in tranches tied to a first sale, a certification, a new hire or a bank account opened with proper records. Unused or unearned tranches return to the pool for the next entrepreneur.

Measure: share of grant value released against verified milestones.

5

Make agency credit a bridge to bank credit

Report every agency loan repayment to CCRIS so good borrowers build a record. Use credit guarantees so banks can take over lending once a business has a buyer and a track record, as Indonesia does with KUR.

Measure: share of agency borrowers who obtain a bank facility within 3 years.

6

Vendor programmes with an exit, not a home

GLC and PETRONAS vendor programmes should require suppliers to grow revenue from customers outside the anchor buyer, and should have a fixed end date.

Measure: share of vendor revenue from non-anchor customers.

7

Make fronting unprofitable

Verify beneficial ownership, who manages the business and who holds the assets for any Bumiputera-status contract or licence. Enforce penalties, and publish them.

Measure: number of verified checks and enforcement actions published each year.

10A call to action

To the Government

The PuTERA35 progress report is due around the end of this year. That is the window. I urge the Ministry of Economy and the Bumiputera Economic Council to use it to:

  1. Adopt graduation as the headline measure of the Bumiputera enterprise agenda, alongside ownership.
  2. Publish the first national Graduation Scorecard, by agency and programme, within twelve months.
  3. Mandate one shared business ID and one outcomes database across all Bumiputera-mandated agencies and GLICs.
  4. Write sunset and taper clauses into every new programme, and review existing ones against them.

A five-question check for any entrepreneur
  1. Do I have paying customers who would stay if the support ended?
  2. Could a bank read my accounts and understand my business?
  3. Have I repaid every agency loan on time?
  4. Do I have at least one certification my market expects?
  5. Do I have a date by which I plan to stop needing support?

Three or more "no" answers mean the next step is not another grant. It is the work these questions point to.

My take

After fifty years, the question is no longer whether we should help Bumiputera entrepreneurs. We should, and we will. The question is whether our help is designed to end.

The upper hand is better than the lower hand. A system that keeps people receiving, however generous, has not yet done its job. The real measure of the Bumiputera agenda is not how much we hand out, or even how much we own. It is how many of our businesses no longer need us, and go on to help the next ones up.

Over to you: which of these questions is hardest for your organisation to answer honestly? And what one change would you make first? I would like to hear from entrepreneurs, bankers, agency officers and policymakers alike.

Bantuan Kita Sebenarnya Membantu Siapa?

Sudah lima dekad negara menyalurkan bantuan kepada usahawan Bumiputera. Sebahagiannya berjaya membina syarikat yang kukuh. Namun, jauh lebih ramai yang masih belum mampu berdiri sendiri. Sebelum kajian semula PuTERA35 dimuktamadkan, ada persoalan yang lebih mustahak daripada "berapa banyak yang telah kita salurkan?"

Azman Taufik22 Oktober 202618 minit bacaan
Ringkasan pantas
  • PMKS menyumbang 39.5% KDNK dan 48.7% pekerjaan, tetapi hanya 14.3% eksport. Bagi setiap satu syarikat sederhana, ada hampir 39 perniagaan mikro.
  • Bumiputera merangkumi 63.6% penduduk, tetapi selepas lima dekad dibantu, hanya memiliki 29.8% perniagaan.
  • Isunya bukan kekurangan bantuan, tetapi bantuan yang tidak dikaitkan dengan pasaran, serta sistem yang lebih menghargai tindakan memberi berbanding kejayaan usahawan berdikari.
  • Dalam sistem sedia ada, tiada pihak yang diberi ganjaran apabila sesebuah perniagaan tidak lagi memerlukan bantuan. Selagi hal ini tidak diubah, kita akan terus melahirkan peserta program, bukan usahawan.
  • Kerajaan sedang mengkaji semula PuTERA35. Artikel ini dirumuskan dengan tujuh cadangan praktikal dan satu gesaan: ukur kejayaan usahawan naik taraf, umumkan hasilnya, dan kaitkan setiap ringgit dengannya.

Ada satu persoalan yang jarang sekali saya dengar dibangkitkan dalam mesyuarat tentang usahawan Bumiputera: sekiranya semua program dihentikan esok, berapa banyakkah perniagaan ini yang masih mampu bertahan tiga tahun kemudian?

Kita lazimnya bertanya berapa banyak yang diluluskan, berapa ramai yang dilatih dan berapa kontrak yang diberikan. Angka-angka ini mudah dikira dan mudah diumumkan. Persoalan yang lebih sukar ialah sama ada bantuan itu benar-benar berkesan: adakah ia meninggalkan perniagaan yang tidak lagi memerlukannya?

Ini bukan hujah untuk menghentikan bantuan. Setiap negara maju menyokong usahawan baharu, dan sokongan kepada usahawan Bumiputera telah banyak membuahkan hasil. Hujah ini berkisar tentang bentuk bantuan yang diberikan, apa yang kita harapkan sebagai balasan, dan bagaimana kita mengetahui keberkesanannya. Tiga perkara inilah yang menentukan sama ada bantuan mengangkat seseorang, atau sekadar mengekalkannya di takuk lama.

01Tangan yang di atas: apa tujuan sebenar bantuan

Prinsip yang sering terlepas pandang dalam penggubalan dasar sebenarnya telah lama terkandung dalam ajaran kita. Rasulullah SAW bersabda: "Tangan yang di atas lebih baik daripada tangan yang di bawah", iaitu tangan yang memberi lebih baik daripada tangan yang menerima (Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim). Matlamatnya bukan untuk menjadikan seseorang terus menerima, tetapi untuk membawanya ke tahap mampu memberi.

Terdapat satu kisah yang masyhur. Seorang lelaki datang meminta sedekah. Baginda tidak terus memberinya wang, sebaliknya bertanya apa yang dimilikinya, lalu menjual barangannya yang tidak seberapa dan menggunakan hasilnya untuk membeli sebilah kapak. Baginda menyuruhnya mencari kayu api untuk dijual dan kembali selepas lima belas hari. Lelaki itu kembali dengan pendapatan yang melebihi apa yang mungkin diperolehnya melalui sedekah (riwayat Abu Dawud).

Itulah intipati pembiayaan pembangunan dalam satu kisah. Bukan wang percuma, bukan juga sekadar nasihat. Alat untuk bekerja, pasaran untuk menjual, tempoh yang jelas, dan pemantauan susulan terhadap hasilnya.

Tiga prinsip yang wajar kita pegang
  1. Bantuan yang baik ialah bantuan yang akhirnya tidak diperlukan lagi. Jika penerima yang sama masih kembali ke program yang sama selepas sepuluh tahun, program itu belum berjaya. Ia telah bertukar menjadi subsidi.
  2. Meletakkan syarat ialah tanda hormat. Apabila kita menuntut pinjaman dibayar dan menetapkan sasaran, kita meletakkan kepercayaan bahawa usahawan itu mampu. Apabila kita memberi tanpa sebarang harapan, secara tidak langsung kita menyatakan bahawa kita tidak yakin dengan kemampuannya.
  3. Bantuan atas dasar simpati mengekalkan seseorang di tempatnya. Bantuan atas dasar kepercayaan membolehkannya berkembang. Perbezaannya terletak pada harapan yang diletakkan, bukan pada jumlahnya.

Dasar Ekonomi Baru dibina atas dua matlamat: membasmi kemiskinan, dan menyusun semula masyarakat supaya kaum tidak lagi dikenali berdasarkan fungsi ekonomi. Sasaran pemilikan ialah alat untuk mencapai matlamat tersebut, bukan matlamat itu sendiri. Namun, lama-kelamaan, alat itu bertukar menjadi ukuran.

Kebanyakan perniagaan tidak pernah melepasi anak tangga pertama. Tujuh daripada sepuluh PMKS ialah perniagaan mikro. Peralihan daripada mikro ke kecil, dan seterusnya ke sederhana, merupakan fasa paling mencabar. Malangnya, pada fasa inilah sokongan paling terhad.

Kebanyakan agensi melaporkan jumlah geran yang diluluskan, pinjaman yang disalurkan dan peserta yang dilatih. Jarang sekali yang melaporkan kedudukan perniagaan tersebut tiga tahun kemudian. Pada Mac 2026, IDEAS mendapati program vendor dan perdagangan Bumiputera lebih banyak diukur berdasarkan penyertaan dan jumlah dana. Tiada definisi yang jelas tentang bilakah seseorang usahawan dianggap telah "bergraduasi" daripada program, dan tiada pangkalan data tunggal yang menjejak perniagaan dari awal hingga akhir.

Program yang dapat menunjukkan hasil sebenar, seperti kadar kelangsungan syarikat di bawah TERAJU dan pertumbuhan jualan vendor UEM Edgenta, mempunyai satu persamaan: bantuan dikaitkan dengan pembeli, pasaran atau sasaran yang jelas.

04Siapa yang berjaya, dan mengapa

Mereka yang berjaya bukanlah penerima bantuan terbanyak, tetapi mereka yang menggunakan bantuan itu untuk memperoleh pelanggan yang membayar atas dasar komersial.

Kejayaan di bandar

Pengeluar produk halal Al-Meswak Mu'min menggunakan sokongan TERAJU untuk membesarkan kilang dan menembusi pasaran runcit arus perdana. SumiSaujana Group dan Orkim pula disenaraikan di Bursa Malaysia dalam tempoh setahun selepas PuTERA35 dilancarkan. Apabila sesebuah syarikat tersenarai, ia mampu mengumpul modal daripada pelabur dan tidak lagi bergantung pada agensi.

Kejayaan di luar bandar

  • FELDA: kira-kira 15,779 usahawan, kebanyakannya dalam sektor peruncitan dan penternakan, menjana anggaran RM1.5 bilion setahun.
  • Homestay Banghuris, Sepang: tiga kampung bergabung dan berkongsi tempahan, standard serta promosi, lalu beroperasi seperti sebuah syarikat.
  • Kampung Sungai Kubang Badak, Langkawi: sebahagian besar daripada 82 usahawan nelayan dan petani telah menjual secara dalam talian, memanfaatkan kehadiran pelancong ke Geopark.
  • Usahawan wanita: mencatat RM229 juta potensi jualan eksport melalui WiEX di MIHAS 2026.
Faktor kejayaan mereka
  1. Jualan dahulu, bantuan kemudian. Sama ada melalui pasar raya, kontrak bersyarat prestasi, pelancong atau pesanan eksport, pelanggan diperoleh terlebih dahulu.
  2. Pensijilan sebelum perluasan. Pensijilan halal, MeSTI, GMP atau homestay membuka peluang yang tidak mampu dibuka oleh geran.
  3. Kekuatan secara berkelompok. Koperasi, kluster dan dapur berkongsi membolehkan pengusaha kecil mencapai skala pengeluaran yang besar.
  4. Hubungan dengan institusi perbankan. Mereka beralih daripada pinjaman agensi kepada pembiayaan bank, lazimnya dengan sokongan jaminan, lalu membina rekod kredit.
  5. Keuntungan dilaburkan semula ke dalam perniagaan, bukan dibelanjakan untuk menaik taraf gaya hidup.

05Lapan perangkap yang membuatkan usahawan kekal kecil

Perangkap yang paling berbahaya bukanlah yang paling ketara. Ia hadir dalam bentuk bantuan yang kelihatan seperti kemajuan, tetapi secara perlahan menghakis dorongan untuk berkembang. Dalam jangka pendek, setiap satunya kelihatan wajar bagi usahawan. Itulah sebabnya ia terus berlarutan.

1. Bantuan tanpa syarat

Geran diberikan tanpa sasaran. Usahawan menjadi mahir mengisi borang permohonan, tetapi tidak mahir mencari pelanggan.

Penyelesaian
Salurkan bantuan secara berperingkat berdasarkan pencapaian: jualan, pensijilan, pengambilan pekerja.

2. Pemburu geran

Pemohon yang sama berpindah dari satu agensi ke agensi yang lain. Tiada pihak yang melihat gambaran keseluruhan.

Penyelesaian
Satu nombor rujukan perniagaan yang dikongsi oleh semua agensi.

3. Pinjaman dianggap pemberian

Pinjaman agensi dianggap "wang kerajaan" yang tidak perlu dibayar balik. TEKUN mencatat RM340 juta tunggakan melebihi 24 bulan. Kegagalan membayar merosakkan rekod kredit dan menutup pintu pembiayaan bank untuk tempoh yang lama.

Penyelesaian
Jadikan pinjaman agensi batu loncatan ke pembiayaan bank.

4. Satu kontrak, satu pelanggan

Bergantung sepenuhnya pada kuota atau seorang pembeli GLC. Apabila kontrak tamat, perniagaan turut terhenti.

Penyelesaian
Program vendor dengan tempoh tamat yang jelas.

5. Ali Baba

Lesen atau nama syarikat Bumiputera "dipinjamkan" kepada pihak lain. Pemiliknya menerima komisen, tetapi tidak membina sebarang keupayaan.

Penyelesaian
Sahkan pihak yang sebenarnya mengurus, bekerja dan memiliki aset.

6. Sengaja kekal kecil

Bantuan terhenti sebaik sahaja saiz perniagaan melepasi had tertentu, menyebabkan usahawan enggan berkembang. Di Korea, fenomena ini dikenali sebagai "sindrom Peter Pan".

Penyelesaian
Kurangkan bantuan secara beransur-ansur dan beri insentif untuk berkembang.

7. Program yang bertindih

Berpuluh-puluh skim, dengan setiap agensi mengira pesertanya sendiri. Usahawan menghabiskan masa mencari program yang sesuai.

Penyelesaian
Satu pusat sehenti, satu ukuran prestasi.

8. Tertumpu pada sektor yang sama

Kuih-muih, gerai makanan dan peruncitan kecil. Mudah dimulakan, tetapi margin nipis dan sukar dikembangkan.

Penyelesaian
Salurkan bantuan ke sektor yang mempunyai ruang pertumbuhan.

Tentang bantuan "percuma"

Bantuan itu sendiri tidak salah. Yang menjadi masalah ialah bantuan yang tanpa syarat, diberikan berulang kali dan tidak dikaitkan dengan pasaran.

Ujian mudahnya: apabila program tamat, adakah usahawan itu sudah mempunyai pelanggan tetap, rekod kewangan yang teratur dan kemahiran yang tidak dimilikinya sebelum ini? Jika tidak, wang tersebut sekadar dibelanjakan, bukan dilaburkan untuk membina perniagaan.

06Siapa yang untung apabila usahawan kekal kecil?

Bahagian ini mungkin kurang selesa untuk dibincangkan. Perangkap-perangkap tadi berterusan bukan kerana kecuaian, tetapi kerana hampir setiap pihak dalam sistem memperoleh sesuatu daripada keadaan sedia ada.

07Belajar daripada negara lain

Negara yang berjaya meletakkan syarat pada bantuan dan mengurangkannya secara berperingkat. Negara yang gagal pula memindahkan pemilikan atau kontrak kepada golongan tertentu tanpa membina keupayaan mereka untuk mengurusnya.

Berjaya

Indonesia

Pinjaman mikro KUR disalurkan melalui bank: Rp270 trilion kepada 4.58 juta peminjam pada 2025. Kerajaan memantau bilangan peminjam yang meningkat ke kategori perniagaan yang lebih besar.

Pengajaran
Salurkan pembiayaan melalui bank; umumkan bilangan yang naik taraf.

Berjaya

Taiwan

PKS berkembang sebagai pembekal kepada pengilang eksport, disokong skim jaminan kredit dan institut penyelidikan kerajaan.

Pengajaran
Hubungkan perusahaan kecil dengan rantaian bekalan eksport.

Berjaya, tetapi ada kelemahan

Korea Selatan

PKS berkembang pesat, tetapi ramai yang sengaja tidak membesar demi mengekalkan bantuan. Pada 2025, kerajaan Korea mengubah pendekatan: bantuan berasaskan aktiviti dan dikurangkan secara beransur-ansur.

Pengajaran
Elakkan pemberhentian bantuan secara mendadak.

Sebahagian berjaya

Malaysia

Kadar kemiskinan menurun dengan ketara dan kelas menengah Bumiputera berjaya dibentuk. Namun, sasaran ekuiti 30% masih belum tercapai selepas 50 tahun.

Pengajaran
Ubah fokus daripada "berapa banyak kita miliki" kepada "berapa banyak kita hasilkan dan eksport".

Tidak capai matlamat

Afrika Selatan

Di bawah dasar BEE, pengkritik mendakwa manfaatnya tertumpu kepada segelintir pihak yang mempunyai hubungan politik. Amalan "pinjam nama" berleluasa, dan banyak projek gagal kerana syarikat yang memenangi tender tidak berkeupayaan.

Pengajaran
Pemilikan tanpa keupayaan hanya melahirkan orang tengah.

Gagal

Zimbabwe

Akta 2008 mewajibkan lebih 51% pemilikan perniagaan dipindahkan kepada pemilik tempatan. Pelabur menarik diri, dan ekonomi yang sedia tenat bertambah buruk.

Pengajaran
Pemindahan milik secara paksa hanya mengecilkan kek ekonomi.

Malaysia berada di pertengahan. Kita tidak menempuh jalan Zimbabwe, dan institusi kita lebih kukuh berbanding Afrika Selatan. Namun, kita berdepan masalah "pinjam nama" seperti Afrika Selatan, serta masalah usahawan yang sengaja kekal kecil seperti Korea. Penyelesaian yang kini diguna pakai oleh Korea dan Indonesia sebenarnya berada dalam jangkauan kita.

08Persoalan yang perlu kita berani tanyakan

Dasar yang baik bermula dengan persoalan yang jujur. Berikut ialah persoalan yang wajar direnungkan oleh setiap menteri, ketua agensi, jurubank dan usahawan.
