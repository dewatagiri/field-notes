Equity guarantee: an oxymoron, or a tool we have not shaped yet?

Bank Negara's RM1 billion recapitalisation facility found almost no takers. The reasons are clear. So are the two narrow places where a guarantee on risk capital can still work.

By Azman Taufik · 27 November 2026 · 10 min readComment on LinkedIn

The short version
  • BNM's RM1 billion BRF offered SMEs equity or debt-plus-equity from February 2022. In October 2023 BNM still listed the full RM1,000 million as available, and its 2023 annual report slides showed no approvals.
  • Banks stayed away for three reasons: equity sits outside their normal business and needs BNM approval, it eats capital (150% risk weight today, 1250% if the firm becomes a subsidiary), and the equity holder is paid last with no clear exit.
  • Guaranteeing pure equity is a losing trade for the guarantor. Guaranteeing a term-limited, profit-sharing stake is not: German guarantee banks cover 25–80% of such stakes.
  • Two doors are open in Malaysia: social finance, where the funder already accepts the loss (iTEKAD has reached 14,387 microentrepreneurs), and the crowd on SC-registered platforms, where MyCIF already invests on a first-loss basis.
  • My proposal: a guaranteed portfolio of 5 to 10 short-cycle projects (korban cattle, a padi season), with the guarantor covering part of the capital and sharing the upside.

Can you guarantee someone's equity and still call it equity? A finance veteran I worked with years ago said no. To him, "equity guarantee" was an oxymoron. Equity is the money that takes the loss first and gets paid last. Guarantee it, and you remove the very thing that makes it equity.

He was mostly right. Malaysia tested the idea at national scale and the market answered. But "mostly" leaves a gap, and the gap sits exactly where small and rural businesses need help.

This note looks at what happened to Bank Negara's Business Recapitalisation Facility, why banks stayed away, and where a guarantee on risk capital can still work.

01 · What the BRF offered

The BRF was announced in Budget 2022 and opened on 3 February 2022 with RM1 billion under BNM's Fund for SMEs. Its aim was sensible: help SMEs grow without piling on more debt.

SMEs could take equity through preference shares, ordinary shares or equity-like instruments. Or they could take blended finance: bank debt plus equity from third-party equity financiers.

The terms were generous. Up to RM5 million per SME, up to 10 years, no collateral. The debt portion was capped at 3.5% a year without a guarantee and 5% with one. There was no cap on the equity return. Guarantee cover for the debt portion was available too.

02 · What happened

What the data says

BNM's table of SME facilities dated 21 October 2023, about 20 months after launch, listed the BRF with RM1,000 million available, the same amount it opened with.

BNM's 2023 annual report slides (March 2024) showed only dashes in the BRF row for approvals and utilisation. I could not find a later public figure.

People in the industry describe the take-up as close to zero, and BNM's own tables fit that. BNM itself said in June 2024 that it was redesigning how it builds SME funds, after only a third of another facility had been used.

A fund nobody draws on is not a funding problem. It is a design problem.

03 · Why banks walked away

It is not their business

Under the Financial Services Act 2013, a licensed bank may not carry on business outside its authorised business unless BNM allows it. Holding a material interest in a corporation needs BNM's approval under section 85. I did not find a clause that bans a bank outright from holding SME equity. The law puts a gate in front of it, and few banks want to queue at that gate for a RM300,000 stake.

It eats capital

Under BNM's current rules, unlisted equity and venture capital carry a 150% risk weight. A stake in a non-financial subsidiary carries 1250%. The Basel III global standard is harsher still: 250% for equity, 400% for speculative unlisted equity.

This is where the "100% capital" view comes from. If the stake is big enough to make the SME a subsidiary, a bank holds roughly a ringgit of capital for every ringgit it puts in.

Last in line, and no way out

Equity is paid after every creditor. Shares in a small private firm have no market, so an exit needs another willing buyer. A bank's credit model prices default and recovery. It has no line for "maybe in seven years, if someone buys".

Investors feel the same. On SC-registered platforms in 2025, P2P financing raised about twenty times what equity crowdfunding did.

04 · So, is it an oxymoron?

For a bank, yes. For a guarantor, it depends on what is being guaranteed.

Guarantee a full equity return and the guarantor carries the downside while the investor keeps the upside. That loses money every cycle. But guarantee part of a stake that has a fixed term, a base return and a share of profit, and the arithmetic changes. Germany has done this for decades.

Worked
Germany
17 guarantee banks and 15 SME investment companies (MBGs). MBGs take silent partnerships with no say in daily business. Guarantee banks cover 25–80% of those stakes, backed by federal and state counter-guarantees.
Failed
Malaysia, BRF
Open-ended equity offered through banks that are not built to hold it. No exit design and no guarantee on the equity itself. The money sat unused.
Working, small
Malaysia, MyCIF
Public money invests with the crowd: RM1 for every RM4 raised (RM1 for RM2 in food security). It invests on a first-loss basis in Islamic risk-sharing campaigns. RM1.5bn co-invested for 11,512 MSMEs to Q4 2025.

The lesson from Germany is the instrument. A silent partnership sits between debt and equity. It has an end date. That is what makes it possible to guarantee.

05 · Door one: social finance

Here the oxymoron disappears, because the funder has already accepted the loss. Zakat, waqf and CSR money is given to build livelihoods, not to earn a return.

BNM's iTEKAD has combined that money with seed capital, training and microfinance since May 2020. It had reached 14,387 microentrepreneurs by end-March 2026. By end-2025 it had disbursed RM94.2 million in social funds and RM77.2 million in financing and investment.

A guarantee can make each ringgit of social money go further. The social fund takes the first loss, the guarantor takes the next slice, and the bank or platform funds the rest. The zakat ringgit stops being spent once and starts working as a buffer, again and again.

06 · Door two: the crowd with an appetite for risk

Some investors do not mind a small loss if they can win more. I would not call them gamblers. They are natural risk takers, and the platforms already reach them: institutional investors were 2% of ECF and P2P investors in 2024 but put in 60% of the money.

A note on terms. P2P in Malaysia is financing, not equity. A profit-sharing stake would go through equity crowdfunding, or through a Shariah-compliant structure such as mudarabah or musharakah. Shariah-compliant deals were 30% of ECF and P2P funds raised in 2024.

A kampung example

Take a villager with two or three idle acres. He needs RM10,000 to buy five young cattle and four months to fatten them before Aidiladha. He hopes to sell them for RM25,000. News reports put korban cattle at roughly RM4,800 to RM6,440 a head in 2025 and 2026, so RM5,000 each is a fair sale assumption. The buying price, feed and any deaths decide the real profit. These are my working numbers, not a costed plan.

The same shape fits a padi season of about four months, or a farmer whose children have all moved to town and who needs working capital to plant. One project on its own is too risky to guarantee. A pool of 5 to 10 similar short-cycle projects, sold as one portfolio, is a different thing.

Illustration, not data. My assumptions: RM100,000 pool, ten RM10,000 projects, a successful project returns 12% to investors in one cycle, a failed project returns nothing. Guarantee covers 50% of capital lost, for a 1% fee and 20% of profit.

On these numbers the guarantor earns RM3,400 on a clean pool and loses RM1,840 if one project fails. It breaks even only if fewer than about 6.5% of projects fail outright. So the guarantor must never stand first. The owner's own money and a social or MyCIF first-loss layer must sit below it.

The case against, in its strongest form
  • Annualising misleads. A korban cycle comes once a year. "400% a year" is one good season multiplied by three seasons that do not exist.
  • Livestock is risky. Disease, deaths, and cheap smuggled cattle that farmers say undercut prices, as reported in May 2026.
  • Fraud and moral hazard. Small tickets, far from town, hard to check. A guarantee can make investors stop asking questions.
  • Due diligence is costly against a RM10,000 ticket. The platform may not earn enough to check properly.
  • Shariah structure. A guaranteed return breaks a mudarabah. Any capital guarantee must come from an independent third party, and a fee-based guarantee needs a Shariah adviser's ruling.
  • No exit before the cycle ends. There is no secondary market. Investors are locked in for the season.
  • Adverse selection. The weakest projects will seek the guarantee first.

07 · Hard questions

For BNMWill the lessons of the BRF be published before the next SME fund is designed, so the same mistake is not repeated?
For the SCCan a single campaign offer a pool of 5 to 10 projects, with one guarantee over the pool?
For guarantee agenciesIs a guarantee on part of a profit-sharing stake within your mandate, and how would you price the upside share?
For zakat and waqf bodiesWould you place a first-loss layer under a guarantee, if it lets the same fund support three times as many asnaf?
For platformsCan you check a cattle project in Pasir Mas as well as a tech start-up in Bangsar, at a cost that makes sense?
For Shariah advisersWhich structure lets a third party guarantee capital, and take a share of profit, without breaking the contract?

08 · A practical plan

1
Start with social finance. Pilot a guarantee under an iTEKAD-style programme, with zakat or CSR money taking the first loss.Measure: number of entrepreneurs funded per ringgit of social money, against the current grant-only model.
2
Run one portfolio for one season. Pool 5 to 10 korban cattle projects for the 2027 Aidiladha season on one Shariah-compliant platform.Measure: projects completed, loss rate, investor return, time from funding to sale.
3
Fix the first-loss order in writing. Owner's stake first, social or MyCIF layer second, guarantor third, investors last.Measure: guarantor losses stay inside the agreed slice in every cycle.
4
Add a second cycle type. A padi season or a harvest cycle, so the portfolio does not ride on one Raya.Measure: losses in one project type do not move the other.
5
Review after two cycles, then decide. Scale up, change the terms, or stop.Measure: a public one-page result, good or bad.
What I am asking for

To Bank Negara and the Securities Commission: within 2027, publish what the BRF taught us, and agree a sandbox for one guaranteed project portfolio on an Islamic platform. Give it two cycles before judging it.

Guarantee agenciesWrite a term sheet for a partial guarantee on a pooled profit-sharing stake, with a share of upside.
Zakat and waqf bodiesSet aside a small first-loss pool for one pilot.
PlatformsBuild a low-cost check for rural short-cycle projects.
InvestorsRead the loss order before the return. Price the risk, not the story.
Check yourself
  • Is what I am guaranteeing equity, or a stake with an end date?
  • Who loses first, and is that written down?
  • How does the investor get out, and when?
  • Am I annualising a return that happens once a year?
  • If three of ten projects fail, who still stands?
My take

The finance veteran was right about banks. A licensed bank should not hold the equity of a kampung cattle project, and the BRF proved the market agrees. But an equity guarantee is not one thing. Social money that has already accepted the loss, and risk-takers who want upside on a pool with an end date, are both places where a partial guarantee can work. Small, pooled, short-cycle, with the guarantor taking a share of the upside. Not a national fund. A pilot, done properly, for two seasons.

Over to you

Would you put RM1,000 into a pool of ten kampung projects if part of your capital were guaranteed, and what return would make it worth it?

  • Cadangan saya: portfolio 5 hingga 10 projek kitaran pendek yang dijamin (lembu korban, satu musim padi), dengan penjamin menanggung sebahagian modal dan berkongsi keuntungan.

Bolehkah ekuiti seseorang dijamin dan masih dipanggil ekuiti? Seorang tokoh kewangan yang pernah bekerja dengan saya bertahun-tahun dahulu berkata tidak. Baginya, "jaminan ekuiti" ialah istilah yang bercanggah sendiri. Ekuiti ialah wang yang menanggung rugi dahulu dan dibayar paling akhir. Jika ia dijamin, hilanglah sifat yang menjadikannya ekuiti.

Sebahagian besar katanya betul. Malaysia telah menguji idea ini pada skala nasional, dan pasaran sudah memberikan jawapannya. Namun "sebahagian besar" masih meninggalkan ruang, dan ruang itulah tempat perniagaan kecil serta luar bandar paling memerlukan bantuan.

Catatan ini meneliti apa yang berlaku kepada Kemudahan Permodalan Semula Perniagaan (BRF) Bank Negara, mengapa bank menjauhkan diri, dan di mana jaminan ke atas modal berisiko masih boleh berfungsi.

01 · Apa yang ditawarkan BRF

BRF diumumkan dalam Belanjawan 2022 dan dibuka pada 3 Februari 2022 dengan peruntukan RM1 bilion di bawah Dana BNM untuk PKS. Tujuannya munasabah: membantu PMKS berkembang tanpa menambah timbunan hutang.

PMKS boleh memperoleh ekuiti melalui saham keutamaan, saham biasa atau instrumen seakan-ekuiti. Atau mereka boleh mengambil pembiayaan gabungan, iaitu hutang bank serta ekuiti daripada pembiaya ekuiti pihak ketiga.

Syaratnya longgar. Sehingga RM5 juta bagi setiap PMKS, tempoh sehingga 10 tahun dan tanpa cagaran. Bahagian hutang dihadkan pada 3.5% setahun tanpa jaminan dan 5% dengan jaminan. Tiada had ke atas pulangan ekuiti. Perlindungan jaminan bagi bahagian hutang juga disediakan.

02 · Apa kata angka

Apa kata data

Jadual kemudahan PKS BNM bertarikh 21 Oktober 2023, kira-kira 20 bulan selepas dilancarkan, menyenaraikan BRF dengan RM1,000 juta tersedia, sama dengan jumlah semasa ia dibuka.

Slaid laporan tahunan BNM 2023 (Mac 2024) hanya menunjukkan tanda sengkang pada baris BRF bagi kelulusan dan penggunaan. Saya tidak menemui angka awam yang lebih terkini.

Orang dalam industri menyifatkan sambutannya hampir sifar, dan jadual BNM sendiri selari dengan gambaran itu. BNM juga menyatakan pada Jun 2024 bahawa ia sedang merombak cara dana PKS direka, selepas hanya satu pertiga daripada satu lagi kemudahan digunakan.

Dana yang tidak diguna oleh sesiapa bukan masalah kekurangan dana. Ia masalah reka bentuk.

03 · Mengapa bank menjauhkan diri

Bukan bidang mereka

Di bawah Akta Perkhidmatan Kewangan 2013, bank berlesen tidak boleh menjalankan perniagaan di luar perniagaan yang dibenarkan melainkan dengan kebenaran BNM. Memegang kepentingan material dalam sesebuah perbadanan memerlukan kelulusan BNM di bawah seksyen 85. Saya tidak menemui peruntukan yang melarang terus bank memegang ekuiti PMKS. Undang-undang hanya meletakkan pintu pagar di hadapannya, dan tidak ramai bank yang sanggup beratur di pagar itu demi pegangan RM300,000.

Ia menelan modal

Di bawah peraturan BNM sekarang, ekuiti tidak tersenarai dan modal teroka membawa wajaran risiko 150%. Pegangan dalam anak syarikat bukan kewangan membawa 1250%. Piawaian global Basel III lebih ketat: 250% bagi ekuiti dan 400% bagi ekuiti tidak tersenarai yang bersifat spekulatif.

Paling akhir dibayar, tiada jalan keluar

Ekuiti dibayar selepas semua pemiutang. Saham syarikat persendirian kecil tiada pasaran, jadi untuk keluar mesti ada pembeli lain yang sanggup. Model kredit bank menilai risiko gagal bayar dan pemulihan. Tiada ruang dalam model itu untuk "mungkin tujuh tahun lagi, jika ada yang mahu beli".

Pelabur juga berfikiran sama. Di platform berdaftar SC pada 2025, pembiayaan P2P mengumpul kira-kira dua puluh kali ganda berbanding pendanaan ramai ekuiti.

04 · Jadi, memang bercanggah?

Bagi bank, ya. Bagi penjamin, bergantung pada apa yang dijamin.

Jika pulangan ekuiti dijamin sepenuhnya, penjamin menanggung kerugian manakala pelabur menikmati keuntungan. Itu rugi setiap kitaran. Tetapi jika yang dijamin hanya sebahagian pegangan yang mempunyai tempoh tetap, pulangan asas dan bahagian keuntungan, kiraannya berubah. Jerman sudah melakukannya berdekad-dekad.

05 · Pintu pertama: kewangan sosial

Di sini percanggahan itu hilang, kerana pemberi dana sudah menerima kerugian. Wang zakat, wakaf dan CSR diberikan untuk membina mata pencarian, bukan untuk mendapat pulangan.

iTEKAD oleh BNM menggabungkan dana sebegini dengan modal benih, latihan dan pembiayaan mikro sejak Mei 2020. Program ini telah membantu 14,387 usahawan mikro setakat akhir Mac 2026. Sehingga akhir 2025, RM94.2 juta dana sosial dan RM77.2 juta pembiayaan serta pelaburan telah disalurkan.

Jaminan boleh memanjangkan hayat setiap ringgit dana sosial. Dana sosial menanggung kerugian pertama, penjamin menanggung lapisan berikutnya, dan bank atau platform membiayai bakinya. Ringgit zakat tidak lagi habis sekali guna, sebaliknya menjadi penampan yang bekerja berulang kali.

06 · Pintu kedua: orang ramai yang berani berisiko

Ada pelabur yang tidak kisah rugi sedikit asalkan berpeluang untung lebih. Saya tidak memanggil mereka kaki judi. Mereka memang berjiwa pengambil risiko, dan platform sudah pun sampai kepada mereka: pelabur institusi hanya 2% daripada pelabur ECF dan P2P pada 2024, tetapi menyumbang 60% nilai pelaburan.
