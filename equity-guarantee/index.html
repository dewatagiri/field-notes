Equity guarantee: an oxymoron, or a tool we have not shaped yet?

Bank Negara's RM1 billion recapitalisation facility found almost no takers. The reasons are clear. So are the two narrow places where a guarantee on risk capital can still work.

By Azman Taufik · 27 November 2026 · 10 min readComment on LinkedIn

The short version
  • BNM's RM1 billion BRF offered SMEs equity or debt-plus-equity from February 2022. In October 2023 BNM still listed the full RM1,000 million as available, and its 2023 annual report slides showed no approvals.
  • Banks stayed away for three reasons: equity sits outside their normal business and needs BNM approval, it eats capital (150% risk weight today, 1250% if the firm becomes a subsidiary), and the equity holder is paid last with no clear exit.
  • Guaranteeing pure equity is a losing trade for the guarantor. Guaranteeing a term-limited, profit-sharing stake is not: German guarantee banks cover 25–80% of such stakes.
  • Two doors are open in Malaysia: social finance, where the funder already accepts the loss (iTEKAD has reached 14,387 microentrepreneurs), and the crowd on SC-registered platforms, where MyCIF already invests on a first-loss basis.
  • My proposal: a guaranteed portfolio of 5 to 10 short-cycle projects (korban cattle, a padi season), with the guarantor covering part of the capital and sharing the upside.

Can you guarantee someone's equity and still call it equity? A finance veteran I worked with years ago said no. To him, "equity guarantee" was an oxymoron. Equity is the money that takes the loss first and gets paid last. Guarantee it, and you remove the very thing that makes it equity.
