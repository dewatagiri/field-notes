Kajian strategik · 2026–2076

Ekonomi Perang Kekal

Kuasa, keuntungan, legitimasi yang direka, dan masa depan kedaulatan. Soalan sebenar bukan siapa yang untung. Soalan sebenar: bagaimana negara yang lemah mahu mengejar?

Azman Taufik · 16 November 2026 · Bacaan 11 minit
Ringkasan pantas
  • Perbelanjaan ketenteraan dunia pada 2025: US$2.887 trilion. Naik 11 tahun berturut-turut.
  • Kontrak Pentagon 2020–24: US$2.4 trilion. Lima kontraktor terbesar ambil US$771 bilion, kira-kira 32%.
  • Perang pasca-9/11: sekurang-kurangnya 4.5 juta mati dalam zon perang, dan 38 juta dipindahkan (Brown University).
  • Pentagon gagal audit tahun kelapan berturut-turut. Ini tidak bermakna wang hilang. Ini bermakna pengaudit tidak cukup bukti.
  • Hipotesis utama: jurang kuasa makin bergabung dan membesar. Negara lemah tidak boleh tunggu masa membantu mereka.

Mulakan dengan fizik, bukan dengan marah

Kebanyakan perbahasan tentang perang tersangkut pada satu soalan: siapa jahat?

Itu soalan yang salah. Ia tidak boleh diuji. Ia hanya melahirkan pertengkaran.

Mari kita pecahkan kepada asas. Ada wang. Ada insentif. Ada keputusan. Ada naratif yang menjustifikasikan keputusan itu. Dan ada manusia yang menanggung akibatnya. Kalau kita faham kelima-limanya, kita faham sistemnya.

Tesis saya mudah. Perang bukan sekadar kegagalan diplomasi. Ia sudah menjadi sistem ekonomi-politik yang berkekalan. Institusi berkuasa boleh mengaut untung daripada konflik, mempengaruhi dasar yang menyokongnya, dan membentuk cerita awam yang mengesahkannya.

Tetapi saya tidak akan mendakwa setiap perang ada satu punca. Saya juga tidak akan mendakwa pihak yang untung itulah yang memulakan konflik. Itu tidak boleh dibuktikan. Kajian yang baik membezakan apa yang terbukti, apa yang dipertikaikan dan apa yang sekadar ramalan.

01Lima tesis yang perlu diuji

Tesis 1

Ekonomi perang kekal

Belanjawan, perolehan, penumpuan korporat dan pengaruh politik saling menguatkan. Ia boleh bertahan lebih lama daripada konflik yang mulanya menjustifikasikannya.
Tesis 2

Legitimasi yang direka

Kerajaan, media dan industri strategik bersaing menentukan perang mana yang perlu, mangsa mana yang penting dan campur tangan mana yang halal.
Tesis 3

Jurang kuasa bergabung

Keunggulan tentera kini bergantung pada semikonduktor, AI, kewangan, tenaga, satelit dan kapasiti industri. Kelebihan ini boleh membesar lebih laju daripada kemampuan negara lemah mengejar.
Tesis 4

Paradoks kedaulatan

Negara yang mahu merdeka perlu bina kuasa ekonomi dan pertahanan. Kuasa yang sama boleh melahirkan perampasan oleh elit, autoritarianisme dan kepentingan tentera-industri sendiri.
Tesis 5

Dilema seratus tahun

Kesabaran strategik perlu. Menunggu secara pasif berbahaya. Soalannya: boleh institusi, kerjasama serantau dan penyebaran teknologi mengatasi kelebihan kuasa sedia ada?

Gaza: yang paling baru

Kementerian Kesihatan Gaza melaporkan 74,237 kematian pada 4 Oktober 2026. Itu kira-kira 3.4%, atau seorang daripada setiap 30 penduduk sebelum perang.

UNICEF melaporkan sehingga 31 Mac 2026 lebih 73,300 rakyat Palestin dilaporkan terbunuh, termasuk sekurang-kurangnya 21,500 kanak-kanak.

Gencatan senjata diumumkan pada 10 Oktober 2025. Ia tidak menghentikan kematian. Menurut OCHA, sehingga awal September 2026, 1,334 orang dilaporkan terbunuh selepas gencatan senjata. UNICEF pula berkata sekurang-kurangnya 300 kanak-kanak terbunuh dalam 300 hari pertama gencatan itu, kira-kira seorang sehari.

PBB menganggarkan kira-kira 1.7 juta orang, lebih kurang 80% penduduk, kekal berpindah. Sekitar 76.6% unit rumah musnah atau rosak.

Apa yang angka ini tidak katakan
  • Ia tidak membuktikan industri senjata mencetuskan perang. Itu soalan 4 di bahagian 06, dan ia belum terjawab.
  • Brown sendiri menyatakan ia tidak menyalahkan satu pihak, sebab atau tempoh tertentu. Banyak pihak bertempur.
  • Data Brown mengikut zon perang, bukan agama mangsa. Kebanyakan negara itu majoriti Muslim. Jadi saya tulis "dalam negara majoriti Muslim", bukan "Muslim disasarkan".
  • Angka Gaza ialah angka Kementerian Kesihatan, yang beroperasi di bawah pihak berkuasa de facto. PBB menggunakannya sebagai angka yang dilaporkan. Perang itu bermula selepas serangan 7 Oktober 2023 ke atas Israel.

Maka apa kaitannya dengan tesis kita? Satu perkara. Sistem yang dibincang dalam bahagian 05 bukan sistem abstrak. Apabila ia gagal dikawal, pihak yang membayar ialah orang awam di zon perang, yang tidak ada suara dalam lobi, belanjawan atau kontrak.

Soalan keadilanSiapa yang menanggung kos, dan siapa yang membuat keputusan? Adakah mereka orang yang sama?

04Kebencian yang terorganisasi

Ada yang berkata artikel ini menapis. Jadi inilah buktinya, tanpa dilembutkan. Saya asingkan kepada tiga tahap: apa yang terbukti, apa yang dituduh dan apa yang masih dipertikaikan.

Terbukti

Kebencian anti-Muslim yang terorganisasi

CAIR merekodkan 8,683 aduan diskriminasi anti-Muslim dan anti-Arab di AS pada 2025, tertinggi sejak 1996. Daripadanya, 555 ialah insiden kebencian langsung dan 33 menyasarkan institusi Islam. Insiden terhadap masjid pada Mei–Julai 2026 lebih tinggi 183% berbanding tempoh sama 2025. CAIR ialah kumpulan advokasi dan angkanya ialah aduan, bukan kes yang disahkan mahkamah.
Terbukti

Mesin undang-undang

Antara 2010 dan 2017, 201 RUU anti-Syariah dibentangkan di 43 negeri AS (SPLC, Haas Institute). CAIR berkata RUU 2025 memakai istilah yang dicipta ekstremis anti-Muslim pada pertengahan 2000-an.
Terbukti

Keganasan dan simbol

Penyerang Christchurch (2019) dan Breivik (2011, 77 mati) menulis manifesto yang merujuk Perang Salib. Mereka penjenayah tunggal, bukan dasar negara. Pada 2001, Bush menyebut perang melawan keganasan sebagai "crusade", dan Rumah Putih menarik balik perkataan itu dua hari kemudian.
Dituduh

Genosid di Gaza

Suruhanjaya Siasatan bebas PBB (Navi Pillay) memutuskan pada 16 September 2025 bahawa genosid berlaku di Gaza dan Israel bertanggungjawab. Ia bukan badan undang-undang dan tidak bercakap bagi pihak PBB. Israel menolaknya sebagai "distorted and false".
Dituduh

Jenayah perang

ICC mengeluarkan waran tangkap untuk Netanyahu dan Gallant atas jenayah perang dan jenayah terhadap kemanusiaan, termasuk kebuluran sebagai kaedah perang. Itu bukan pertuduhan genosid.
Dipertikaikan

Niat

Genosid dalam undang-undang memerlukan niat memusnahkan sesuatu kumpulan. Di ICJ, kes Afrika Selatan lawan Israel belum diputuskan. Mahkamah memerintahkan langkah kecemasan yang mengikat, tetapi tiada keputusan akhir. Israel membantah bidang kuasa. Afrika Selatan dijangka membalas hanya menjelang 22 November 2027.
Empat perkara berbeza Bukti di atas menunjuk kepada empat perkara berbeza: kebencian yang terorganisasi, penjenayah ekstremis, perang yang menelan orang awam, dan tuduhan niat di Gaza yang belum diputuskan mahkamah.

05Bagaimana sistem ini berjalan

Tiada pusat arahan rahsia. Yang ada ialah gelung insentif. Setiap langkah munasabah bagi pihak yang berada di situ. Hasil akhirnya, sistem terus membesar.

Siapa sebenarnya pemilik? Lima kontraktor teratas ialah syarikat awam: Lockheed Martin ($313B), RTX ($145B), General Dynamics ($116B), Boeing ($115B) dan Northrop Grumman ($81B), 2020–24. Pemilik mereka boleh dinamakan.

Contoh Lockheed Martin (fail SEC 13F, Mac 2026): State Street kira-kira 14.1%, BlackRock 7.7%, Vanguard sekitar 6.1% pada satu entiti dan lebih lagi pada entiti lain. Institusi memegang kira-kira 75% saham. Pemegang saham teratas bagi lima syarikat itu secara umum juga dana besar seperti Vanguard, BlackRock dan State Street, tetapi saya belum semak angka bagi empat lagi satu demi satu.

Satu perkara perlu jujur: dana ini mengurus wang untuk ramai pelabur, termasuk dana pencen dan penabung biasa. Mereka pemilik atas kertas, bukan satu kumpulan yang berpakat.

Dua angka yang benar serentak. Pemegang saham dan pengurusan menerima untung dan gaji dalam laporan tahunan. Di zon perang, orang awam menanggung kos (bahagian 03). Menghubungkan keduanya sebagai sebab dan akibat ialah soalan 4 di bahagian 06, dan ia belum terjawab. Tetapi jurang antara yang menerima dan yang membayar itu sendiri sudah cukup untuk dipersoalkan.

Model ini lebih kukuh daripada teori konspirasi. Kelebihannya: ia boleh diuji.

Seberapa kuat? Dan di mana hadnya

Pembaca patut yakin sistem ini kuat dan mengakar. Tetapi bukti juga menunjukkan ia bukan tidak boleh dihadkan. Dua-dua perlu ditunjukkan.

Di mana ia kuat

Sukar dipotong, sukar dibunuh

Pengaruh bukan sahaja di Washington. V-22 Osprey pernah cuba dibatalkan oleh dua setiausaha pertahanan, Harold Brown dan Dick Cheney, tetapi Kongres membatalkan keputusan mereka. Apabila Rumsfeld mahu membunuh Crusader pada 2002, pejabat Tentera Darat didakwa menyediakan "talking points" untuk melobi Kongres, dan sebahagian ahli Kongres yang negerinya menerima manfaat berikrar melawan.
Di mana ia terhad

Selepas Perang Dingin

Menurut Mercatus, perbelanjaan pertahanan AS jatuh daripada 5.2% KDNK pada 1990 kepada 3.0% pada 2000, dengan konsensus dua parti. CFR mengira 3.7% pada 2000 dengan takrifan berbeza. Arahnya sama: ia turun banyak apabila ancaman dilihat hilang.
Di mana ia terhad

Program yang dibunuh

Rumsfeld membatalkan Crusader (program US$11 bilion) pada 2002. Comanche dibatalkan pada 2004 selepas 22 tahun dan kira-kira US$6.9 bilion, tanpa sebuah helikopter pun. Gates membunuh FCS pada 2009 selepas kira-kira US$19–21 bilion.
Apa maknanya Kuasa ini mengakar, bukan mutlak. Ia kuat melawan pemotongan kecil dan pembatalan satu-satu. Ia kalah pada skala besar apabila ancaman dilihat sudah berlalu. Tetapi perbelanjaan naik semula selepas 2001. Maknanya pengungkit sebenar ialah persepsi ancaman. Siapa yang membentuk persepsi itu memegang kuncinya. Itu tesis 2, dan inilah sebabnya legitimasi yang direka begitu penting.

Bagi negara yang lemah, ini berita yang tidak mudah tetapi bukan putus asa. Anda tidak boleh menumbangkan sistem ini dengan konfrontasi. Anda boleh mengurangkan kebergantungan anda padanya.

06Ujian paling sukar untuk tesis ini

Saya mesti hadapi bantahan terkuat dahulu.

Perang sering lahir daripada dilema keselamatan yang sebenar, cita-cita wilayah, pertembungan ideologi dan salah kira. Bukan semata-mata wang.

Perang di Ukraine menunjukkan perkara yang sama. Penjelasannya dipertikaikan: ada yang menekankan pencerobohan Rusia dan ancamannya kepada negara jiran, ada yang menekankan perluasan NATO ke arah sempadan Rusia dan perjanjian lisan yang dikatakan dilanggar. Apa pun pandangan anda, pengeluar senjata untung daripada permintaan yang naik. Itu tidak membuktikan mereka yang mencetuskan perang.

Pengeluaran senjata juga boleh jadi pencegahan yang sah. Memotong belanja tanpa menyelesaikan ancaman boleh menaikkan risiko perang, bukan menurunkannya.

07Dari mana kuasa datang pada 2036, 2046 dan 2076

Ini senario bersyarat. Bukan ramalan dengan kebarangkalian statistik.

Tiga horizon, satu pergeseran: dari senjata ke infrastruktur

2036: dekad pemiliteran teknologi

Industri tentera berubah daripada pengeluar senjata dan platform kepada ekosistem teknologi tentera. Perisikan dibantu AI, dron autonomi, keselamatan siber, rangkaian satelit dan infrastruktur data medan perang menjadi sumber leveraj strategik.

Quincy sudah menyenaraikan Palantir, Anduril dan SpaceX sebagai pemain industri pertahanan yang sedang muncul, bersama kontraktor tradisional.

ImplikasiKuasa tentera makin bergantung pada perisian, data, kapasiti pembuatan dan akses kepada komponen canggih. Bukan lagi bilangan pesawat atau kapal perang.

Pantau: bahagian perolehan untuk firma teknologi tentera, tadbir urus AI, penggunaan senjata autonomi, dan kebergantungan negara kecil pada infrastruktur digital asing.

2046: era sistem kuasa yang bersaing

Pengaruh global jadi lebih berselerak. Tetapi negara kecil mungkin menghadapi tekanan lebih besar untuk memilih blok keselamatan, kewangan dan teknologi.

Multipolar tidak semestinya aman. Kuasa yang bersaing boleh bina industri pertahanan, kemampuan pengawasan, mekanisme sekatan dan naratif politik masing-masing.

Negara tanpa autonomi teknologi boleh tukar kebergantungan satu kuasa kepada kuasa lain.

Pantau: akses semikonduktor, pemprosesan mineral kritikal, kepelbagaian mata wang rizab, penyelesaian dagangan serantau, dan keupayaan ASEAN menjaga pilihan diplomatik yang bebas.

2076: kedaulatan atau kebergantungan kekal

Pembahagian penentu mungkin bukan lagi Barat lawan bukan-Barat. Ia mungkin antara masyarakat yang mengawal infrastruktur teknologi dan ekonomi asas mereka, dengan yang kekal bergantung secara struktur.

AI canggih, pengeluaran automatik, sistem tenaga dan rangkaian maklumat boleh mendemokrasikan keupayaan pengeluaran. Atau menumpukan kuasa yang belum pernah ada kepada segelintir kerajaan dan korporat.

Pantau: penumpuan kapasiti pengkomputeran, inovasi domestik, akauntabiliti institusi, akses teknologi dan keupayaan negara membuat keputusan sendiri.

08Tiga masa depan yang bersaing

Tesis ini tidak boleh anggap konflik berterusan itu pasti. Kita perlukan senario bersaing sebagai alat uji.

Senario A

Pemiliteran berakar

Persepsi ancaman, belanja pertahanan dan pengaruh komersial saling menguatkan. Teknologi tentera baharu memperluas sistem, bukan menggantikannya.
Senario B

Multipolariti bersaing

Beberapa pusat kuasa bersaing dengan sistem pertahanan, kewangan dan teknologi sendiri.
Senario C

Pemiliteran terkawal

Belanja dan pengaruh dihadkan oleh institusi, perjanjian dan akauntabiliti.
Penunjuk awal untuk Senario ABelanja terus naik walaupun ada pembukaan diplomatik. Penumpuan perolehan meningkat. Pengawasan lemah.

Senario ini tidak saling menafikan. Rantau berbeza boleh mengalami hasil berbeza pada masa yang sama.

09Apa maknanya untuk Malaysia dan ASEAN

Malaysia tidak boleh menandingi kuasa besar dari segi jumlah kuasa tentera. Itu fakta.

Tetapi autonomi strategik tidak bermaksud menyaingi senjata demi senjata. Ia bermaksud mengurangkan bilangan kelemahan kritikal yang boleh dieksploitasi pihak luar.

Soalan teras untuk ASEANBolehkah ASEAN membina kuasa tawar-menawar kolektif tanpa menjadi blok tentera dan tanpa mengorbankan autonomi setiap ahli?

Jepun, China, Korea Selatan dan Singapura menunjukkan kemampuan industri dan pembangunan institusi boleh mengubah kuasa tawar-menawar sesebuah negara. Tetapi geografi, jaminan keselamatan luar, sistem politik dan sejarah mereka berbeza. Kejayaan mereka tidak boleh disalin bulat-bulat.

Paradoks kedaulatanKuasa yang kita bina untuk merdeka boleh jadi sumber masalah baharu: elit yang merampas, autoritarianisme dan kepentingan tentera-industri tempatan. Reka institusi dahulu. Bina keupayaan kemudian.

Kuasa tanpa senjata: berkecuali dan bersuara untuk keamanan

Malaysia tidak punya leveraj ketenteraan. Terima hakikat itu. Soalan yang berguna ialah: apa yang kita ada?

Aset 1

Kredibiliti berkecuali

Kita bukan sebahagian blok tentera mana-mana kuasa besar. Deklarasi ZOPFAN diisytiharkan di Kuala Lumpur pada 1971, dengan hasrat menjadikan Asia Tenggara bebas daripada campur tangan kuasa luar. Tradisi itu masih ada.
Aset 2

Boleh bercakap dengan semua pihak

Negara yang tidak mengancam sesiapa lebih mudah dipercayai sebagai pengantara dan penganjur dialog. Itu aset yang tidak boleh dibeli dengan belanjawan pertahanan.
Aset 3

Suara moral yang konsisten

Angka di bahagian 03 ialah orang sebenar. Negara kecil yang menyebut kos manusia dengan jelas, di mana-mana ia berlaku, membawa autoriti yang tidak bergantung pada saiz tentera.
Syarat dan kos
  • Berkecuali bukan diam. Ia aktif: bercakap, menjadi tuan rumah, membawa isu ke forum antarabangsa.
  • Suara keamanan hanya dipercayai jika piawainya sama untuk semua mangsa dan semua pihak. Piawai berganda menghapuskan kredibiliti dengan cepat.
  • Berkecuali ada harga. Tekanan untuk memilih blok akan datang (bahagian 07). Sebab itu ia mesti disokong ekonomi yang tidak mudah ditekan: semikonduktor, mineral, digital dan kewangan.
Apa yang Malaysia boleh buat
  1. Pertahankan berkecuali yang aktif dalam ASEAN, bukan berkecuali yang pasif.
  2. Bawa keamanan dan undang-undang antarabangsa ke setiap forum yang kita ada.
  3. Sebut kos manusia dengan angka dan sumber, supaya suara itu sukar dipertikai.
  4. Kurangkan kebergantungan kritikal, supaya berkecuali itu pilihan dan bukan sekadar harapan.

Seruan Untuk penggubal dasar Malaysia dan ASEAN: tiga langkah, bermula sekarang.
  • Sebut. Jadilah suara berkecuali yang konsisten untuk keamanan, dengan piawai yang sama untuk setiap mangsa, siapa pun pelakunya. Malaysia tiada kuasa senjata, tetapi suara yang konsisten itulah modal kita.
  • Tunjuk. Bawa ketelusan ke ASEAN: dedahkan kontrak perolehan pertahanan dan perbelanjaan senjata secara terbuka. Sistem ini subur dalam kekaburan, dan cahaya ialah ubat pertama.
  • Lindung. Petakan dan kurangkan kebergantungan kritikal, iaitu semikonduktor, mineral, digital dan kewangan. Kita tidak boleh urus apa yang belum kita ukur.
Pandangan saya Tragedi ekonomi perang kekal bukan semata-mata konflik menjana untung. Tragedinya, institusi yang ditugaskan melindungi masyarakat boleh menjadi bergantung, dari segi kewangan, politik dan organisasi, pada persepsi bahaya yang berterusan. Dan yang membayar ialah orang yang tidak pernah ada di meja keputusan.

Namun kebergantungan ini tidak universal dan tidak kekal. Ancaman boleh nyata. Kesiapsiagaan boleh perlu. Kerajaan masih mampu menukar keutamaan.

Bagi negara lemah, cabarannya berat. Ia tidak boleh meruntuhkan tatanan antarabangsa melalui konfrontasi. Ia juga tidak boleh menganggap masa akan menjadikannya lebih kuat. Strategi paling boleh dipercayai: kumpul keupayaan ekonomi, teknologi, institusi dan diplomatik dengan sabar.

Bagi Malaysia, jawapannya bukan senjata. Ia berkecuali yang aktif dan suara yang konsisten untuk keamanan, disokong oleh ekonomi yang tidak mudah ditekan.

Sepuluh tahun akan datang mungkin menentukan siapa kawal teknologi keselamatan. Dua puluh tahun berikutnya menentukan negara mana yang masih ada pilihan strategik. Dalam lima puluh tahun, soalannya: adakah teknologi menyebar kuasa lebih luas, atau menumpukannya dalam institusi yang makin tidak bertanggungjawab?

Matlamatnya bukan membina empayar saingan. Matlamatnya dunia di mana kedaulatan tidak bergantung pada tunduk kepada satu empayar.
Untuk anda: jika yang kuat boleh gunakan kekayaan dan teknologi hari ini untuk mengunci dominasi esok, bagaimana negara lemah boleh mengejar? Dan apa jadi jika tidak boleh?
Strategic study · 2026–2076

The Permanent War Economy

Power, profit, manufactured legitimacy, and the future of sovereignty. The real question is not who profits. It is: how can weaker nations ever catch up?

Azman Taufik · 16 November 2026 · 11 min read
The short version
  • Global military spending in 2025: US$2.887 trillion. Up 11 years in a row.
  • Pentagon contracts 2020–24: US$2.4 trillion. The top five contractors took US$771 billion, about 32%.
  • Post-9/11 wars: at least 4.5 million dead in the war zones and 38 million displaced (Brown University).
  • The Pentagon failed its audit for the eighth year running. That does not mean money vanished. It means auditors lacked enough evidence.
  • Core hypothesis: the power gap is compounding. Weaker states cannot wait for time to help them.

Start with physics, not anger

Most debate about war gets stuck on one question: who is evil?

Wrong question. You cannot test it. It only produces arguments.

Break it down to first principles. There is money. There are incentives. There are decisions. There is a story that justifies the decisions. And there are people who pay the price. Understand all five and you understand the system.

My thesis is simple. War is not merely a failure of diplomacy. It has become an enduring political-economic system. Powerful institutions can profit from conflict, influence the policies that sustain it, and shape the public narratives that legitimise it.

But I will not claim every war has one cause. I will not claim that those who profit started the conflict. That cannot be proven. Good research separates what is documented, what is contested and what is only a forecast.

01Five theses to test

Thesis 1

The permanent war economy

Spending, procurement, corporate concentration and political influence reinforce each other. They can outlast the conflicts that first justified them.
Thesis 2

Manufactured legitimacy

Governments, media and strategic industries compete to define which wars are necessary, which victims matter and which interventions are acceptable.
Thesis 3

The compounding power gap

Military superiority now rests on semiconductors, AI, finance, energy, satellites and industrial capacity. These advantages may compound faster than weaker states can catch up.
Thesis 4

The sovereignty paradox

States that want independence must build economic and defensive power. The same power can produce elite capture, authoritarianism and their own military-industrial interests.
Thesis 5

The hundred-year dilemma

Strategic patience is necessary. Passive waiting is dangerous. Can institutions, regional cooperation and technology diffusion overcome the incumbents' advantage?

What the data says These numbers do not prove waste. Modern armed forces genuinely need equipment, logistics, research and maintenance.
They prove one thing: large, concentrated commercial interests are embedded in national-security decisions. Quincy also documents industry lobbying, think-tank funding and advisory-commission ties. Those are identifiable channels of influence.
Watch-out: the Pentagon audit The Pentagon failed its agency-wide audit for the eighth consecutive year in FY2025. This does not mean trillions were stolen or disappeared. It means auditors lacked enough reliable evidence to give an opinion on the financial statements. The difference matters.

Section 02 was about money. This section is about people.

You cannot understand a war economy if you read only the profit side. There is a cost side. The people who make the decisions do not pay it.

Gaza: the most recent

Gaza's Health Ministry reported 74,237 deaths on 4 October 2026. That is about 3.4% of the pre-war population, roughly one in 30.

UNICEF reported that by 31 March 2026 more than 73,300 Palestinians had been reported killed, including at least 21,500 children.

A ceasefire was announced on 10 October 2025. It did not end the killing. OCHA reports 1,334 people killed after the ceasefire as of early September 2026. UNICEF says at least 300 children were killed in the first 300 days of the ceasefire, about one a day.

The UN estimates about 1.7 million people, roughly 80% of the population, remain displaced. About 76.6% of housing units are destroyed or damaged.

What these numbers do not say
  • They do not prove arms makers started these wars. That is question 4 in section 06, and it is not answered here.
  • Brown states it does not attribute responsibility to any single party, cause or period. Many parties fought.
  • Brown's data is by war zone, not by victims' religion. Most of these countries are Muslim-majority. So I write "in Muslim-majority countries", not "Muslims were targeted".
  • Gaza figures come from the Ministry of Health, which operates under the de facto authorities. The UN uses them as reported figures. That war began after the 7 October 2023 attacks on Israel.

So what does this have to do with our thesis? One thing. The system in section 05 is not abstract. When it fails to restrain itself, the people who pay are civilians in war zones. They have no voice in the lobbying, the budget or the contracts.

The justice questionWho bears the cost, and who makes the decision? Are they the same people?

04Organised hatred

Some will say this article filters. So here is the evidence, unsoftened. I separate it into three tiers: what is documented, what is alleged and what is still contested.

Documented

Organised anti-Muslim hatred

CAIR recorded 8,683 anti-Muslim and anti-Arab discrimination complaints in the US in 2025, the highest since 1996. Of these, 555 were direct hate incidents and 33 targeted Islamic institutions. Incidents against mosques in May–July 2026 were 183% higher than the same period in 2025. CAIR is an advocacy group and its figures are complaints, not court-verified cases.
Documented

The legislative machine

Between 2010 and 2017, 201 anti-Sharia bills were introduced in 43 US states (SPLC, Haas Institute). CAIR says 2025 bills used terminology developed by anti-Muslim extremists in the mid-2000s.
Documented

Violence and symbols

The Christchurch attacker (2019) and Breivik (2011, 77 dead) wrote manifestos that referenced the Crusades. They were lone criminals, not state policy. In 2001 Bush called the war on terror a "crusade", and the White House walked the word back two days later.
Alleged

Genocide in Gaza

The UN's independent Commission of Inquiry (chair Navi Pillay) found on 16 September 2025 that genocide is occurring in Gaza and that Israel is responsible. It is not a legal body and does not speak for the UN. Israel rejected it as "distorted and false".
Alleged

War crimes

The ICC issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu and Gallant for war crimes and crimes against humanity, including starvation as a method of war. That is not a genocide charge.
Contested

Intent

Genocide in law requires intent to destroy a group. At the ICJ, South Africa v Israel is undecided. The Court ordered binding emergency measures but made no final ruling. Israel objects to jurisdiction. South Africa's reply is not due until 22 November 2027.
Four different things The evidence above points to four different things: organised hatred, lone extremist criminals, wars that swallow civilians, and an allegation of intent in Gaza that no court has yet decided.

05How the system runs

There is no secret command centre. There is a loop of incentives. Each step makes sense to the people in it. The result: the system keeps growing.

The reinforcing loop: a hypothesis to test
1. Real or perceived security threatsWar, rivalry, terrorism, territorial disputes
↓
2. Political mobilisation and military budgetsPublic legitimacy, legislative appropriations
↓
3. Procurement, contracts and institutional expansionIndustry revenue, organisational resources, employment
↓
4. Lobbying, expertise and political influenceRevolving doors, advisory networks, research funding
↓
5. Threat perceptions and spending priorities may be reinforcedBack to step 1. To be tested empirically, not assumed in every case.
Who actually owns it? The top five contractors are public companies: Lockheed Martin ($313B), RTX ($145B), General Dynamics ($116B), Boeing ($115B) and Northrop Grumman ($81B), 2020–24. Their owners can be named.

Lockheed Martin as an example (SEC 13F filings, March 2026): State Street about 14.1%, BlackRock 7.7%, Vanguard about 6.1% in one entity and more in others. Institutions hold roughly 75% of the shares. Across the five firms the top holders also include big funds such as Vanguard, BlackRock and State Street, but I have not checked the other four one by one.

One thing to be honest about: these funds manage money for many investors, including pension funds and ordinary savers. They are owners on paper, not a group acting in concert.

Two true numbers at once. Shareholders and management receive profit and pay in the annual report. In war zones, civilians bear the cost (section 03). Linking the two as cause and effect is question 4 in section 06, and it is not answered. But the gap between who receives and who pays is reason enough to question it.

This model is more defensible than a conspiracy theory. Its strength: you can test it.

For researchersDoes industry lobbying precede policy changes?
For researchersDo procurement commitments survive the disappearance of the threats that justified them?
For researchersDoes the availability of diplomatic alternatives change spending decisions?

How strong? And where are its limits

Readers should be convinced the system is strong and deeply rooted. But the evidence also shows it can be limited. Both need to be shown.

Where it is strong

Hard to cut, hard to kill

Influence is not only in Washington. Two defence secretaries, Harold Brown and Dick Cheney, tried to cancel the V-22 Osprey, and Congress reversed them. When Rumsfeld moved to kill Crusader in 2002, the Army's legislative office was reported to have drafted "talking points" to lobby Congress, and some lawmakers whose states benefited vowed to fight.
Where it is limited

After the Cold War

Per Mercatus, US defence spending fell from 5.2% of GDP in 1990 to 3.0% in 2000, with bipartisan consensus. CFR counts 3.7% in 2000 on a different definition. The direction is the same: it fell sharply when the threat was seen to have gone.
Where it is limited

Programmes killed

Rumsfeld cancelled Crusader (an $11 billion programme) in 2002. Comanche was cancelled in 2004 after 22 years and about $6.9 billion, without a single helicopter delivered. Gates killed FCS in 2009 after roughly $19–21 billion.
What it means This power is entrenched, not absolute. It is strong against small cuts and one-off cancellations. It loses at large scale when the threat is seen to have passed. But spending rose again after 2001. So the real lever is threat perception. Whoever shapes that perception holds the key. That is thesis 2, and it is why manufactured legitimacy matters so much.

For weaker nations this is not easy news, but it is not hopeless. You cannot topple this system by confrontation. You can reduce your dependence on it.

06The hardest test for this thesis

I must face the strongest objection first.

Wars often come from real security dilemmas, territorial ambition, ideological conflict and miscalculation. Not only money.

The war in Ukraine shows the same thing. Explanations are contested: some stress Russia's invasion and the threat it poses to its neighbours, others stress NATO's expansion toward Russia's border and verbal assurances said to have been broken. Whatever your view, arms makers benefit from rising demand. That does not show they caused the war.

Arms production can also be legitimate deterrence. Cutting spending without addressing the threat may raise the risk of war, not lower it.

The trap: mixing up four questions My original paper sometimes treated these as interchangeable. They are not.
Question 1

Who benefits financially?

Measurable: contracts, margins, ownership, market concentration.
Question 2

Who influences policy?

Needs evidence of lobbying, donations, advisory roles and actual decisions.
Question 3

Who shapes public legitimacy?

Needs media, discourse, funding and public-opinion analysis.
Question 4

Who causes or prolongs conflict?

The hardest causal question. Financial incentives alone cannot answer it.

07Where power goes in 2036, 2046 and 2076

These are conditional scenarios. Not forecasts with statistical probabilities.

Three horizons, one shift: from weapons to infrastructure

2036: the technological militarisation decade

The military industry evolves from weapons and platforms into a broader military-technology ecosystem. AI-assisted intelligence, autonomous drones, cybersecurity, satellite networks and battlefield data infrastructure become sources of strategic leverage.

Quincy already lists Palantir, Anduril and SpaceX as emerging defence-industry players alongside traditional contractors.

ImplicationMilitary power depends more on software, data, manufacturing capacity and access to advanced components. Not on the number of aircraft or warships.

Monitor: the share of procurement going to military-technology firms, AI governance, autonomous-weapons deployment, and smaller states' dependence on foreign digital infrastructure.

2046: the era of competing power systems

Global influence becomes more distributed. But smaller states may face more pressure to align with competing security, financial and technology blocs.

Multipolar does not mean peaceful. Rival powers can build competing defence industries, surveillance, sanctions tools and political narratives.

States without technological autonomy may swap one dominant power for another.

Monitor: semiconductor access, critical-mineral processing, reserve-currency diversification, regional trade settlement, and ASEAN's ability to keep independent diplomatic choices.

2076: sovereignty or permanent dependency

The decisive divide may no longer be West versus non-West. It may be between societies that control their essential technological and economic infrastructure and those that stay structurally dependent.

Advanced AI, automated production, energy systems and information networks could democratise productive capability. Or concentrate unprecedented power in a few governments and corporations.

Monitor: concentration of computing capacity, domestic innovation, institutional accountability, technology access, and states' ability to decide for themselves.

08Three competing futures

The thesis cannot assume perpetual conflict is inevitable. We need competing scenarios to test the evidence against.

Scenario A

Entrenched militarisation

Threat perceptions, defence spending and commercial influence reinforce each other. New military technology expands the system rather than replacing it.
Scenario B

Competitive multipolarity

Several power centres compete with their own defence, financial and technology systems.
Scenario C

Constrained militarisation

Spending and influence are limited by institutions, treaties and accountability.
Early indicators for Scenario ASpending keeps rising despite diplomatic openings. Procurement concentration increases. Oversight is weak.

These scenarios are not mutually exclusive. Different regions can see different outcomes at the same time.

09What this means for Malaysia and ASEAN

Malaysia cannot match the major powers in aggregate military power. That is a fact.

But strategic autonomy does not mean matching weapon for weapon. It means reducing the number of critical vulnerabilities that outsiders can exploit.

Area 1Semiconductors and advanced manufacturing
Area 2Food and energy security
Area 3Digital sovereignty
Area 4Regional financial resilience
Area 5Maritime security
Area 6Institutional quality
Core question for ASEANCan ASEAN build real collective bargaining power without becoming a military bloc or sacrificing each member's autonomy?

Japan, China, South Korea and Singapore show that industrial capability and institutional development can transform a nation's bargaining power. But their geography, external security guarantees, political systems and histories differ. Their successes cannot simply be copied.

The sovereignty paradoxThe power we build to be free can become a new problem: elite capture, authoritarianism and a domestic military-industrial interest. Design the institutions first. Build the capability second.

Power without weapons: neutrality and a voice for peace

Malaysia has no military leverage. Accept that. The useful question is: what do we have?

Asset 1

Credible neutrality

We are not part of any great power's military bloc. The ZOPFAN declaration was made in Kuala Lumpur in 1971, aiming to keep Southeast Asia free of outside interference. That tradition is still there.
Asset 2

Able to talk to every side

A country that threatens no one is easier to trust as a go-between and host of dialogue. That is an asset no defence budget can buy.
Asset 3

A consistent moral voice

The numbers in section 03 are real people. A small state that names the human cost clearly, wherever it occurs, carries authority that does not depend on the size of its army.
Conditions and costs
  • Neutral does not mean silent. It is active: speaking, hosting, taking issues to international forums.
  • A voice for peace is trusted only if the standard is the same for every victim and every side. Double standards destroy credibility fast.
  • Neutrality has a price. Pressure to pick a bloc is coming (section 07). That is why it must be backed by an economy that is hard to squeeze: semiconductors, minerals, digital and finance.
What Malaysia can do
  1. Defend active neutrality inside ASEAN, not passive neutrality.
  2. Take peace and international law to every forum we sit in.
  3. State the human cost with numbers and sources, so the voice is hard to dispute.
  4. Cut critical dependencies, so neutrality is a choice and not just a hope.

10Research agenda and tests

  1. Deep case studies. The post-9/11 wars, the Iraq invasion, Libya, Ukraine, Gaza and US–China competition. Each: security justification, beneficiaries, institutional influence, humanitarian consequences and competing explanations.Measure: each case answers the four questions in section 06 separately.
  2. One consistent quantitative definition. Contract awards, not profits. Estimates, not facts.Measure: every figure has a source, a year and a definition.
  3. Counterexamples. Find cases where military spending fell after the threat disappeared.Measure: at least three counterexamples, written honestly.
  4. A transparent forecasting method. Every projection has clear assumptions and conditions.Measure: readers can check it and challenge it.
Call to action For Malaysian and ASEAN policymakers: three steps, starting now.
  • Speak. Be a consistent, neutral voice for peace, with the same standard for every victim, whoever the perpetrator. Malaysia has no military leverage, but a consistent voice is the asset we do have.
  • Show. Bring transparency to ASEAN: publish defence procurement contracts and arms spending in the open. This system thrives in opacity, and daylight is the first remedy.
  • Shield. Map and reduce critical dependencies: semiconductors, minerals, digital and financial. You cannot manage what you have not measured.
My take The tragedy of the permanent war economy is not merely that conflict generates profit. It is that the institutions tasked with protecting societies can become financially, politically and organisationally dependent on the continued perception of danger. And the people who pay were never at the decision table.

Yet this dependence is neither universal nor immutable. Threats can be genuine. Preparedness can be necessary. Governments keep the capacity to change their priorities.

For weaker nations the challenge is severe. They cannot safely dismantle the international order through confrontation. Nor can they assume time will make them stronger. Their most credible strategy is the patient accumulation of economic, technological, institutional and diplomatic capacity.

For Malaysia, the answer is not weapons. It is active neutrality and a consistent voice for peace, backed by an economy that is hard to squeeze.

The next ten years may determine who controls the technologies of security. The following twenty may determine which nations keep meaningful strategic choices. Over fifty years, the deeper question: will technology spread power more widely, or consolidate it in increasingly unaccountable institutions?

The objective is not to build a rival empire. It is to create a world in which sovereignty does not depend on submission to one.
Over to you: if the powerful can use today's wealth and technology to secure tomorrow's dominance, how can weaker nations ever catch up? And what happens if they cannot?
