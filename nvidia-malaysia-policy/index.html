FIELD NOTES

Dasar dan geopolitik

CEO Nvidia berundur. Tiga ibu negara kini memerhati cip Malaysia

Jawapan ringkas: ruang Malaysia untuk bersahabat dengan Washington, Beijing dan Taipei sekali gus semakin sempit. Washington mahukan bukti kawalan. Beijing dan Taipei mahu tahu di mana Malaysia berdiri.

Azman Taufik · 13 Nov 2026 · bacaan 9 minit

Ringkasan pantas

  • Jensen Huang meletakkan jawatan sebagai CEO Nvidia pada 12 November. Siasatan di sebaliknya tertumpu pada Megaspeed, syarikat Singapura yang anak syarikatnya di Malaysia, Speedmatrix, menyewa ruang di pusat data Johor. Ini masih dakwaan. Tiada sesiapa didakwa.
  • Malaysia bertindak pantas tetapi lewat: permit untuk cip AI buatan AS (14 Julai 2025), semakan ke atas Speedmatrix (12 November 2026) dan rang undang-undang pusat data yang sedang digubal. AS kini memegang lebih banyak kad: perjanjian dagangan terbatal dan tarif 10% dikenakan di bawah Seksyen 301.
  • Malaysia menjual lebih banyak kepada AS (14.5% eksport) berbanding China (11.7%), tetapi membeli jauh lebih banyak daripada China (24.3% import). Ini kiraan saya.
  • Taiwan menjadikannya lebih sukar. Pada Ogos, perdana menteri berkata Taiwan sebahagian daripada China. Taipei telah membekukan rundingan pelaburan. Taiwan ialah rakan dagang keempat terbesar Malaysia dan bekerjasama rapat dengan industri cip Malaysia.
  • Pandangan saya: buktikan kawalan ke atas cip, kurangkan kenyataan tentang Taiwan, dan pelihara saluran ke ketiga-tiga ibu negara.

Dahulu cip ialah cerita perdagangan. Kini ia cerita kepercayaan. Setiap ibu negara mahu tahu Malaysia di pihak siapa, dan Malaysia sudah bertahun-tahun berkata ia tidak memihak kepada sesiapa.

Itu lebih sukar dikekalkan apabila satu musim membawa siasatan AS, tarif baharu dan nota marah daripada Taipei. Tiada siapa menunjukkan Malaysia melanggar peraturan. Tetapi sebuah tapak di Malaysia berada dalam siasatan AS, dan sesebuah hab dinilai berdasarkan apa yang boleh dibuktikannya. Nota ini melihat apa yang boleh dilakukan setiap ibu negara, dan apa yang patut dibuat Malaysia dahulu.

01Mengapa Malaysia ada dalam kisah ini

Megaspeed ialah syarikat Singapura. Anak syarikatnya di Malaysia, Speedmatrix, ialah penyewa di pusat data Johor. Pegawai AS mensyaki seorang bekas eksekutif Megaspeed bekerja untuk China. Megaspeed menafikan menghantar cip ke China, dan Nvidia berkata perkhidmatan awannya dibenarkan di bawah peraturan eksport AS.

New York Times melaporkan Megaspeed membeli kira-kira AS$4.8 bilion cip Nvidia antara Jun 2024 dengan Februari 2025, dan seorang pegawai perdagangan AS menemui kotak cip yang belum dibuka di tapak Speedmatrix. Megaspeed berkata seorang pegawai AS, MITI dan Nvidia telah memeriksanya dan tidak menemui perdagangan haram.

Pada 13 November, MITI berkata ia tidak pernah meluluskan Megaspeed atau Speedmatrix. Menteri berkata Malaysia akan menyemak sama ada cip dieksport semula, dan belum ada pihak berkuasa asing yang membuat permintaan rasmi. Laporan menyebut siasatan Jabatan Perdagangan AS telah dipindahkan ke Jabatan Kehakiman.

02Apa yang boleh dilakukan Washington

Menulis peraturan yang lebih keras

Penggubal undang-undang kedua-dua parti mahu peraturan eksport cip yang lebih ketat dimasukkan ke dalam rang undang-undang pertahanan hujung tahun (NDAA). Speaker Dewan Mike Johnson berkata konsensus sedang terbentuk. Huang ialah suara industri yang paling lantang menentang had yang lebih ketat, dan suara itu kini tiada. Satu laporan menyebut Jabatan Perdagangan turut menyiapkan peraturan tentang syarikat China yang menggunakan cip di pusat data negara ketiga secara jauh. Saya hanya menemuinya dalam satu ringkasan, jadi anggaplah ia belum disahkan.

Menekan dari sudut perdagangan

Perjanjian perdagangan AS-Malaysia pada 26 Oktober 2025 menetapkan tarif 19%. Mahkamah Agung AS membatalkan tarif itu pada 20 Februari 2026, dan menteri Malaysia mengisytiharkan perjanjian itu terbatal pada Mac. Sejak Julai, tarif 10% dikenakan di bawah Seksyen 301, dan siasatan lebihan kapasiti yang berasingan boleh menambah lagi.

Menjejaskan akses syarikat

Pada 2025 seorang peguam memberitahu FMT bahawa syarikat Malaysia yang sangat bergantung pada barangan terhad boleh kehilangan lebih separuh hasil jika dimasukkan ke dalam senarai terhad AS. Itu anggaran seorang penasihat, tetapi ia menunjukkan betapa besar risikonya.

03Apa yang dipegang Beijing

China telah menjadi rakan dagang terbesar Malaysia selama 17 tahun. Perdagangan bernilai RM541.45 bilion pada 2025, kira-kira 17.7% daripada jumlah keseluruhan.

Pada April 2025, semasa lawatan negara Xi Jinping, kedua-dua negara bersetuju menubuhkan dialog bersama hal ehwal luar dan pertahanan. Tuas Beijing kebanyakannya bersifat perdagangan: akses pasaran dan pelaburan. Bahagian itu penilaian saya.

Kuala Lumpur cepat berundur apabila Washington membantah. Pada 2025 ia menarik diri daripada satu rancangan yang dikaitkan dengan cip Huawei, dan MITI berkata Malaysia tidak terlibat dalam projek itu.

AS ialah pembeli Malaysia. China ialah pembekalnya. Washington melihat lebihan dan mahukan kerjasama. Beijing melihat pelanggan bagi bahagian dan mesinnya. Dasar yang menyinggung mana-mana pihak ada harganya.

04Taiwan menjadikannya lebih sukar

Pendirian Malaysia

Malaysia mengikuti dasar Satu China sejak mengiktiraf Beijing pada 1974. Dalam kenyataan bersama dengan China pada April 2025, Malaysia berkata ia tidak akan menyokong seruan kemerdekaan Taiwan. Dalam temu bual Al Jazeera pada Ogos 2026, perdana menteri berkata beliau melihat Taiwan sebagai sebahagian daripada China. Beberapa hari kemudian beliau berkata dasar itu tidak berubah dan pendekatan aman adalah yang terbaik.

Apa yang dilakukan Taipei

Kementerian luar Taiwan berkata kenyataan itu boleh menjejaskan keyakinan pelabur Taiwan, dan dewan perniagaan Taiwan di Malaysia menyatakan kebimbangan mendalam. Taiwan kemudian menggantung rundingan pelaburan dua hala, yang disahkan kementeriannya pada 10 November. Perdana menteri berkata perdagangan dan pelaburan tidak terjejas dan pelabur tidak menarik diri.

Mengapa Taiwan penting

Taiwan ialah rakan dagang keempat terbesar Malaysia. Perdagangan kira-kira RM225 bilion pada 2025, naik 32.3%, dan RM147.8 bilion dalam separuh pertama 2026, iaitu 8.2% daripada jumlah dagangan Malaysia. Pelaburan pembuatan Taiwan bernilai RM3.7 bilion merentasi 45 projek pada 2025. Foxconn, ASE dan Chipbond telah melabur dalam elektronik dan cip di Malaysia. Eksport Malaysia ke Taiwan mencecah AS$4.2 bilion pada Oktober, berbanding AS$1.6 bilion setahun sebelumnya.

Mengapa ia merumitkan kisah cip

Ini bacaan saya. Pertama, Washington mungkin membaca pendirian Malaysia tentang Taiwan dan penelitian cip bersama-sama, lalu ragu sama ada Malaysia hab yang neutral. Penganalisis yang dipetik SCMP berkata kenyataan itu tidak mengubah dasar tetapi boleh mengukuhkan tanggapan Malaysia lebih rapat dengan Beijing. Kedua, Taiwan ialah pihak pembekal dalam perniagaan perkakasan AI, dan syarikat Taiwan melakukan banyak kerja cip bahagian belakang di Malaysia. Pembekuan menjejaskan industri yang mahu dilindungi Washington. Ketiga, apa sahaja yang dikatakan Malaysia kepada satu ibu negara dibaca oleh dua yang lain.

Pendirian undang-undang Malaysia tidak berubah. Yang berubah ialah betapa lantang ia diucapkan, dan siapa yang mendengar.

05Rekod Malaysia: langkah cip datang selepas tekanan

  1. Mac 2025

    AS meminta Malaysia memantau penghantaran Nvidia. Menteri perdagangan mengumumkan peraturan lebih ketat dan pasukan petugas.

  2. Apr 2025

    Kenyataan bersama dengan China semasa lawatan Xi Jinping: Malaysia tidak akan menyokong seruan kemerdekaan Taiwan.

  3. Jul 2025

    Tarif AS 25% diumumkan. Malaysia mengenakan permit ke atas cip AI buatan AS pada 14 Julai.

  4. Okt 2025

    Perjanjian perdagangan ditandatangani. Malaysia dinamakan rakan rantaian bekalan yang dipercayai.

  5. Feb hingga Mac 2026

    Keputusan Mahkamah Agung AS. Malaysia mengisytiharkan perjanjian terbatal.

  6. Jul 2026

    Tarif 10% di bawah Seksyen 301 berkuat kuasa.

  7. Ogos 2026

    Perdana menteri berkata Taiwan sebahagian daripada China. Taipei membantah.

  8. Okt hingga Nov 2026

    Taiwan menggantung rundingan pelaburan, disahkan pada 10 November. Dua hari kemudian Malaysia mengumumkan semakan Speedmatrix dan rang undang-undang pusat data.

Setiap langkah cip datang selepas tekanan AS. Tiada yang datang dahulu. Perselisihan dengan Taiwan bermula dengan kata-kata, bukan peraturan.

06Tiga kemungkinan perjalanan seterusnya

A. Jaring ketat

Jika ini berlaku: Kongres meluluskan semakan lokasi atau had akses jauh.

Maknanya: Import GPU menjadi lebih perlahan dan lebih mahal. Penyewa berkaitan China meninggalkan Johor. Tapak dengan penyewa AS dan Barat beroleh manfaat.

Tanda untuk diperhatikan: Teks NDAA pada Disember. Peraturan Jabatan Perdagangan tentang akses jauh (dilaporkan, belum disahkan).

B. Reda perlahan

Jika ini berlaku: Siasatan tidak menemui pelanggaran di Malaysia, dan Taipei menyambung semula rundingan.

Maknanya: Tidak banyak berubah dalam urusan cip. Tekanan perdagangan berterusan melalui Seksyen 301.

Tanda untuk diperhatikan: Penemuan MITI yang bersih tentang Speedmatrix. Rundingan dengan Taiwan disambung.

C. Ditekan dari tiga arah

Jika ini berlaku: AS menamakan tapak Malaysia, pembekuan Taiwan sampai ke pelaburan cip, dan Beijing mengharapkan lebih banyak sebagai balasan.

Maknanya: Penalti ke atas syarikat, saluran cip bahagian belakang yang lebih perlahan, dan tekanan dari semua pihak.

Tanda untuk diperhatikan: Tindakan Jabatan Kehakiman. Kelulusan projek Taiwan menurun dalam data MIDA. Permintaan baharu daripada Beijing.

Penilaian saya: bersedialah untuk A, dalam bentuk separa, dan perhatikan Taipei dengan teliti. Risiko AS dan risiko Taiwan boleh tiba serentak.

Policy and geopolitics

Nvidia’s CEO is out. Three capitals are now watching Malaysia’s chips

Short answer: Malaysia’s room to stay friends with Washington, Beijing and Taipei at once is getting smaller. Washington wants proof of control. Beijing and Taipei want to know where Malaysia stands.

Azman Taufik · 13 Nov 2026 · 9 min read

The short version

  • Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia’s CEO on 12 November. The probe behind it centres on Megaspeed, a Singapore firm whose Malaysian arm, Speedmatrix, rents space in Johor data centres. These are allegations. Nobody has been charged.
  • Malaysia has moved fast but late: a permit rule for US-origin AI chips (14 July 2025), a review of Speedmatrix (12 November 2026) and a draft data centre law. The US now holds more cards: the trade deal is void and a 10% tariff applies under Section 301.
  • Malaysia sells more to the US (14.5% of exports) than to China (11.7%), but buys far more from China (24.3% of imports). These are my calculations.
  • Taiwan makes it harder. In August the prime minister said Taiwan is part of China. Taipei has frozen investment talks. Taiwan is Malaysia’s fourth-largest trading partner and works closely with Malaysia’s chip industry.
  • My view: prove control over the chips, say little about Taiwan, and keep a line open to all three capitals.

Chips used to be a trade story. Now they are a trust story. Each capital wants to know whose side Malaysia is on, and Malaysia has said for years that it is on nobody’s side.

That is harder to keep when one season brings a US probe, a new tariff and an angry note from Taipei. Nobody has shown that Malaysia broke a rule. But a Malaysian site is inside a US probe, and a hub is judged on what it can prove. This note looks at what each capital can do, and what Malaysia should do first.

01Why Malaysia is in the story

Megaspeed is a Singapore company. Its Malaysian arm, Speedmatrix, is a tenant in Johor data centres. US officials suspect a former Megaspeed executive of working for China. Megaspeed denies shipping chips into China, and Nvidia says its cloud service is allowed under US export rules.

The New York Times reported that Megaspeed bought about US$4.8 billion of Nvidia chips between June 2024 and February 2025, and that a US trade official found unopened boxes of chips at a Speedmatrix site. Megaspeed says a US official, MITI and Nvidia inspected it and found no illegal trade.

On 13 November, MITI said it never cleared Megaspeed or Speedmatrix. The minister said Malaysia will review whether chips were re-exported, and that no foreign authority had formally asked yet. Reports say the US Commerce probe has moved to the Justice Department.

02What Washington can do

Write tougher rules

Lawmakers from both parties want tighter chip export rules in the year-end defence bill (the NDAA). House Speaker Mike Johnson says a consensus is forming. Huang was the industry’s loudest voice against tougher limits, and that voice is gone. One report says Commerce is also finishing a rule on Chinese firms using chips in third-country data centres by remote access. I found that in only one summary, so treat it as unconfirmed.

Squeeze on trade

The US-Malaysia trade deal of 26 October 2025 set the tariff at 19%. The US Supreme Court struck down the tariffs on 20 February 2026, and Malaysia’s minister declared the deal void in March. Since July, a 10% tariff applies under Section 301, and a separate excess-capacity probe could add more.

Hit company access

In 2025 a lawyer told FMT that Malaysian firms heavily dependent on restricted goods could lose over half their revenue if placed on a US restricted list. That is one adviser’s estimate, but it shows how large the risk can be.

03What Beijing holds

China has been Malaysia’s largest trading partner for 17 years. Trade was RM541.45 billion in 2025, about 17.7% of the total.

In April 2025, during Xi Jinping’s state visit, the two countries agreed to set up a joint foreign and defence dialogue. Beijing’s levers are mostly commercial: market access and investment. That part is my assessment.

Kuala Lumpur steps back quickly when Washington objects. In 2025 it retreated from a plan linked to Huawei chips, and MITI said Malaysia had no involvement in the project.

The US is Malaysia’s buyer. China is its supplier. Washington sees a surplus and wants cooperation. Beijing sees a customer for its parts and machines. A policy that annoys either side has a price.

04Taiwan makes it harder

Malaysia’s position

Malaysia has followed One China since it recognised Beijing in 1974. In the April 2025 joint statement with China, Malaysia said it will not support calls for Taiwan’s independence. In an August 2026 Al Jazeera interview, the prime minister said he sees Taiwan as part of China. Days later he said the policy has not changed and that a peaceful approach is best.

What Taipei did

Taiwan’s foreign ministry said the remarks could hurt the confidence of Taiwanese investors, and the Taiwan chamber in Malaysia voiced deep concern. Taiwan then suspended bilateral investment talks, which the ministry confirmed on 10 November. The prime minister says trade and investment are unaffected and that investors are not pulling out.

Why Taiwan matters

Taiwan is Malaysia’s fourth-largest trading partner. Trade was about RM225 billion in 2025, up 32.3%, and RM147.8 billion in the first half of 2026, or 8.2% of Malaysia’s total trade. Taiwanese manufacturing investment was RM3.7 billion across 45 projects in 2025. Foxconn, ASE and Chipbond have invested in Malaysian electronics and chips. Malaysia’s exports to Taiwan hit US$4.2 billion in October, against US$1.6 billion a year earlier.

Why it complicates the chip story

This is my reading. First, Washington may read Malaysia’s Taiwan line and its chip scrutiny together, and doubt that Malaysia is a neutral hub. Analysts quoted by SCMP say the remarks did not change the policy but could reinforce a view of closer alignment with Beijing. Second, Taiwan is the supply side of the AI hardware business, and Taiwanese firms do much of Malaysia’s chip back-end work. A freeze hurts the industry Washington wants Malaysia to protect. Third, whatever Malaysia tells one capital is read by the other two.

Malaysia’s legal position has not changed. What changed is how loudly it was said, and who was listening.

05Malaysia’s record: chip steps follow pressure

  1. Mar 2025

    The US asks Malaysia to monitor Nvidia shipments. The trade minister announces tighter rules and a task force.

  2. Apr 2025

    Joint statement with China during Xi Jinping’s visit: Malaysia will not support calls for Taiwan’s independence.

  3. Jul 2025

    A 25% US tariff is announced. Malaysia imposes the permit rule on US-origin AI chips on 14 July.

  4. Oct 2025

    The trade deal is signed. Malaysia is named a trusted supply chain partner.

  5. Feb to Mar 2026

    The US Supreme Court ruling. Malaysia declares the deal void.

  6. Jul 2026

    A 10% tariff under Section 301 takes effect.

  7. Aug 2026

    The prime minister says Taiwan is part of China. Taipei objects.

  8. Oct to Nov 2026

    Taiwan suspends investment talks, confirmed on 10 November. Two days later Malaysia announces a Speedmatrix review and a draft data centre law.

Each chip step followed US pressure. None came first. The Taiwan rift started with words, not rules.

06Three ways this could go

A. Tight net

If this happens: Congress passes location checks or remote-access limits.

What it means: GPU imports get slower and costlier. Chinese-linked tenants leave Johor. Sites with US and Western tenants gain.

Signs to watch: The NDAA text in December. A Commerce rule on remote access (reported, not confirmed).

B. Quiet fade

If this happens: The probe finds no Malaysian breach, and Taipei restarts talks.

What it means: Little changes in chips. Trade pressure continues through Section 301.

Signs to watch: A clean MITI finding on Speedmatrix. Taiwan talks resume.

C. Squeezed from three sides

If this happens: The US names Malaysian sites, Taiwan’s freeze reaches chip investment, and Beijing expects more in return.

What it means: Penalties on firms, a slower back-end chip pipeline, and pressure from every side.

Signs to watch: Justice Department action. Falling approvals of Taiwanese projects in MIDA data. New asks from Beijing.

My judgment: plan for A, in a partial form, and watch Taipei closely. The US risk and the Taiwan risk can arrive together.

07What I would do

The following are my own recommendations.

1

Track every advanced GPU from port to rack.

One national register with serial number, importer, site and end user. The draft data centre law can carry it.

Test: Can an officer find any GPU on a site visit?

2

Make operators know their tenants.

Operators should name the real owners of tenants and of remote-access customers, and be liable if they do not.

Test: Can the operator name the end user of every rack?

3

Keep Taiwan as a legal position, not a talking point.

Malaysia’s One China policy is old and stable. State it once, in the same words every time, and stop there. Do not comment on the use of force.

Test: Does every official use the same sentence?

4

Reassure the Taiwanese investors already here.

Meet the Taiwan chamber on a fixed schedule. Keep approvals for Taiwanese projects moving and publish the numbers each quarter.

Test: Are approvals of Taiwanese projects holding up?

5

Keep one open channel to each capital.

Hold regular technical talks with US export-control officials and Chinese counterparts, and keep a working line to Taipei’s trade office. Publish rules before they take effect.

Test: Is any new rule a surprise to any of the three?

6

Use compliance as credit, quietly.

Show chip-control results in the Section 301 talks. Do not announce it as a concession.

Test: Does the US trade team have the data?

7

Build local demand.

Grow Malaysian users of these GPUs, in industry, universities and government, so Johor is a user and not a transit point.

Test: What share of installed GPUs serves Malaysian customers? Publish it yearly.

Plan for delay too. Analysts warn that tighter controls could raise the cost and slow the arrival of advanced GPUs in Malaysia.

Dates to watch

  • 17 November: Nvidia’s results, the first under interim CEO Colette Kress.
  • December: the NDAA text on chip exports.
  • Whether Taiwan restarts the investment talks.
  • Any day: a US Commerce rule on remote access (reported, not confirmed).
  • MITI’s finding on Speedmatrix, and when the draft data centre law reaches Parliament.
  • The result of the US excess-capacity probe on Malaysia.

My take

Malaysia does not need to pick a side. It needs to show that it controls its own doors, and to say less about everything else. Today the chip rules are reactive, and the draft data centre law is the chance to get ahead. Done well, this episode makes Malaysia a more trusted hub. Done late, the next headline is about us.
