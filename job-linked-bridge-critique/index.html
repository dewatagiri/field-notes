The Poverty Trap Is Not a Failure of the System. It Is the System Working.

I built a loan engine for tradespeople. Then I read what its own numbers say.

By Azman Taufik · Companion to how the engine thinks · Code: github.com/dewatagiri/self_employed_bridge

1We Are Exporting the Failed American Dream to the Rest of the World

The American Dream died the moment the system stopped helping working people build equity and started hooking them on perpetual access to debt. When real wages stagnated in the U.S., the establishment didn't fix bargaining power. It "democratized credit." Credit cards, subprime loans, and Buy-Now-Pay-Later were sold as inclusion while workers turned into 1099 gig serfs: doing all the physical sweating, absorbing all the liability, and owning zero capital at the end of the week.

The entire developing world is now blindly copying that exact Wall Street rent-extraction blueprint. Everywhere you look, "financial inclusion" just means connecting the bottom 40% to the extraction pipes of the top 10% with better APIs.

2The Digital Company Store

When a bridge-loan engine forces a tradesperson to buy paint only from approved hardware stores and intercepts the customer's payment so the lender and the supplier eat first, the worker stops being an entrepreneur. He is an unpaid branch manager for the supplier and the lender. He loses the right to haggle for cash discounts, manage his own liquidity, or hold cash between jobs. All the upside goes to the ecosystem. All the physical risk stays on his back.

And here is the uncomfortable part. In my own simulation that control is the whole product. If just 5% of customers pay the painter in cash instead, the funders lose about RM210 on every RM10,000 loan. Below roughly 1% it starts to work. The business only exists because the painter is not allowed to hold his own money.

Controlling the account matters more than the price.

3Socializing Risk for Institutional Cowardice

Look at the loss waterfall. When a cohort goes bad, the first-loss reserve (a development institution's money, the same kind of pot that Zakat, Waqf or CSR could fill tomorrow) takes the hit first, so the lender's balance sheet barely gets scratched. On my assumptions, which are assumptions and not findings because no Malaysian loss data exists:

90%of expected loss lands on the reserve
7%on the guarantor
3%on the lender
37%of groups wipe out the whole reserve

Meanwhile, the rich homeowner or corporate buyer gets away without paying an upfront mobilization deposit, forcing the poor contractor to borrow against his own future labor just to subsidize the buyer's cash flow. The engine credits a deposit if there is one. It never asks for one. Charity-grade money is being used as free credit-default insurance for risk-averse lenders.

4The Debt Treadmill vs. Capital Accumulation

A loan that a painter has to take out every single month just to buy RM3,000 of paint is not a ladder out of poverty. It is a treadmill. Without a mechanism to retain surplus, he never accumulates enough cash to buy his own materials. He stays permanently leveraged, forever paying a toll to the payment rail, the supplier, and the lender just for permission to work.

5Algorithmic Exile for Being Poor

Deterministic collections code (collections.py) cannot tell the difference between a crook and a guy whose kid ended up in the ER or whose client stalled on an invoice. Paying late is not treated as a bypass, to be fair. But the first time money goes round the lender, he has to repay in full and wait for an officer's approval. The second time, he is blocked for the rest of the pilot. That skims the top 10% of compliant workers and throws the rest right back to Ah Long loan sharks.

The silver liningRewriting the Code Before the Cage Hardens

The only reason this engine isn't a total loss is that the rules are written in open-source code, not carved in stone by Wall Street lobbyists. Because the math is exposed in broad daylight, showing exactly where reserves run dry and who eats the loss, we can rewrite the state machine before the trap hardens:

  • Encode a Graduation Exit (decide.py). Force a 5% to 10% slice of every settled job into a locked savings pot owned by the worker. The moment that pot covers three jobs' worth of paint, the algorithm kicks him off debt because he now owns his own working capital.
  • Flip the Escrow onto the Buyer (screen.py). Stop underwriting the worker's desperation and start locking the buyer's cash upfront, charging automatic late penalties to corporate clients and homeowners that flow straight into the worker's pocket.
  • Build Human-Shock Grace (simulate.py). Give reliable workers automatic restructuring tokens so a family emergency buys a 60-day pause instead of permanent algorithmic exile.
None of these three is in the code yet.

The only metric that mattersGraduation

If an engine only measures lender profit and default rates, all it proves is that the bars of the cage are strong. Unless the code tracks Borrower Graduation Rate (how many small people actually accumulate enough capital to tell the bank "I don't need you anymore") we aren't breaking the poverty cycle. We are just automating it.
