Are digital platforms becoming the private landlords of the economy?

Fees, rent and dependence in Amazon, DoorDash, Grab, Booking.com and the Malaysian marketplaces. And what happens when AI agents start choosing the seller for us, 2026 to 2076.

Azman Taufik · 18 November 2026 · about 40 minutes (long read)
Comment on LinkedIn
The short version
  • The question: can a platform charge far more than its services are worth, because sellers have nowhere else to go? A high fee alone does not prove it.
  • Read the number first. Amazon's US$240.8B from seller services and advertising is not a 50% take rate. DoorDash keeps 13.6% of order value as revenue, but only 1.4% as net profit.
  • The better signal is dependence, not the commission. I propose a five-part Dependence Index. A merchant who cannot replace its platform sales within a few months is exposed, whatever the fee says.
  • Malaysia has moved. MyCC published its digital economy review on 10 Feb 2026. The Gig Workers Act took effect on 31 Mar 2026. Shopee, Lazada and TikTok Shop all changed fees in 2026.
  • The next toll may sit above the marketplace. If AI agents choose and pay for us, power moves to whoever controls recommendation, identity and payment approval. That is my central 2036 view. It is a judgement, not a forecast.

Is a platform fee a price, or a rent? The answer decides whether we need a cap, a rule on access, or nothing at all.

A restaurant pays DoorDash. A homestay pays an online travel agent. A home baker pays Shopee, then pays again for the voucher, the delivery programme and the advertisement that lifts her listing. Each of them says the total is too high. Each of them also says they cannot leave.

This paper tests that complaint. It does not assume the fee is unfair, and it does not assume it is fair. It builds on my earlier draft, corrects several claims in it, and adds new evidence from filings and regulators up to 18 November 2026.

01The question, and what would count as an answer

Main point: a fee is rent only when it exceeds what a competitive market would pay for the service, and the seller cannot walk away.

Platforms do real work. They find buyers, hold payments, check fraud, run delivery and take on risk. A merchant who would otherwise pay for the same work elsewhere is not being exploited by a fee that covers it. That is the honest starting point, and Marketplace Pulse, the research firm behind the most-cited Amazon fee estimates, makes the same point: the services would not be free elsewhere, because Google and Facebook ads and outside warehouses cost money too.

The concern starts when a platform controls the rules of access as well as the market. It can rank rivals, sell the advertising needed to be seen, set the terms for delivery and payment, and hold the data. At that point the fee is no longer only a price for service. It is also the price of staying visible.

For the experts

Working definition of platform rent (my operational use): revenue attributable to control of a scarce access bottleneck, above the competitive return needed to supply the underlying service.

Decomposition: merchant payment = efficient service cost + normal competitive return + bottleneck rent + measurement error. Each term is hard to estimate because platforms share infrastructure and cross-subsidise sides. The rent term is identified only with a counterfactual: a parity-rule change, a payment-rail change, a ranking change or new entry.

I use five tests. A platform is more likely to be earning rent when most of them are true.

  1. Dependence: the merchant cannot replace the platform's sales quickly.
  2. Layering: the platform also controls discovery, delivery, payment or credit, so leaving one layer costs the others.
  3. Opacity: the merchant cannot see how ranking or fees are set.
  4. Fee drift: total charges rise faster than the cost of the services they cover.
  5. Weak entry: rivals come and fail, or never come.

None of the five is proof alone. Together they form a pattern that regulators, courts and researchers can check.

02Read the number before you argue about it

Main point: four different numbers all get called "the take rate". They answer four different questions.

  • Headline commission: the percentage on the fee schedule.
  • Seller expense ratio: everything the seller pays the platform, as a share of sales. This includes fulfilment, advertising, storage and returns.
  • Platform net revenue margin: the platform's recognised revenue divided by order value.
  • Platform profit margin: what is left after the platform's own costs.

Mixing them produces headlines that sound sharp and mean little. Here is the first example.

Trap: adding two Amazon lines

Amazon reported US$172.2B of third-party seller services and US$68.6B of advertising for 2025. Added together that is US$240.8B, or about 34% of net sales (my calculation). It looks like proof of a huge cut from sellers. It is not.

The advertising line also covers vendors, publishers, authors and brands that are not marketplace sellers. Dividing by sellers' total sales gives a number nobody can defend. The right use of the US$240.8B is scale: it shows how large the monetisation of the marketplace has become.

A ten-minute filing check
  1. Find how revenue is defined. Is it a commission, or the full sale?
  2. Find order value (GMV). Divide revenue by it, and write down what the ratio includes.
  3. Find incentives and subsidies. Are they falling or rising as a share of order value?
  4. Find any seller or merchant count, and ask who counted it.
  5. Read the legal notes. Treat claims as claims.
  6. Check the date and whether two periods are on the same basis, for example before and after an acquisition.

03Amazon: one marketplace, several layers

Main point: Amazon shows how a platform can be efficient and exclusionary at the same time. Which one dominates is a court question, not a blog question.

Third-party sellers made up about 61 to 62% of units sold on Amazon, according to Marketplace Pulse estimates. They are not a side business. They are the shelf.

Marketplace Pulse built its fee estimate from sample seller profit-and-loss statements. It reported a typical seller paying roughly 15% in referral fees, 20 to 35% for fulfilment and storage, and up to 15% for advertising. Its 2022 average was about 50% of sales. In a March 2024 follow-up it put a typical private-label seller at 50 to 60%. The firm also reports that fees as a share of sales rise year after year, because the same services got dearer or became unavoidable.

What Amazon argues

The fees pay for real services

Fulfilment, Prime delivery and returns have costs. Amazon disputes parts of Marketplace Pulse's data and says some of its seller counts understate active sellers.

What critics argue

The fees are unavoidable

Sellers say they need Fulfilment by Amazon to win the Prime badge and need ads to be seen. Both become a toll.

Two data points complicate the story. Marketplace Pulse counted about 584,000 active sellers on Amazon.com in January 2025 and about 500,000 in March 2026, and said new registrations in 2025 were the lowest since it began tracking in 2015. Falling seller numbers could mean tougher conditions for small sellers. They could also mean tougher competition, tariffs and Chinese sellers undercutting on price. The data cannot separate the two, and Amazon disputes the count.

On the legal side, the US Federal Trade Commission and state plaintiffs allege that Amazon uses interlocking practices to keep monopoly power. These are allegations. The federal bench trial is set for 29 March 2027, after the judge refused on 9 October 2026 to move it to June. A separate California case is reported as scheduled for 19 January 2027. I treat both as open until judgment.

Lesson

The same bundle (search, Prime, delivery, ads) can lift a seller's sales and also trap her. Only an experiment can tell which. Ask: if this seller left Amazon, what share of sales would she lose, and how fast could she win it back?

04DoorDash: high merchant fees, thin platform profit

Main point: a fee can burden a merchant without being rent for the platform.

DoorDash reported Q3 2026 marketplace gross order value of US$33.9B and revenue of US$4.6B. Net revenue margin was 13.6%. GAAP gross profit was 6.8% of order value, contribution profit 5.2%, and net income 1.4%. Total orders reached 993 million.

This is why I do not call 13.6% a rent. A delivery platform pays for drivers, insurance, support, refunds and fraud. Little of the revenue reaches profit. The merchant may still be worse off. A restaurant whose customers would have ordered directly pays the fee on orders the platform did not create. A restaurant that gains new customers may come out ahead.

Watch

The test for delivery apps is merchant contribution margin per incremental order. Few researchers have that number. Ask restaurants to share their own, order by order, for a month. That is a better evidence base than any commission table.

05Hotels and online travel agents

Main point: the real question is whether a hotel can steer guests to its own website.

Online travel agents lower search costs and bring foreign guests to independent hotels. They also create dependence. The old tool for that dependence was the parity clause, which stops a hotel from offering a lower price on its own site.

The European Commission designated Booking.com a gatekeeper in May 2024. Its Digital Markets Act duties applied from 14 November 2024, and parity is banned. On 28 September 2026 the Commission published a factsheet saying businesses in the European Economic Area can now set prices and conditions freely on and off Booking.com, including on their direct channels, after further changes by Booking following regulatory dialogue. Hotel groups argue that paid placement still shapes visibility, which is the point of Section 07.

Malaysia's MyCC named online travel agents as one of its review areas and said enforcement action against them needs close work between agencies. I return to this in Section 10.

Lesson

Whether the commission is 15% or 25% matters less than whether a hotel can divert demand. Parity bans give the hotel an exit. They do not give it traffic.

06Why platforms tip, and why they sometimes do not

Main point: scale, network effects and conduct are three different things. Only the third is a reason to act.

Rochet and Tirole showed that platforms price each side of the market differently. One side may pay little or nothing, because it brings the other side in. So free consumer apps with paid merchant fees are not suspicious on their own. Parker and Van Alstyne added that network effects can make one side's gain depend on the other's, which is why growth feeds on itself.

That only produces one winner when switching is hard and users stay on one platform. Where people use several (multi-homing), or the product differs by city, tipping is weak. Food delivery in a big city can hold several apps. A single search destination for one product category may not.

Venture money adds a twist. A platform can run at a loss to build a network, then reprice. Sometimes that is a fair cost of building an efficient service. Sometimes it is pricing below cost to push out rivals who are just as efficient. To prove the second, a researcher needs evidence of below-cost pricing, likely recoupment and barriers to entry. A chart of discounts followed by higher commissions is a hint, not proof.

Grab shows the subsidy is not a one-off. In FY2025 it reported US$1.00B of partner incentives and US$1.27B of consumer incentives, about US$2.27B in total, against US$3.37B of revenue. Its On-Demand incentives were 10.4% of On-Demand GMV in Q4 2025. Subsidies are still a normal operating cost, after more than a decade.

Lens

"Enshittification" (Doctorow)

A story of three stages: platforms treat users well, then favour business customers, then squeeze both. A useful warning about what happens after lock-in. Not a tested model with a fixed sequence.

Lens

"Technofeudalism" (Varoufakis)

Argues that digital territories are replacing markets. Platforms still hire, invest and compete, so I use "rent" as a tool without claiming capitalism has ended.

I use both authors as lenses to ask better questions. I do not use them as evidence of conduct.

07Visibility is the scarce thing

Main point: when search results are a ranked list, a place near the top is inventory, and the platform sells it.

Sponsored placement can be efficient. It helps a new seller get found and helps buyers see relevant offers. It turns into an access fee when organic results are weakened so that paying becomes the only way to be seen. A consumer cannot tell which has happened. A seller can only guess.

The test is whether extra advertising brings extra sales, or only moves sales the seller would have had anyway. That needs holdout experiments, not gross return on ad spend. Amazon's US$68.6B advertising line shows the scale of the business, but it does not tell us the "ad tax" on any single seller, because the line includes non-sellers.

My reading

The strongest market-power signal may be the ability to charge for visibility while also governing the ranking. A seller who pays for an ad inside a ranking the platform controls has no neutral referee.

08The worker pays too: incidence and the Indonesian cap

Main point: a commission cap can move the charge, not remove it.

Platform fees are only one side of the distribution. Drivers and riders supply vehicles, fuel, insurance and idle time, while jobs are handed out by an algorithm. The platform also gives flexible work and quick matching. The net effect depends on what the worker would earn otherwise, so the label "employee" or "contractor" is not enough.

Indonesia moved first and hardest. President Prabowo signed Presidential Regulation 27 of 2026, which lowers the maximum platform commission on two-wheeler ride-hailing from 20% to 8%. The Manpower Minister said the 8% scheme has applied since 1 July 2026. The regulation also adds accident and health insurance duties. Grab and Gojek said they would comply.

Trap

A drivers' union says that after the cap, the total deducted from some two-wheeler fares was still 16 to 24%, because of separate "application" and service fees. A commentator in the Jakarta Post made a similar point. If that is right, the cap moved the charge from one label to another. If it is wrong, the cap worked. Either way, regulators need to measure the total deduction, not the commission line.

Malaysia chose a different path. The Gig Workers Act 2025 (Act 872) took effect on 31 March 2026. It created the Malaysian Gig Economy Commission (SEGiM), a tribunal for disputes, and social security coverage through SOCSO. Estimates of the covered workforce range from about 1.2 million to 1.64 million in different reports. Critics, such as Focus Malaysia, say it leaves algorithm power largely intact and does not require platform matching contributions. Supporters say it gives workers a legal route for disputes without turning them into employees.

The European Union's Directive 2024/2831 takes a third route. It sets a rebuttable presumption of employment where the facts show control, plus rules on algorithmic transparency and human oversight. Member states must transpose it by 2 December 2026. It does not make every platform worker an employee.

Lesson

Labour rules and fee rules are the same debate. If you cap one line, the platform will look at the others. A good rule says what the worker or merchant takes home, not what the line item is called.

10Southeast Asia and Malaysia in focus

Main point: Malaysia has the evidence, the regulator and the new law. It does not yet have merchant-level data.

The e-Conomy SEA 2025 report by Google, Temasek and Bain puts the region's digital economy at over US$300B in gross merchandise value for 2025, growing 15%. E-commerce is about US$185B of that, with platform revenue of about US$41B. Malaysia's digital economy is projected at US$39B and is called the fastest-growing in the report, with e-commerce about US$20B.

Trap: revenue divided by GMV

US$41B of e-commerce revenue on US$185B GMV is about 22% (my calculation). It is tempting to call that the platforms' take rate. It is not. The report's revenue definition covers more than seller commissions, and it is a regional aggregate, not what a single seller pays. Use it for scale only.

The platforms

Grab is a useful window into the economics. For FY2025 it reported revenue of US$3.37B (up 20%), adjusted EBITDA of US$0.50B and its first full-year net profit. Malaysia was its biggest market at US$1.04B of revenue. On the incentive side it is still spending heavily.

Grab and GoTo have been reported to be in merger talks since 2025. The Indonesian Palace confirmed on 7 November 2025 that the government was involved and that the sovereign fund Danantara may take part. In sources I checked on 18 November 2026 I found no completed deal. If it happens, reports suggest one firm could hold a very large share of Indonesian on-demand services. That is a merchant-dependence question for Indonesia and a precedent for Malaysia.

The marketplaces: fees moved three times in 2026

Seller guides record that Shopee Malaysia added a 5% technical support fee on completed orders from 1 February 2026, and revised commission rates on 1 February, 21 May and 14 August. TikTok Shop Malaysia introduced a flat platform support fee of RM0.54 per delivered order (including SST) from 15 February 2026. Lazada changed its category commissions on 14 August 2026. Commission is also subject to 8% SST.

For a small seller, the visible fees on this order are 17 to 22 sen in the ringgit before payment charges, shipping programmes, vouchers or advertising. These are charges announced in one year. That is the fee-drift test from Section 01.

The regulator

The Malaysia Competition Commission (MyCC) published its Market Review on the Digital Economy Ecosystem on 10 February 2026, after 18 months of work. It covers mobile operating systems and payments, retail e-commerce, digital advertising and online travel agents, with a cross-cutting look at data privacy. It states that practices such as high commission fees, contract terms and control of consumer data can restrict competition and raise costs for local businesses. It made 18 policy recommendations. For e-commerce marketplaces it recommended more transparency and amendments to the Electronic Commerce Act 2006 so that marketplaces must act fairly and transparently. On 7 July 2026 the Dewan Rakyat passed amendments that widen MyCC's powers. Commentators note that a merger-control gap remains.

What the data says
  • Fee schedules in Malaysia changed several times in one year. Seller costs rose by announcement, not by negotiation.
  • MyCC has named commission levels and data control as competition concerns. A market review is a study, not a finding of infringement.
  • There is no public merchant-level dataset of total platform costs in Malaysia. This is the gap.

11Measuring dependence: a five-part index

Main point: dependence is a better early warning than the commission rate.

Two merchants can pay the same fee and face very different power. One earns 70% of revenue from a single platform and would need a year to rebuild it. The other has repeat customers on WhatsApp and can leave in a month. Their fee is the same. Their bargaining power is not.

I propose a Dependence Index for merchants. It is my own construct and has not been validated. Each of five parts is scored from 0 (free to leave) to 100 (trapped), and the index is the simple average.

S · Sales concentration

Share of revenue from the single biggest platform.

R · Replacement time

How many months to rebuild that revenue elsewhere.

O · Ownership of customers

Can the merchant contact and keep the buyer, or does the platform hold the relationship?

X · Contract and ranking limits

Parity clauses, forced programmes, opaque ranking, no data access.

B · Bundled reliance

Does the merchant also rely on the platform's delivery, payment or credit?

Use

DI = (S + R + O + X + B) ÷ 5

Track it over time. A rising index with rising fees is a stronger warning than either alone.

For the experts

The index is a reduced-form proxy for the merchant's outside option in a bargaining model. A fuller design: a panel of merchants matched on category and country, monthly net sales, fees, ads, fulfilment, returns and direct-channel costs. Identification from staggered rule changes (parity bans, cap introductions, fee revisions), platform entry and seller-level experiments. Pre-register outcomes, and handle ad-spend endogeneity, seasonality, product mix and survivorship.

A worked example: a home baker in Malaysia

Take a hypothetical baker, Aini. Her monthly sales are RM20,000. About 70% (RM14,000) comes through Shopee and TikTok Shop, and 30% through her own WhatsApp list. I assume the low-end Shopee case from Exhibit 9: 11% commission, 8% SST on the commission and a 5% technical support fee, which is about 16.9% of marketplace sales. I also assume she spends 6% of marketplace sales on vouchers and advertising. These are my assumptions to show the mechanics, not a measured average.

122026 to 2036: when the agent chooses for us

Main point: AI can weaken marketplace toll booths and build new ones at the same time.

An AI shopping agent can compare delivered prices, read specifications, and send an order straight to a merchant. If buyers hand that job to an independent agent, the marketplace's sponsored-listing auction loses power. That is the hopeful case.

The rest of 2026 shows why it is not automatic. In September 2025 OpenAI and Stripe released the Agentic Commerce Protocol. In January 2026 Google and Shopify announced the Universal Commerce Protocol, built with retailers including Etsy, Wayfair, Target and Walmart. By March 2026, reports say OpenAI scaled back its native Instant Checkout to app-based flows. Amazon joined neither protocol. Reports say it blocks outside AI crawlers and lets only its own agents, such as Rufus, Alexa+ and Buy for Me, reach its catalogue. Mastercard reported its first agentic transaction in the third quarter of 2025, and Visa and Mastercard are both building agent payment programmes.

Look at what has to sit between a buyer and a purchase. The model that thinks. The identity that proves the agent speaks for you. The approval to spend. The ranking that picks the seller. Each is a place for a toll. Marketplaces took a cut for ranking and delivery. The agent era adds layers above them.

My central 2036 view (a judgement, not a forecast)

Market power partly migrates from marketplace search to agent-mediated discovery and payment approval. Marketplace fees may fall in some categories, while new placement, routing and authorisation fees appear. The seller still has a toll to pay. It is just collected at a different gate.

2036 scenario A

Open-agent competition

Independent agents compare merchant APIs. Payments interoperate. Direct channels regain bargaining power. Verification services still take a fee.

2036 scenario B

Assistant oligopoly

A few model and operating-system firms control recommendation and payment. Today's ad rents move into recommendation placement and routing fees.

2036 scenario C

Fragmented sovereign stacks

Blocs require local identity, cloud and payment. Domestic champions grow, cross-border commerce costs more.

Reading

Not mutually exclusive

Different regions and categories can follow different scenarios in the same year. I expect a mix, with B strongest where one firm already owns the phone and the assistant.

132036 to 2076: automation, people and borders

Main point: the long view is about who owns the rails. The technology is secondary.

Fifty-year forecasts are conditional pathways, not predictions. Wars, energy breakthroughs, ecological shocks and new technology can overturn any straight line from today's platforms. I give signposts, not probabilities.

Automation and income

Robots and software may lower the cost of fulfilment, service, routing and some manufacturing. Speed is uncertain because physical deployment, energy, capital, regulation and reliability slow it down. My earlier draft said production costs would head to zero and that labour's buying power would collapse. Neither follows. Land, energy, materials, scarce skills and trust keep their price. Incomes depend on ownership, bargaining institutions, tax and new jobs. Automation is a distribution problem, not a guarantee of a wage-free economy.

People

The UN's World Population Prospects 2024 projects the world population at about 8.2 billion in 2024, a peak of about 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s, then a slow fall to about 10.2 billion by 2100. Many countries peak sooner. As of 2024, 63 countries and areas, including China, Germany, Japan and Russia, had already passed their peak. By about 2080 there will be more people aged 65 and over than children under 18. I use the demographic point carefully. Ageing economies have reasons to automate care and logistics. Younger economies need jobs and affordable access to digital markets. Population does not translate directly into demand. Age-specific spending, savings, migration and productivity all matter.

Borders

States now treat payments, cloud, chips, identity and telecoms as strategic. Fragmentation can protect a country from outside shocks. It also duplicates standards and shrinks cross-border scale. A sovereign payment system or cloud is not automatically open. State-backed systems can also become monopolies. The test is the same everywhere: transparent access, contestable procurement, privacy, auditability and proportionate security rules. Digital sovereignty should mean accountable control with interoperability, not only domestic ownership of a monopoly.

14The bear case, in its strongest form

Main point: the strongest argument against my thesis is that platform fees mostly pay for real work and competition is already biting.

A fair critic would say this.

  • Platform margins are thin. DoorDash keeps US$1.40 of every US$100 as net income. Grab earned US$0.50B of adjusted EBITDA on US$3.37B of revenue, after paying US$2.27B in incentives. That does not look like a monopolist's margin.
  • Fees buy real services. Fulfilment, delivery, fraud control, payment and customer support have costs. Marketplace Pulse itself says sellers would pay for those elsewhere.
  • Entry is real. TikTok Shop grew quickly in Southeast Asia. Chinese sellers pushed prices down on Amazon. Falling seller numbers can mean harder competition, not extraction.
  • Consumers gain. Low prices, fast delivery and wide choice are real benefits. Courts weigh them.
  • Caps can backfire. A cap can hurt smaller platforms with higher unit costs, push the charge into other lines, and entrench the biggest players.
  • AI may open markets. If agents do compare prices across merchants, the gatekeepers lose, and my "new toll" story is wrong.

I accept the first five as live possibilities. The facts that would keep my view alive are fee drift, rising dependence and weak entry together. If those three do not appear in data, the thesis fails.

18A practical plan

1
Publish a total-cost disclosure. Every large marketplace discloses, by category, what a typical seller pays in all charges as a share of sales, with the method.
Measure: published ratio each year; gap between headline and total.
2
Add notice and audit for fee changes. Several 2026 fee changes were announced within the year. Require long notice and a reason.
Measure: days of notice; number of changes per year.
3
Open the ranking to audit. Label sponsored results, and let an independent party test whether paid placement degrades organic results.
Measure: share of top slots that are paid; audit findings.
4
Give sellers their customer data. Portable orders, reviews and contact rights, within PDPA limits.
Measure: share of sellers who can export their full customer history.
5
Build a merchant-level dataset. Anonymised, monthly, for a few hundred Malaysian MSMEs, run by an independent body.
Measure: dataset live within 12 months.
6
Set agent-commerce rules early. Open protocols, disclosure of sponsored recommendations, no privileged access to payment approval.
Measure: independent agents' share of agent-routed orders.

For a merchant: a 90-day plan to lower dependence

Days 1–30: measure

For every order, write down total platform charges: commission, SST, fees, vouchers, ads and delivery programmes. Divide by sales. Do it for a full month.

Days 1–30: score yourself

Work out your five Dependence Index parts. Write the number down. Do it again in 90 days.

Days 31–60: build a direct list

Offer repeat buyers a reason to order directly: a small discount, a loyalty stamp or first notice of new items. Keep their consent and respect PDPA.

Days 31–60: test a price

Offer the same item a little cheaper on your own channel. See how many switch. This is the direct test of your outside option.

Days 61–90: try a second channel

Open one more marketplace or a social shop, with a small stock. Learn how fast sales can be rebuilt.

Measure

Targets at day 90

Total platform charges as a share of sales known. Direct share of sales up. Biggest platform below the share you started with.

Call to action: for the Malaysian government
  • Within 6 months: have MyCC and KPDN publish a plan and timeline for the Electronic Commerce Act amendments on marketplace transparency.
  • Within 12 months: run a pilot merchant-cost dataset with SME Corp, MDEC and banks, using the Dependence Index or a similar tool.
  • Within 24 months: report publicly, by category, total platform charges for sellers and whether fees changed faster than costs.

Self-check: five questions for you
  1. What share of my sales comes from my biggest platform?
  2. How long would it take to rebuild that elsewhere?
  3. Do I own my customer's contact details?
  4. Have my total platform charges risen faster than my costs?
  5. Do I rely on this platform for delivery, payment or credit as well?
My take

I do not think platforms are villains, and I do not think every fee is fair. I think the durable power is the right to set the rules of access. Fees are a symptom. The next contest is over who owns the layers that choose and approve. For Malaysian MSMEs the practical move is simple: know your dependence, keep your customers, and push for numbers that show total cost, not only the commission line.

Field Notes · Ekonomi platform

Adakah platform digital sedang menjadi tuan tanah swasta ekonomi?

Yuran, sewa ekonomi dan kebergantungan peniaga di Amazon, DoorDash, Grab, Booking.com dan pasar dalam talian Malaysia. Dan apa jadi apabila ejen AI mula memilih penjual untuk kita, 2026 hingga 2076.

Azman Taufik · 18 November 2026 · kira-kira 40 minit (bacaan panjang)
Komen di LinkedIn
Ringkasan pantas
  • Soalannya: bolehkah platform mengenakan bayaran jauh melebihi nilai perkhidmatannya, kerana penjual tiada tempat lain untuk pergi? Yuran yang tinggi sahaja tidak membuktikannya.
  • Baca angka dahulu. AS$240.8B daripada perkhidmatan penjual dan iklan Amazon bukan kadar potongan 50%. DoorDash menyimpan 13.6% nilai pesanan sebagai hasil, tetapi hanya 1.4% sebagai untung bersih.
  • Isyarat yang lebih baik ialah kebergantungan, bukan komisen. Saya mencadangkan Indeks Kebergantungan lima bahagian. Peniaga yang tidak dapat menggantikan jualan platformnya dalam beberapa bulan sedang terdedah, walau apa pun yuran yang tertera.
  • Malaysia sudah bergerak. MyCC menerbitkan kajian ekonomi digital pada 10 Februari 2026. Akta Pekerja Gig berkuat kuasa pada 31 Mac 2026. Shopee, Lazada dan TikTok Shop semuanya mengubah yuran pada 2026.
  • Tol seterusnya mungkin berada di atas pasar dalam talian. Jika ejen AI memilih dan membayar bagi pihak kita, kuasa berpindah kepada pihak yang menguasai cadangan, identiti dan kelulusan bayaran. Itulah pandangan utama saya untuk 2036. Ia pertimbangan, bukan ramalan.

Adakah yuran platform itu harga, atau sewa? Jawapannya menentukan sama ada kita perlukan had, peraturan akses, atau tiada apa-apa.

Sebuah restoran membayar DoorDash. Sebuah homestay membayar ejen pelancongan dalam talian. Seorang pembuat kek rumah membayar Shopee, kemudian membayar lagi untuk baucar, program penghantaran dan iklan yang menaikkan kedudukan produknya. Masing-masing berkata jumlahnya terlalu tinggi. Masing-masing juga berkata mereka tidak boleh keluar.

Kertas ini menguji rungutan itu. Ia tidak menganggap yuran itu tidak adil, dan tidak menganggapnya adil. Ia dibina atas draf awal saya, membetulkan beberapa dakwaan di dalamnya, dan menambah bukti baharu daripada penyata syarikat dan pengawal selia sehingga 18 November 2026.

01Soalannya, dan apa yang dikira sebagai jawapan

Intinya: yuran menjadi sewa apabila ia melebihi apa yang akan dibayar pasaran yang bersaing untuk perkhidmatan itu, dan penjual tidak boleh berundur.

Platform melakukan kerja yang nyata. Mereka mencari pembeli, memegang bayaran, menyemak penipuan, mengurus penghantaran dan menanggung risiko. Peniaga yang terpaksa membayar untuk kerja yang sama di tempat lain tidak dieksploitasi oleh yuran yang menampung kerja itu. Itulah titik tolak yang jujur. Marketplace Pulse, firma penyelidikan di sebalik anggaran yuran Amazon yang paling banyak dipetik, juga berkata begitu: perkhidmatan itu tidak percuma di tempat lain, kerana iklan Google dan Facebook serta gudang luar pun berharga.

Kebimbangan bermula apabila platform menguasai peraturan akses sekali gus dengan pasarannya. Ia boleh menyusun kedudukan pesaing, menjual iklan yang diperlukan untuk dilihat, menetapkan syarat penghantaran dan bayaran, dan menyimpan data. Pada saat itu yuran bukan lagi sekadar harga perkhidmatan. Ia juga harga untuk kekal kelihatan.

Untuk yang pakar

Takrifan kerja sewa platform (penggunaan operasi saya): hasil yang boleh dikaitkan dengan penguasaan atas titik sempit akses yang terhad, melebihi pulangan bersaing yang diperlukan untuk membekalkan perkhidmatan asas.

Penguraian: bayaran peniaga = kos perkhidmatan yang cekap + pulangan bersaing biasa + sewa titik sempit + ralat ukuran. Setiap unsur sukar dianggar kerana platform berkongsi infrastruktur dan menyubsidi satu pihak oleh pihak lain. Unsur sewa hanya dapat dikenal pasti dengan kes tandingan: perubahan peraturan pariti, perubahan saluran bayaran, perubahan kedudukan carian atau kemasukan pemain baharu.

Saya menggunakan lima ujian. Platform lebih cenderung memperoleh sewa apabila kebanyakannya benar.

  1. Kebergantungan: peniaga tidak dapat menggantikan jualan platform dengan cepat.
  2. Berlapis: platform turut menguasai penemuan, penghantaran, bayaran atau kredit, jadi meninggalkan satu lapisan merugikan lapisan lain.
  3. Tidak telus: peniaga tidak dapat melihat bagaimana kedudukan carian atau yuran ditetapkan.
  4. Yuran terus naik: jumlah caj naik lebih pantas daripada kos perkhidmatan yang ditampungnya.
  5. Kemasukan lemah: pesaing datang lalu gagal, atau tidak pernah datang.

Tiada satu pun daripada lima ini cukup sebagai bukti sendirian. Bersama-sama, ia membentuk corak yang boleh diperiksa oleh pengawal selia, mahkamah dan penyelidik.

02Apa kata angka

Intinya: empat angka yang berbeza semuanya dipanggil "kadar potongan". Setiap satu menjawab soalan yang berbeza.

  • Komisen utama: peratusan dalam jadual yuran.
  • Nisbah perbelanjaan penjual: semua yang dibayar penjual kepada platform sebagai peratusan jualan. Ini termasuk pemenuhan, iklan, storan dan pemulangan.
  • Margin hasil bersih platform: hasil yang diiktiraf platform dibahagi nilai pesanan.
  • Margin untung platform: apa yang tinggal selepas kos platform sendiri.

Mencampuradukkan keempatnya menghasilkan tajuk berita yang kedengaran tajam tetapi kurang bermakna. Inilah contoh pertama.

Perangkap: mencampur dua baris Amazon

Amazon melaporkan AS$172.2B perkhidmatan penjual pihak ketiga dan AS$68.6B pengiklanan bagi 2025. Dicampur, jumlahnya AS$240.8B, kira-kira 34% jualan bersih (kiraan saya). Ia kelihatan seperti bukti potongan besar daripada penjual. Ia bukan.

Baris iklan itu turut merangkumi vendor, penerbit, pengarang dan jenama yang bukan penjual pasar. Membahagikannya dengan jumlah jualan penjual menghasilkan angka yang tiada siapa dapat pertahankan. Kegunaan yang betul bagi AS$240.8B ialah saiz: ia menunjukkan betapa besarnya pemonetan pasar itu.

03Amazon: satu pasar, beberapa lapisan

Intinya: Amazon menunjukkan bagaimana platform boleh cekap dan menyekat pada masa yang sama. Mana satu yang menang ialah soalan mahkamah, bukan soalan blog.

Penjual pihak ketiga merangkumi kira-kira 61 hingga 62% unit yang dijual di Amazon, menurut anggaran Marketplace Pulse. Mereka bukan perniagaan sampingan. Merekalah rak kedai itu.

Marketplace Pulse membina anggaran yurannya daripada sampel penyata untung rugi penjual. Ia melaporkan penjual biasa membayar kira-kira 15% fi rujukan, 20 hingga 35% untuk pemenuhan dan storan, dan sehingga 15% untuk iklan. Purata 2022 ialah kira-kira 50% jualan. Dalam susulan Mac 2024, ia meletakkan penjual jenama sendiri yang biasa pada 50 hingga 60%. Firma itu juga melaporkan bahawa yuran sebagai peratusan jualan naik dari tahun ke tahun, kerana perkhidmatan yang sama menjadi lebih mahal atau tidak dapat dielakkan.

Hujah Amazon

Yuran membayar perkhidmatan yang nyata

Pemenuhan, penghantaran Prime dan pemulangan ada kosnya. Amazon menolak sebahagian data Marketplace Pulse dan berkata sebahagian bilangan penjualnya kurang menganggar penjual aktif.

Hujah pengkritik

Yuran tidak dapat dielakkan

Penjual berkata mereka perlukan Fulfilment by Amazon untuk mendapat lencana Prime dan perlukan iklan untuk dilihat. Kedua-duanya menjadi tol.

Dua titik data merumitkan cerita ini. Marketplace Pulse mengira kira-kira 584,000 penjual aktif di Amazon.com pada Januari 2025 dan kira-kira 500,000 pada Mac 2026, dan berkata pendaftaran baharu pada 2025 ialah yang terendah sejak ia mula menjejak pada 2015. Bilangan penjual yang menurun boleh bermakna keadaan lebih sukar bagi penjual kecil. Ia juga boleh bermakna persaingan lebih sengit, tarif, dan penjual China menjual lebih murah. Data tidak dapat memisahkan kedua-duanya, dan Amazon mempertikaikan bilangan itu.

Dari segi undang-undang, Suruhanjaya Perdagangan Persekutuan (FTC) AS dan pihak pendakwa negeri mendakwa Amazon menggunakan amalan berkait untuk mengekalkan kuasa monopoli. Ini dakwaan. Perbicaraan bangku persekutuan dijadual pada 29 Mac 2027, selepas hakim menolak pada 9 Oktober 2026 permintaan menggesernya ke Jun. Satu kes California yang berasingan dilaporkan dijadual pada 19 Januari 2027. Saya menganggap kedua-duanya terbuka sehingga penghakiman.

Pengajaran

Gabungan yang sama (carian, Prime, penghantaran, iklan) boleh menaikkan jualan penjual dan juga memerangkapnya. Hanya eksperimen yang dapat menentukan mana satu. Tanyalah: jika penjual ini keluar dari Amazon, berapa peratus jualan akan hilang, dan sepantas mana dia boleh memenanginya semula?

04DoorDash: yuran peniaga tinggi, untung platform nipis

Intinya: yuran boleh membebankan peniaga tanpa menjadi sewa bagi platform.

DoorDash melaporkan nilai pesanan pasar S3 2026 sebanyak AS$33.9B dan hasil AS$4.6B. Margin hasil bersih ialah 13.6%. Untung kasar GAAP ialah 6.8% nilai pesanan, untung sumbangan 5.2%, dan pendapatan bersih 1.4%. Jumlah pesanan mencecah 993 juta.

Sebab itu saya tidak memanggil 13.6% sebagai sewa. Platform penghantaran membayar pemandu, insurans, sokongan, bayaran balik dan penipuan. Sedikit sahaja hasil sampai ke untung. Peniaga masih boleh rugi. Restoran yang pelanggannya akan memesan terus membayar yuran atas pesanan yang tidak dicipta platform. Restoran yang mendapat pelanggan baharu mungkin untung.

Perhatian

Ujian bagi apl penghantaran ialah margin sumbangan peniaga bagi setiap pesanan tambahan. Hanya segelintir penyelidik memiliki angka itu. Minta restoran berkongsi angka sendiri, pesanan demi pesanan, selama sebulan. Itu asas bukti yang lebih baik daripada mana-mana jadual komisen.

05Hotel dan ejen pelancongan dalam talian

Intinya: soalan sebenar ialah sama ada hotel boleh mengarahkan tetamu ke laman webnya sendiri.

Ejen pelancongan dalam talian mengurangkan kos carian dan membawa tetamu asing kepada hotel bebas. Mereka juga mewujudkan kebergantungan. Alat lama untuk kebergantungan itu ialah klausa pariti, yang menghalang hotel menawarkan harga lebih rendah di laman sendiri.

Suruhanjaya Eropah menetapkan Booking.com sebagai penjaga pintu pada Mei 2024. Kewajipan Akta Pasaran Digital (DMA) terpakai mulai 14 November 2024, dan pariti diharamkan. Pada 28 September 2026 Suruhanjaya menerbitkan lembaran fakta yang menyatakan perniagaan di Kawasan Ekonomi Eropah kini boleh menetapkan harga dan syarat dengan bebas di dalam dan di luar Booking.com, termasuk di saluran terus mereka, selepas perubahan tambahan oleh Booking berikutan dialog dengan pengawal selia. Pengulas industri hotel berhujah bahawa penempatan berbayar masih membentuk keterlihatan, yang menjadi isi Bahagian 07.

MyCC Malaysia menamakan ejen pelancongan dalam talian sebagai satu bidang kajiannya dan menyatakan tindakan penguatkuasaan terhadap mereka memerlukan kerjasama rapat antara agensi. Saya kembali kepada ini dalam Bahagian 10.

Pengajaran

Sama ada komisen 15% atau 25% kurang penting berbanding sama ada hotel boleh mengalihkan permintaan. Larangan pariti memberi hotel jalan keluar. Ia tidak memberinya trafik.

06Mengapa platform "tumbang" ke satu pihak, dan mengapa kadangkala tidak

Intinya: skala, kesan rangkaian dan tingkah laku ialah tiga perkara berbeza. Hanya yang ketiga menjadi sebab untuk bertindak.

Rochet dan Tirole menunjukkan bahawa platform meletakkan harga berbeza bagi setiap pihak dalam pasaran. Satu pihak mungkin membayar sedikit atau tidak langsung, kerana ia membawa masuk pihak yang lain. Jadi apl pengguna percuma dengan yuran peniaga berbayar tidak mencurigakan dengan sendirinya. Parker dan Van Alstyne menambah bahawa kesan rangkaian boleh menjadikan manfaat satu pihak bergantung pada pihak yang lain, sebab itu pertumbuhan memberi makan kepada dirinya sendiri.

Itu hanya menghasilkan seorang pemenang apabila bertukar itu sukar dan pengguna kekal pada satu platform. Jika orang menggunakan beberapa platform (multi-homing), atau produknya berbeza mengikut bandar, kecenderungan itu lemah. Penghantaran makanan di bandar besar boleh menampung beberapa apl. Satu destinasi carian bagi satu kategori produk mungkin tidak.

Modal teroka menambah satu kemelut. Platform boleh rugi untuk membina rangkaian, kemudian menaikkan harga. Kadangkala itu kos wajar membina perkhidmatan yang cekap. Kadangkala ia harga di bawah kos untuk menyingkirkan pesaing yang sama cekap. Untuk membuktikan yang kedua, penyelidik memerlukan bukti harga di bawah kos, kemungkinan pemulihan kerugian dan halangan kemasukan. Carta diskaun diikuti komisen lebih tinggi ialah petunjuk, bukan bukti.

Grab menunjukkan subsidi bukan perkara sekali sahaja. Bagi FY2025 ia melaporkan AS$1.00B insentif rakan kongsi dan AS$1.27B insentif pengguna, kira-kira AS$2.27B keseluruhannya, berbanding hasil AS$3.37B. Insentif On-Demand ialah 10.4% GMV On-Demand pada S4 2025. Subsidi masih kos operasi biasa, selepas lebih sedekad.

Lensa

"Enshittification" (Doctorow)

Kisah tiga peringkat: platform melayan pengguna dengan baik, kemudian memihak kepada pelanggan perniagaan, kemudian memerah kedua-duanya. Amaran berguna tentang apa yang berlaku selepas terkunci. Bukan model teruji dengan urutan tetap.

Lensa

"Technofeudalism" (Varoufakis)

Berhujah bahawa wilayah digital sedang menggantikan pasaran. Platform masih mengambil pekerja, melabur dan bersaing, jadi saya menggunakan "sewa" sebagai alat tanpa mendakwa kapitalisme sudah berakhir.

Saya menggunakan kedua-dua pengarang sebagai lensa untuk bertanya soalan yang lebih baik. Saya tidak menggunakannya sebagai bukti tingkah laku.

07Keterlihatan ialah barang yang terhad

Intinya: apabila hasil carian ialah senarai berkedudukan, tempat di bahagian atas ialah inventori, dan platform menjualnya.

Penempatan berbayar boleh cekap. Ia membantu penjual baharu ditemui dan membantu pembeli melihat tawaran yang relevan. Ia menjadi yuran akses apabila hasil organik dilemahkan sehingga membayar menjadi satu-satunya cara untuk dilihat. Pengguna tidak dapat membezakan yang mana berlaku. Penjual hanya mampu meneka.

Ujiannya ialah sama ada iklan tambahan membawa jualan tambahan, atau hanya memindahkan jualan yang memang akan diperoleh penjual. Itu memerlukan eksperimen kumpulan kawalan, bukan pulangan kasar atas belanja iklan. Baris pengiklanan Amazon sebanyak AS$68.6B menunjukkan saiz perniagaan itu, tetapi ia tidak memberitahu kita "cukai iklan" ke atas mana-mana penjual, kerana baris itu merangkumi pihak yang bukan penjual.

Bacaan saya

Isyarat kuasa pasaran yang paling kuat mungkin keupayaan untuk mengenakan bayaran bagi keterlihatan sambil menguasai sistem kedudukan itu sendiri. Penjual yang membayar iklan dalam kedudukan yang dikawal platform tidak mempunyai pengadil yang neutral.

08Pekerja turut membayar: insiden kos dan had Indonesia

Intinya: had komisen boleh memindahkan caj, bukan menghapuskannya.

Yuran platform hanyalah satu sisi pengagihan. Pemandu dan penunggang membekalkan kenderaan, bahan api, insurans dan masa menunggu, sementara kerja diagihkan oleh algoritma. Platform juga memberi kerja fleksibel dan padanan pantas. Kesan bersihnya bergantung pada apa yang akan diperoleh pekerja jika tidak, jadi label "pekerja" atau "kontraktor" tidak mencukupi.

Indonesia bergerak dahulu dan paling keras. Presiden Prabowo menandatangani Peraturan Presiden 27 Tahun 2026, yang menurunkan komisen maksimum platform bagi e-panggilan dua roda daripada 20% kepada 8%. Menteri Ketenagakerjaan berkata skim 8% itu terpakai sejak 1 Julai 2026. Peraturan itu juga menambah kewajipan insurans kemalangan dan kesihatan. Grab dan Gojek berkata mereka akan mematuhinya.

Perangkap

Sebuah kesatuan pemandu berkata selepas had dikuatkuasakan, jumlah yang ditolak daripada sesetengah tambang dua roda masih 16 hingga 24%, kerana yuran "aplikasi" dan perkhidmatan yang berasingan. Seorang pengulas Jakarta Post membuat hujah yang serupa. Jika benar, had itu hanya memindahkan caj dari satu label ke label lain. Jika salah, had itu berjaya. Dalam kedua-dua keadaan, pengawal selia perlu mengukur jumlah potongan, bukan baris komisen.

Malaysia memilih jalan lain. Akta Pekerja Gig 2025 (Akta 872) berkuat kuasa pada 31 Mac 2026. Ia mewujudkan Suruhanjaya Ekonomi Gig Malaysia (SEGiM), tribunal untuk pertikaian, dan perlindungan keselamatan sosial melalui PERKESO. Anggaran tenaga kerja yang dilindungi berbeza antara kira-kira 1.2 juta dan 1.64 juta dalam laporan berlainan. Pengkritik, seperti Focus Malaysia, berkata ia membiarkan kuasa algoritma hampir utuh dan tidak mewajibkan caruman sepadan platform. Penyokong berkata ia memberi pekerja saluran undang-undang untuk pertikaian tanpa menjadikan mereka pekerja tetap.

Arahan 2024/2831 Kesatuan Eropah mengambil jalan ketiga. Ia menetapkan anggapan pekerjaan yang boleh dipatahkan apabila fakta menunjukkan kawalan, serta peraturan tentang ketelusan algoritma dan pengawasan manusia. Negara anggota mesti memindahkannya ke dalam undang-undang menjelang 2 Disember 2026. Ia tidak menjadikan setiap pekerja platform sebagai pekerja tetap.

Pengajaran

Peraturan buruh dan peraturan yuran ialah perdebatan yang sama. Jika anda mengehadkan satu baris, platform akan melihat baris yang lain. Peraturan yang baik menyatakan apa yang dibawa pulang oleh pekerja atau peniaga, bukan apa nama baris itu.

Intinya: Malaysia mempunyai bukti, pengawal selia dan undang-undang baharu. Malaysia belum mempunyai data di peringkat peniaga.

Ekonomi digital Malaysia diunjurkan AS$39B dan disebut yang paling pantas berkembang dalam laporan itu, dengan e-dagang kira-kira AS$20B.

Perangkap: hasil dibahagi GMV

Hasil e-dagang AS$41B atas GMV AS$185B ialah kira-kira 22% (kiraan saya). Menggoda untuk menamakannya kadar potongan platform. Ia bukan. Takrifan hasil dalam laporan itu merangkumi lebih daripada komisen penjual, dan ia agregat serantau, bukan apa yang dibayar seorang penjual. Gunakannya untuk saiz sahaja.

Platform

Grab ialah tingkap yang berguna kepada ekonominya. Bagi FY2025 ia melaporkan hasil AS$3.37B (naik 20%), EBITDA terlaras AS$0.50B dan untung bersih setahun penuh yang pertama. Malaysia ialah pasaran terbesarnya dengan hasil AS$1.04B. Dari sudut insentif, ia masih berbelanja besar.

Grab dan GoTo dilaporkan berada dalam rundingan penggabungan sejak 2025. Istana Indonesia mengesahkan pada 7 November 2025 bahawa kerajaan terlibat dan dana kekayaan negara Danantara mungkin mengambil bahagian. Dalam sumber yang saya semak pada 18 November 2026, saya tidak menemui tawaran yang selesai. Jika ia berlaku, laporan menyatakan satu firma boleh memegang bahagian yang sangat besar perkhidmatan atas permintaan Indonesia. Itu soal kebergantungan peniaga bagi Indonesia dan duluan bagi Malaysia.

Pasar dalam talian: yuran berubah tiga kali pada 2026

Panduan penjual merekodkan bahawa Shopee Malaysia menambah yuran sokongan teknikal 5% atas pesanan selesai mulai 1 Februari 2026, dan menyemak kadar komisen pada 1 Februari, 21 Mei dan 14 Ogos. TikTok Shop Malaysia memperkenalkan yuran sokongan platform tetap RM0.54 bagi setiap pesanan yang dihantar (termasuk SST) mulai 15 Februari 2026. Lazada mengubah komisen kategorinya pada 14 Ogos 2026. Komisen juga tertakluk kepada SST 8%.

Bagi penjual kecil, yuran yang kelihatan atas pesanan ini ialah 17 hingga 22 sen dalam setiap ringgit sebelum caj bayaran, program penghantaran, baucar atau iklan. Ini caj yang diumumkan dalam satu tahun. Itulah ujian yuran terus naik daripada Bahagian 01.

Pengawal selia

Suruhanjaya Persaingan Malaysia (MyCC) menerbitkan Kajian Pasaran Ekosistem Ekonomi Digital pada 10 Februari 2026, selepas kerja selama 18 bulan. Ia meliputi sistem pengendalian mudah alih dan bayaran, e-dagang runcit, perkhidmatan pengiklanan digital dan ejen pelancongan dalam talian, dengan tinjauan merentas sektor tentang privasi data. Ia menyatakan bahawa amalan seperti komisen tinggi, terma kontrak dan kawalan data pengguna boleh menyekat persaingan dan menaikkan kos bagi perniagaan tempatan. Ia membuat 18 cadangan dasar. Bagi pasar e-dagang, ia mengesyorkan ketelusan yang lebih tinggi dan pindaan kepada Akta Perdagangan Elektronik 2006 supaya pasar bertindak dengan adil dan telus. Pada 7 Julai 2026 Dewan Rakyat meluluskan pindaan yang meluaskan kuasa MyCC. Pengulas menyatakan jurang kawalan penggabungan masih ada.

Apa kata data
  • Jadual yuran di Malaysia berubah beberapa kali dalam setahun. Kos penjual naik melalui pengumuman, bukan perundingan.
  • MyCC menamakan tahap komisen dan kawalan data sebagai kebimbangan persaingan. Kajian pasaran ialah kajian, bukan dapatan pelanggaran.
  • Tiada set data awam di peringkat peniaga tentang jumlah kos platform di Malaysia. Inilah jurangnya.

11Mengukur kebergantungan: indeks lima bahagian

Intinya: kebergantungan ialah amaran awal yang lebih baik daripada kadar komisen.

Dua peniaga boleh membayar yuran yang sama tetapi menghadapi kuasa yang jauh berbeza. Seorang memperoleh 70% hasil daripada satu platform dan perlu setahun untuk membinanya semula. Seorang lagi mempunyai pelanggan tetap dalam WhatsApp dan boleh keluar dalam sebulan. Yuran mereka sama. Kuasa tawar-menawar mereka tidak.

Saya mencadangkan Indeks Kebergantungan untuk peniaga. Ia binaan saya sendiri dan belum disahkan. Setiap satu daripada lima bahagian diberi skor 0 (bebas keluar) hingga 100 (terperangkap), dan indeks ialah purata mudahnya.

S · Penumpuan jualan

Bahagian hasil daripada satu platform terbesar.

R · Masa penggantian

Berapa bulan diperlukan untuk membina semula hasil itu di tempat lain.

O · Pemilikan pelanggan

Bolehkah peniaga menghubungi dan mengekalkan pembeli, atau platform yang memegang hubungan itu?

X · Had kontrak dan kedudukan

Klausa pariti, program wajib, kedudukan yang legap, tiada akses kepada data.

B · Kebergantungan berbungkus

Adakah peniaga turut bergantung pada penghantaran, bayaran atau kredit platform itu?

Cara guna

IK = (S + R + O + X + B) ÷ 5

Jejaki dari semasa ke semasa. Indeks yang naik bersama yuran yang naik ialah amaran yang lebih kuat daripada salah satunya sahaja.

Untuk pakar

Indeks ini ialah proksi bentuk terturun bagi pilihan luar peniaga dalam model tawar-menawar. Reka bentuk yang lebih lengkap: panel peniaga yang dipadankan mengikut kategori dan negara, dengan jualan bersih bulanan, yuran, iklan, pemenuhan, pemulangan dan kos saluran terus. Pengenalpastian daripada perubahan peraturan berperingkat (larangan pariti, pengenalan had, semakan yuran), kemasukan platform dan eksperimen peringkat penjual. Daftarkan hasil lebih awal, dan tangani endogeniti belanja iklan, musim, campuran produk dan bias kemandirian.

Contoh kiraan: tukang kek rumah di Malaysia

Ambil seorang tukang kek rumah rekaan, Aini. Jualan bulanannya RM20,000. Kira-kira 70% (RM14,000) melalui Shopee dan TikTok Shop, dan 30% melalui senarai WhatsApp sendiri. Saya juga andaikan beliau membelanjakan 6% jualan pasar untuk baucar dan iklan. Ini andaian saya untuk menunjukkan mekanismenya, bukan purata yang diukur.

122026 hingga 2036: apabila ejen yang memilih untuk kita

Intinya: AI boleh melemahkan pintu tol pasar dan membina pintu tol baharu pada masa yang sama.

Ejen beli-belah AI boleh membandingkan harga sampai ke rumah, membaca spesifikasi dan menghantar pesanan terus kepada peniaga. Jika pembeli menyerahkan tugas itu kepada ejen bebas, kuasa lelongan penyenaraian berbayar di pasar akan berkurang. Itu kes yang menggembirakan.

Peristiwa sepanjang 2026 menunjukkan mengapa ia tidak automatik. Pada September 2025 OpenAI dan Stripe melancarkan Agentic Commerce Protocol. Pada Januari 2026 Google dan Shopify mengumumkan Universal Commerce Protocol, dibina bersama peruncit termasuk Etsy, Wayfair, Target dan Walmart. Menjelang Mac 2026, laporan menyatakan OpenAI mengecilkan Instant Checkout asalnya kepada aliran dalam apl. Amazon tidak menyertai kedua-dua protokol. Laporan menyebut ia menyekat perangkak AI luar dan hanya membenarkan ejennya sendiri, seperti Rufus, Alexa+ dan Buy for Me, mencapai katalognya. Mastercard melaporkan transaksi ejen pertamanya pada suku ketiga 2025, dan Visa serta Mastercard sedang membina program bayaran untuk ejen.

Lihat apa yang perlu ada antara pembeli dan pembelian. Model yang berfikir. Identiti yang membuktikan ejen itu bercakap bagi pihak anda. Kelulusan untuk berbelanja. Kedudukan yang memilih penjual. Setiap satu ialah tempat untuk tol. Pasar mengambil bahagian untuk kedudukan dan penghantaran. Zaman ejen menambah lapisan di atasnya.

Pandangan utama saya bagi 2036 (pertimbangan, bukan ramalan)

Yuran pasar mungkin turun dalam sesetengah kategori, sementara yuran penempatan, penghalaan dan kebenaran yang baharu muncul. Penjual masih perlu membayar tol. Cuma ia dikutip di pintu yang berbeza.

132036 hingga 2076: automasi, manusia dan sempadan

Intinya: pandangan jangka panjang ialah soal siapa memiliki landasan. Teknologi hanya perkara kedua.

Ramalan 50 tahun ialah laluan bersyarat, bukan jangkaan. Perang, kejayaan tenaga, kejutan ekologi dan teknologi baharu boleh membatalkan mana-mana garis lurus daripada platform hari ini. Saya beri tanda arah, bukan kebarangkalian.

Automasi dan pendapatan

Robot dan perisian mungkin menurunkan kos pemenuhan, perkhidmatan, penghalaan dan sebahagian pembuatan. Kelajuannya tidak pasti kerana pemasangan fizikal, tenaga, modal, peraturan dan kebolehpercayaan memperlahankannya. Draf awal saya menyatakan kos pengeluaran akan menuju sifar dan kuasa beli buruh akan runtuh. Kedua-duanya tidak semestinya berlaku. Tanah, tenaga, bahan, kemahiran yang jarang dan kepercayaan masih ada harga. Pendapatan bergantung pada pemilikan, institusi tawar-menawar, cukai dan pekerjaan baharu. Automasi ialah soal agihan, bukan jaminan ekonomi tanpa gaji.

Manusia

Prospek Populasi Dunia 2024 PBB menganggarkan penduduk dunia sekitar 8.2 bilion pada 2024, memuncak kira-kira 10.3 bilion pada pertengahan 2080-an, kemudian menurun perlahan kepada kira-kira 10.2 bilion menjelang 2100. Banyak negara memuncak lebih awal. Setakat 2024, 63 negara dan wilayah, termasuk China, Jerman, Jepun dan Rusia, sudah melepasi puncak. Menjelang kira-kira 2080 bilangan warga emas berumur 65 tahun ke atas akan melebihi kanak-kanak di bawah 18 tahun. Saya menggunakan hujah demografi ini dengan berhati-hati. Ekonomi yang menua ada sebab untuk mengautomasikan penjagaan dan logistik. Ekonomi yang lebih muda memerlukan pekerjaan dan akses digital yang mampu dibayar. Bilangan penduduk tidak terus menjadi permintaan. Perbelanjaan mengikut umur, simpanan, penghijrahan dan produktiviti semuanya penting.

Sempadan

Negara kini menganggap bayaran, awan, cip, identiti dan telekomunikasi sebagai strategik. Pemecahan boleh melindungi sesebuah negara daripada kejutan luar. Ia juga menggandakan piawaian dan mengecilkan skala rentas sempadan. Sistem bayaran atau awan berdaulat tidak semestinya terbuka. Sistem yang disokong negara pun boleh menjadi monopoli. Ujiannya sama di mana-mana: akses telus, perolehan yang boleh dipertandingkan, privasi, kebolehaudit dan peraturan keselamatan yang berpatutan. Kedaulatan digital sepatutnya bermaksud kawalan yang bertanggungjawab dengan kesalingoperasian, bukan sekadar pemilikan domestik atas sebuah monopoli.

14Kes menentang, dalam bentuknya yang paling kuat

Intinya: hujah terkuat terhadap tesis saya ialah yuran platform kebanyakannya membayar kerja sebenar, dan persaingan sudah pun menekan.

Pengkritik yang adil akan berkata begini.

  • Margin platform nipis. DoorDash menyimpan AS$1.40 bagi setiap AS$100 sebagai untung bersih. Grab memperoleh EBITDA terlaras AS$0.50B atas hasil AS$3.37B, selepas membayar AS$2.27B insentif. Itu bukan margin seorang monopolis.
  • Yuran membeli perkhidmatan sebenar. Pemenuhan, penghantaran, kawalan penipuan, bayaran dan sokongan pelanggan ada kosnya. Marketplace Pulse sendiri menyatakan penjual akan membayar untuk perkhidmatan itu di tempat lain.
  • Kemasukan itu nyata. TikTok Shop berkembang pesat di Asia Tenggara. Penjual China menolak harga turun di Amazon. Bilangan penjual yang merosot boleh bermakna persaingan lebih sengit, bukan pemerasan.
  • Pengguna beruntung. Harga rendah, penghantaran pantas dan pilihan luas ialah manfaat sebenar. Mahkamah menimbangnya.
  • Had boleh memakan diri. Had boleh menjejaskan platform kecil yang kos seunitnya lebih tinggi, memindahkan caj ke baris lain dan mengukuhkan pemain terbesar.
  • AI mungkin membuka pasaran. Jika ejen benar-benar membandingkan harga merentas peniaga, penjaga pintu kalah, dan kisah "tol baharu" saya silap.

Saya menerima lima yang pertama sebagai kemungkinan yang hidup. Fakta yang mengekalkan pandangan saya ialah yuran yang terus naik, kebergantungan yang meningkat dan kemasukan yang lemah, ketiga-tiganya sekali. Jika ketiga-tiganya tidak muncul dalam data, tesis ini gugur.
